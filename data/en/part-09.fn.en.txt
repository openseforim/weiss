v1_05_03:1) These are the halakhot in the Mishnah. (Pe'ah 2:6): "Naḥum the Scribe said, etc.: a halakhah [given] to Moses at Sinai, that one who sows his field with two kinds of wheat, if he made them into one threshing floor, gives one pe'ah; two threshing floors, he gives two pe'ot." This halakhah was hidden from Rabban Gamaliel the Elder, who was Nasi; and if he did not know it, how can we say that it was agreed and well known? The second, in Eduyot (8:3⚠12): "R. Joshua said: I have a tradition from Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai, etc., a halakhah to Moses at Sinai, that Elijah will not come to declare unclean or to declare clean." It is clear that he does not mean that Moses received at Sinai what Elijah will do in the future; that is a thing reason cannot bear. But in truth R. Joshua was repeating in this place his view that he made known in another place. That is, R. Joshua laid down a rule that from the day the Torah was given to Israel, they were given the authority to decide their doubts according to their view and according to the agreement of the majority, and "it is not in heaven" (Bava Metzia 59b). In a similar way he said here too that it is not Elijah's office to declare unclean or clean, for this is not the office of the prophets but the office of the sages, and the prophet has no power to change anything; but so it was decreed from Sinai that the sages should decide, that is, so it is set in the statute of the Torah. In this sense he uses the expression "a halakhah to Moses at Sinai," and this matter alone is enough to give us a correct picture of the meaning of this expression. See Maimonides' commentary there. The third, in Mishnah Yadayim (4:3): "a halakhah to Moses at Sinai, that Ammon and Moab give second tithe in the seventh year." It is a clear thing that this is not from Sinai and is not a law of the Torah; it is a late ordinance that the sages enacted. The Rosh already remarked on this in his commentary on tractate Yadayim, in the place mentioned, and in chapter 1 of Hilkhot Mikva'ot; and so wrote R. Simeon⚠13 of Sens in his commentary. See further Maimonides, Hilkhot Mattenot Aniyyim chapter 6, halakhah 1, and what the author of *Kesef Mishneh* wrote there.

v1_05_03:2) In Torat Kohanim [Sifra], Tzav, chapter 11: "half a log of oil for the thank offering, and a quarter [log] of oil for the nazirite, and the eleven days between one menstrual period and the next are a halakhah to Moses at Sinai." R. Akiva disputes all three of them. In Niddah (73a) the reading is "a quarter of wine for the nazirite," and R. Akiva holds that there is no measure of a quarter for the nazirite (Nazir 34a); see there (38a), Tosafot s.v. nazir, and further there in the Yerushalmi, 5:5. The matter is made clear in the Tosefta that is cited in Tosafot Eruvin (4a), s.v. gefen. The reading of Tosafot in Niddah was "a quarter of oil" (not "a quarter of wine" as we have it), for if the reading of Tosafot had been "a quarter of wine," their words would have no meaning, since they said that R. Akiva disputes this too "even though we have not found it," whereas on the quarter of wine R. Akiva disputes explicitly. Therefore it is proven that their reading was "a quarter of oil," like the reading of Torat Kohanim before us. Be that as it may, R. Akiva disputes all three.

v1_05_03:3) Tosefta Yadayim chapter 2 is a copy from the Mishnah. [Tosefta] Pe'ah chapter 2⚠14: "R. Ilai said: I asked R. Joshua about which sheaves the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel dispute" (that is, about a sheaf near the fence, Pe'ah 6:2); "he said to me: this teaching concerns the sheaf, etc. And I asked R. Eliezer, and he said to me: they agree about these; about what did they dispute? About the sheaf that he took hold of to bring to the town, etc. And when I set out the words before R. Eleazar ben Azariah, he said to me: [By] the covenant! These are the very words that were spoken to Moses at Horeb" (see the Yerushalmi and the commentary of R. Samson [of Sens]). Thus for R. Eleazar ben Azariah, that a sheaf near the fence is forgotten [produce] is a halakhah to Moses at Sinai. R. Z. Frankel, in *Darkhei ha-Mishnah*, p. 21, wrote: how can [this] apply to a dispute of the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel, that it should be a halakhah to Moses at Sinai? This is an error, for the dispute is not the halakhah; the halakhah is that a sheaf near the fence is forgotten [produce], and the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel never disputed about it. And in [Tosefta] Ḥallah chapter 1: "R. Eleazar said: I asked R. Joshua: the loaves of the thank offering and the wafers of the nazirite, what is their [law] concerning ḥallah? He said to me: they are exempt. And when I came and asked R. Eliezer, he said to me: if he made them for himself, they are exempt; to sell in the market, they are liable. And when I came and set out the words before R. Eleazar ben Azariah, he said to me: [By] the covenant! These are the words that were spoken to Moses at Horeb." Here in both cases R. Joshua disputes explicitly, and in the words of R. Eleazar ben Azariah too there is nothing to decide whether he said so absolutely or by way of wonder, that is: are the words of R. Eliezer then words from Sinai? (see Pesaḥim 38b). And the author of the Mishnah himself ruled without attribution like R. Joshua; necessarily this halakhah was not received with clarity. In Ḥullin (42a): "eighteen terefot were said to Moses at Sinai"; and in the Mishnah it is said: "whatever is not like it cannot live," and it is not their way to give a sign and a reason for a halakhah to Moses at Sinai. In Menaḥot (35b) and in many places: "Our Rabbis taught: square tefillin are a halakhah to Moses at Sinai." Shabbat (79b) has two halakhot: tefillin on parchment (kelaf) and a mezuzah on dukhsustos; and there is a Tanna there who holds that if he changed in this or in that, it is valid. How is that possible with a halakhah to Moses at Sinai? Was the halakhah not taught without qualification, and whence the distinction between the [ideal] commandment and what is indispensable? Yerushalmi Megillah 1:9 has eleven halakhot: tefillin are written with ink, and lines are scored, and they are wrapped with hair, and hung with a cloth, and stuck with glue, and sewn with sinews, and so for all that is sewn, and one leaves [space] between line and line, between letter and letter, between column and column, and one leaves a margin above and below. In Sukkah (44a) there are three halakhot: the ten saplings, the willow and the water libation; and they did not agree on this, for some say that the willow and the water libation are a law of the Torah, and some say that they are an institution of the prophets (Yerushalmi there). In Yerushalmi Megillah 6:9⚠15: "the [final letters] mem, nun, tsadi, pe, kaf, the Seers said them, a halakhah to Moses at Sinai," and others dispute. Niddah (45a): [a girl] younger than three years (who was violated⚠16) is fit for the priesthood. This is not so in the view of R. Akiva, for in his view it is from reasoning, since he distinguishes between [a girl who] has experienced intercourse and one who has not. And his students, who answered him, "just as the whole Torah is a halakhah to Moses at Sinai, so is this," did not say so absolutely; rather, because they did not know the reason for the matter, and the reason of R. Akiva did not seem good in their eyes, they said: why do we need to push to a far-fetched reason? Is it not better to say that it is a halakhah to Moses at Sinai, as people are accustomed to say of the whole Torah when we do not know the reason of things and their source? Now of these twenty there remain for us only twelve, concerning the tefillin, on which no one disputes.

v1_05_03:4) In Eruvin (4a) it is said in the name of Rav: measures, partitions and interpositions are a halakhah to Moses at Sinai. And likewise R. Yoḥanan said: measures and punishments are a halakhah to Moses at Sinai (Yoma 80a). Some dispute about the measures and about the measures of punishments (Eruvin and Yoma there, and Sukkah 6a), and some say that the sages stated all the measures (Yerushalmi Pe'ah 1:1). And we learned in Torat Kohanim, Shemini, chapter 7, on the impurity of a creeping thing: the sages measured it at the size of a lentil. And for the other measures that are not specified there in the Gemara we always find "the sages measured" (Sifre Beha'alotekha, piska 49; Ki Tetze, [piska] 222; Tosefta Bava Metzia chapter 2; and in many places). Interpositions too are not the view of all, for they asked in the Gemara: are interpositions [a law] of the Torah? And although he tried to answer, his intent is only to settle the view of the one who says [it is a] halakhah. And they said: by the law of the Torah, [an interposition over] most of it and about which one is particular interposes (Sukkah 6a, and in many places). How the expression "a law of the Torah" is mostly explained, see Yerushalmi Sukkah 4:1 and in many places. And about partitions too some dispute in the Gemara (Sukkah there). In Menaḥot (35a): the titura of the tefillin; there and in Eruvin (97a): the knot of the tefillin; in Shabbat (62a): the shin of the tefillin (and see there in Tosafot, and Eruvin 96a and Tosafot); in Menaḥot (35a): the ma'abarta of the tefillin. On these four, concerning tefillin, no one in the Gemara disputes. In Sanhedrin (82a): "one who has intercourse with an Aramean woman, zealots may strike him"; whoever looks there will see that the whole saying there is only words of aggadah. Kiddushin (39a): orlah outside the Land; and there is a dispute about it in the Gemara. Menaḥot (35a): black straps; but see there in the Gemara, where it is made clear that several ancients did not practise so, and they strain to explain the view of the one who says that it is a halakhah. In Nedarim (37a⚠17): the scribal reading, the scribal omission, [words] read but not written, written but not read, are a halakhah to Moses at Sinai. See above, chapter 7, where I showed what the nature of this halakhah is. In Menaḥot (32a): the scoring of a mezuzah is a halakhah to Moses at Sinai; but whoever looks in Megillah (18b) will find that some dispute this halakhah. Thus among all those that the Amoraim mentioned there are only four halakhot on which no one disputes. In sum, all the halakhot on which there is no dispute are only sixteen in number.

v1_05_05:1) "Halakhah" without more: circumcision overrides the Sabbath (Shabbat 132a); the females of an Egyptian and of an Edomite are permitted (Yevamot 76a); a man may impose naziriteship on his son, but a woman may not (Nazir 28a); a man may shave [with the offerings] of his father's naziriteship (there 30a); unspecified money falls to the freewill offering (there 25a); a bone the size of a barley grain defiles (there 57b); covering the blood [may be done] with anything; a nazirite who shaved is liable [whatever he shaved] with anything that shaves; a get may be written on anything (Sotah 16a). In all of them the commentators explained [it as] a halakhah to Moses at Sinai, but I did not find it so stated explicitly in the Gemara. But I found that the halakhah "an Ammonite man and not an Ammonite woman" is a halakhah that was newly made (Yerushalmi Yevamot 3:3⚠18), and the same holds for Egyptian and Edomite females. If you look at all the places I have indicated, you will find that in most of them there is a dispute.

v1_05_05:2) Hilkheta gemiri: the impurity of the deep (Pesaḥim 81b); half damage for pebbles (Bava Kamma 3b); doubtful impurity (Ḥullin 9b); the covering-stone and the side-stone (there 72b); and further in many places. The phrase gemiri is not [a] halakhah to Moses at Sinai; the proof is from Sanhedrin (101b): "we have it by tradition that there is no sitting in the Temple court except for the kings of the house of David"; and see *Tosafot Yom Tov*, Zevaḥim 1:3. And in Pesaḥim (52a) Rashi explained gemiri: a tradition from the fathers.

v1_05_05:3) From Mishnah Shabbat 1:2⚠19 and Terumot 2:1 it is proven that "in truth they said" is not a halakhah to Moses at Sinai; see Bertinoro and *Tosafot Yom Tov*. Rashi explained in Bava Metzia (60a): every "in truth they said" is a halakhah, and one is not to hesitate or stammer about the matter. There in Bava Metzia it says only "halakhah" without more, and in Shabbat (92a) a baraita is brought whose reading is also "halakhah" without more. But in the Yerushalmi the reading in several places is "every 'in truth they said' is a halakhah to Moses at Sinai"; and these words "to Moses at Sinai" are only a gloss from the margin that some corrector added, and some copyist who was not expert brought them into the text.
