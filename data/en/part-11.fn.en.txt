v1_06_03:2) Graetz, vol. 3 p. 478, decides that the translation was made in the days of Philometor, about a hundred years after Ptolemy Philadelphus. But in all the inquiries and investigations of the various critics on this subject, nothing clear has been found that is not contradicted from another place. Yet among most of them, from what I have seen, there is agreement on the main point, namely that the translation came into being in the time of Philadelphus; this view satisfied my mind and I followed it. And in his book on the Septuagint R. Zacharias Frankel brought most of the opinions of the Christian authors on this side.

v1_06_04:1) On the matters in which the Septuagint agrees with the tradition, see the books of R. Zacharias Frankel: *Darkhei ha-Mishnah* p. 11 and p. 314, his book on the translation, and the book *Mi-Midrash ha-Aleksandronim*⚠7.

v1_06_05:1) In the Bavli, Ketubbot, the reading is "formerly, when there were fixed courts in Alexandria." Such is the reading also in the Tosefta of Pe'ah and of Ketubbot, and from this it appears that they referred to an early time. But in Yerushalmi Ketubbot ch. 1 I found this reading: "even in Alexandria, at the time when the courts sit." The difference between the two readings is that according to the Bavli and the Tosefta there was a fixed court only in an early time, and it ceased afterward and was no more; while according to the reading of the Yerushalmi there were times when there was a court and times when they ceased.

v1_06_06:1) Let no one wonder at my deciding that their custom was to write the get in Greek for Greeks; for already from of old they had enacted to write it in the Land of Israel in Aramaic, because that language was the common language of the people.
