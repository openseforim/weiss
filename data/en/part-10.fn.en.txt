v1_05_15:1) The meaning of the phrase "to cause your flesh to sin" is not from the sense of sin, that is, that a man should not give his mouth to bring himself into sin by the speech of his mouth; rather it seems more likely that the root ḥeṭ here has the sense of destruction, as in "he who provokes him to anger sins against his own soul" (Proverbs 20:2), where the meaning is that one who provokes the king destroys his own soul; and likewise "and you have sinned against your soul" (Habakkuk 2:10).

v1_05_16:1) According to the Talmud, Simeon the Just served in the high priesthood forty years; but Zunz and other scholars of antiquity have proved that he did not serve in the high priesthood more than twenty years. And I have seen fit to point out here what we wrote in *Ha-Tsofeh le-Ha-Maggid* of the year [5]627, no. 32, p. 253: that all the aggadot that tell about Simeon the Just in Yoma (39a), in Tosefta Sotah ch. 3 and in Yerushalmi Yoma 6:3, concerning the changes that came after his death in the matter of the lot on the Day of Atonement, the western lamp, the fire of the altar pile, the Omer, the Two Loaves and the Showbread — all this refers neither to the first Simeon the Just nor to the second, but to another Simeon the Just who lived at a later time, in the days of the Roman emperor Caligula, about thirty years before the destruction of the Temple. And see Megillat Ta'anit ch. 11.

v1_05_16:2) In Tosafot there they relied on the midrash on "In every place where I cause My name to be mentioned" etc. [Exodus 20:21], where they transpose the verse: in every place where I am revealed, I command that My name be mentioned. Therefore after the death of Simeon the Just, when there was no longer a revelation of the Shekhinah and they no longer merited the revelation of the Shekhinah, they refrained from blessing with the Name. There is no doubt that the Tosafot have in mind the aggadot that tell of a number of miracles that ceased after the death of Simeon the Just; but we have already shown in the previous note that all this does not refer to the first Simeon the Just. According to the view of the Tosafot they no longer merited the revelation of the Shekhinah after Simeon's death even in the Temple, and it was not included in "every place where I cause My name to be mentioned"; but this is contradicted by the baraita in Sukkah (53a) and by the Mekhilta, Yitro ch. 11 — see there in our work *Middot Soferim* — and we have not found any of the Talmudic sages who says that there was some time when the Holy One, blessed be He, was not revealed in the chosen house. The main point is that the view of the authors of the baraita here is that "in every place where I cause My name to be mentioned" etc. does not mean to exclude any time, for according to the Talmud there was no time in all the days of the Temple when the Holy One, blessed be He, did not cause His Shekhinah to dwell in it; it means only to exclude the place, that is, that outside the Temple it is forbidden to do so.

v1_05_18:1) In the introductory books to the Holy Writings there is great confusion regarding their sealing. Some put their sealing as late as the time of the Maccabees, when the public reading of the books of the Prophets began, and therefore they were compelled to bring the Holy Writings into one collection (Hävernick, in the name of Clericus, vol. 1, p. 68). For this view a proof was brought from the fact that in the book of Daniel there are matters that cannot be earlier than the time of the Maccabees (Bauer, in his Introduction, p. 36). Others say that a collection was made in the days of Nehemiah, and they relied on what is written in the second book of Maccabees, 2:13–14, where the collection of the books made by Nehemiah is explicitly mentioned (Bleek, in Hävernick, 69); and a second collection was made in the time of the Maccabees. And who can count all the imagined hypotheses with which the aforementioned scholars and their many companions who wrote on this question have contented themselves?⚠11 But in my opinion the clearest proof is from the seal of the book of Ecclesiastes, as I wrote in the text; and what is nearer to reason is what follows for us from the aggadah of the Talmud, that the sealing was early, and therefore it was hard for the sages to attach the scroll to the Holy Writings. But even though they did not add to the books, nevertheless they added sections to individual books, as we have made clear.⚠12 And see the book *Moreh Nevukhei ha-Zeman*, gate 11, which dwells on this at length with several reasonable arguments; and see also the book *Gottesdienstliche Vorträge* by R. Yom Tov Zunz, p. 34.

v1_05_23:1) Rav Sherira Gaon, in his letter to the community of Kairouan printed in *Yuḥasin*, wrote in several places that Rabbi wrote down the Mishnah, and Maimonides followed him in his introduction to the Mishnah. Regarding the writing of the Mishnah in particular, we will speak further in the proper place. R. Jacob Ḥagiz, in the introduction to his commentary on the Mishnah *Etz Ḥayyim*, wrote at great length and brings proofs to uphold the words of Rashi, who said in several places in the Talmud that they did not write anything of the Oral Torah. R. Samuel David Luzzatto held this view in *Kerem Ḥemed* vol. 3, and likewise R. Hirsch Chajes in his work *Mevo ha-Talmud*; but all their words are built on shaky foundations. R. The scholar Lebrecht, in his work *Kritische Lese* p. 12, also wrote decisively that they wrote down oral matters, and the words were repeated in the work *Wissenschaftliche Blätter* p. 3.
