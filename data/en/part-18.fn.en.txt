v1_09_16:1) He further expounded: "the scall is healed" [Leviticus 13:37] — and not that a scall broke out within the scall. For by the meaning of the words as written he should be pure, since the scall has healed and the condition of the scall is fulfilled; but he departed from this sense because reasoning requires the opposite, for he understood the Torah's intent to be that every scall disease causes impurity, and he cannot be declared pure unless he is completely healed. He likewise expounded: "the scall is healed, he is pure" — could he then be released and go off? Scripture says, "and the priest shall pronounce him pure" ... could it be that if the priest said of an impure one "pure," [he is pure]? Scripture says "he is pure": he must be pure. In this too he explains that "and the priest shall pronounce him pure" is not to be understood literally, as though impurity and purity were in the hand of the priest; rather impurity depends on the presence of the scall, and purity on its healing, and the priest's task is only to determine whether the scall is present or has healed (see Sifra Tazria ch. 9; Tosefta Nega'im ch. 1). "On the seventh day he shall shave it, and on the eighth day he shall bring two turtle-doves" [Numbers 6:9–10]: if he shaved on the eighth, he brings the offerings on the ninth (Nazir 44b).
