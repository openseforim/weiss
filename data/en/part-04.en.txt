Chapter Four

Continuation of the preceding.

From the time the kingdom was divided, "their heart was divided, and they became guilty" [Hosea 10:2]. Hardly three generations had passed since the death of David, and behold, the whole land of Judah was full of idols, and they did according to all the abominations of the nations (1 Kings 14, 15). And for many days Israel was without the true God, and without a teaching priest, and without Torah [2 Chronicles 15:3], until the days of King Asa. He returned to his God and stirred up the men of Judah to seek the Lord and to do the Torah and the commandment (2 Chronicles 14:3). All that he did to restore religion he did with a whole heart, so much so that he even removed his mother from being queen-mother because she had made an abominable image for an Asherah. And with a willing soul he brought his dedicated things, of silver and of gold, and vessels, into the house of the Lord; and also the dedicated things of his father, which had formerly been brought into the temples of his idols, he dedicated to the Lord and brought them into His sanctuary (1 Kings 15). And it is not to be wondered at that he did not object to bringing into the sanctuary of the Lord vessels that had been designated for the service of other gods, and with which the priests of Baal had ministered; for we have already seen above (chapter 2), in the matter of Gideon, that in ancient times they did not consider it a sin to dedicate or to offer to the Lord a thing that had been designated for idolatry. Perhaps it was the reverse: they thought that by such an act they would make known all the more how much they despised the idols and honoured the true God. But his son Jehoshaphat did more than he to strengthen the Torah among his people. He sent teachers into all the cities of Judah to teach the people, and with them the book of the Torah of the Lord (2 Chronicles 17:9). And he set the judgment of the Torah firmly in its place: in Jerusalem he established a great court of the Levites and the priests and the heads of fathers' houses, for the judgment of the Lord and for controversies, and permanent courts in every city of the fortified cities in Judah. Over the great court in Jerusalem he set two men as heads: the one who would be the head in all matters of the Lord, and this was Amariah the priest; and the second would be the ruler of the house of Judah in all the king's matters (ibid. 19:5–11). In this appointment that Jehoshaphat made in Judah and in Jerusalem we find the first trace of an office that endured in Israel for many days, namely the office of the Pairs (Zugot), who are mentioned at a later time, who stood at the head of the great court in Jerusalem. The one was the Nasi, and he is the one whom the writer of Chronicles calls the ruler (nagid); and the second was the Av Beit Din. We will yet see in this the character of this office and the law of its heads in ancient days.{v1_03_13:1} Now at such a time, when the desire for the study of the Torah was awakened on the part of the government, this study too without doubt spread its branches and bore fruit. And now to Jehoshaphat the praise is due, that he carried the study of the Torah and of wisdom a great step forward. But the fruit of his works did not ripen, for for several generations after him the kingdom of Judah did not come to rest, at times from the pressure of enemies outside and at times from turmoil and unrest within; and also because the kings who reigned after him for more than eighty years did not truly set their hearts to strengthen the great work that Jehoshaphat had founded. Joash, who walked in the right way while guided by Jehoiada the priest, was not a man of strong moral power, and his will and also his power to act were in the hand of the priest as clay in the hand of the potter. And Amaziah his son was like him and even worse than his father. Neither of them sought the commandments of religion in truth, but only for the sight of the eyes. The service of the Lord that they upheld was only external. Their hand was with the priests, not with the prophets; therefore it came to this, until at last the king and his princes, and after them also the people of Judah, fell into the pitfalls of idolatry (2 Chronicles 24, 25). But not so was Uzziah, who reigned in place of his father Amaziah. Already at the beginning of his reign he took counsel with the prophet who had understanding in the visions of God (ibid. 23:5),⚠1 and the fruit of this act of his was seen in the land: a prophetic literature began to flourish in his time, for most of the prophets who lived in his generation and after it handled the pen of the scribe. And because he sought the Lord he prospered: he raised the horn of his rule, increased the glory of his people in temporal respects, widened the borders of his land, and built buildings like the heights. The religious, moral and intellectual state of his people also rose. And if Amos, who lived in his time, prophesied against the transgressions of Judah and against their rejecting the Torah of the Lord (Amos 2:4), this is not to be wondered at in our eyes, for so it was at the beginning of his reign, for the moral state of a people is not overturned in one day. And although the king's efforts to turn many away from iniquity were great, not all of them returned. The high and the princes did not know how to do good (Hosea 5:10). But if on the one side the prophets of that time had a controversy with Judah too (ibid. 12:3), on the other side they set their hope in Judah, that the saviour of Israel would go out from its midst, that under his sceptre the fallen tabernacle of David would be raised up, to wall up the breaches and to raise up the ruins (Amos 9:11); and there is no doubt⚠2 that the prophet supported this hope of his on the reign of King Uzziah. But this hope was not fulfilled. The relief that Israel had under his rule became, at the end of his days, the ruin of morality in the people. For the increase of wealth and of good among the people worked for evil with the masses, who stood on a low level of enlightenment, and they bred pride of heart, love of pleasures, and also iniquity and violence. The prophecy that Isaiah prophesied in the year that Uzziah died is full of these traces, when the prophet tells them that all hope of improving them is lost, until they perish and nothing remains of them but a small remnant (Isaiah 6). The success that Uzziah had during the days of his long reign came to him because his hand was always with the prophets. But to the priests he showed no favour, and they too were not his friends. Once he came into the temple of the Lord and burned incense on the altar of incense, and behold, Azariah the priest, and with him eighty priests, valiant men, were angry with him and said to him: "It pertains not unto you, Uzziah, to burn incense unto the Lord ... go out of the sanctuary, for you have trespassed" (2 Chronicles 26:16–18). Perhaps the priests saw a trespass in this deed because they thought that Uzziah wished to use the crown of priesthood, as was done among the Assyrians and the other kings of the East, where the king is the chief priest. But if we consider the ways of Uzziah, it was not so.{v1_03_14:1} Rather, what he did he did only in his simplicity, his righteousness and the uprightness of his heart. He saw, and also knew, that the first righteous kings had also done so and burned incense on the altar of the Lord, not to commit a trespass against the Lord but to give honour to the name of the Lord; and how was he lower than they, that he should not do so? And for the aim of our inquiry we see in this a new proof that the ancients did not object to departing from the form of the law, if its intent and its spirit were not corrupted by their deeds. Through the quarrel of the priests with the king a great evil came about. His son Jotham kept away from coming into the house of the Lord, and the end of this was that "the people did yet corruptly" (2 Chronicles 27:2). But this separation became in the course of the days a curse to the whole people of Judah. Death hurried Jotham away in the middle of the course of his life, and he was no more. And quickly all the good things of religion, of morality and of his people were lost, and they were as though they had not been. His son Ahaz came to the kingship after him, and he corrupted his way more than all who had been before him; and with the moral corruption the temporal good also vanished from the land. Already at the end of the days of Jotham the king of Aram and the king of Israel conspired against him (2 Kings 15:37), and in the days of Ahaz the conspiracy grew strong, and they came into the land of Judah and laid it waste, except Jerusalem, which they could not overcome (ibid. 16:5). The inner moral and religious state the prophet Isaiah describes in a very terrible manner (Isaiah 1). But there is one thing among his rebukes that throws light on the character of their way in keeping religion. For although they went astray from the way of understanding, to go astray after other gods, and moreover broke loose in their morals and their corruption was in them, for all that they did not abolish the service of the sacrifices; and while they abandoned faithfulness, justice and righteousness, they did not cease from doing statutory acts, without paying heed to the purpose these were designed to attain, nor to the spirit and the morality in them. And against these contradictions Isaiah rebuked them, saying that this is not the Torah of our God. For God does not desire the multitude of their sacrifices, and does not ask that they come to appear before Him; rather their meal-offering and their incense are an abomination to Him; also the new moon and the sabbath, the calling of assemblies, His soul hates [Isaiah 1:11–14]. And why all this? Because He cannot endure iniquity along with the solemn assembly.{v1_03_14:2} And what is it that the Lord requires of them? Purity of heart, and the seeking of justice, judgment and uprightness. If we turn our gaze to the land of the kingdom of Israel, we meet this same sight too. In all the history of that kingdom, from the day it was founded until it perished from the earth, we see that they transgressed the teachings; there was no faithfulness and no morality among them, but they did abominable deeds, adding sin to sin; and for all that they held on to the customs of their fathers and lived by the external acts of the Torah, and especially by the service of the sacrifices. For otherwise, how could the prophet Hosea have rebuked the priests, saying: "They feed on the sin of My people, and set their heart on their iniquity" (Hosea 4:8)? In this is described the perverseness of thought in which they were ensnared through the guilt of the priests, who wished out of love of gain that the people should sin, so that they would bring their sin-offerings and the priests would eat them. And he complains against the people, who think that by their sacrifices they will attain God's favour, and who go with their flocks and with their herds to seek the Lord (ibid. 5:6). And two and three times this prophet rebukes them about the doing of external acts without any heed to the inner intent. And like him the prophet Amos too, who prophesied one generation before Hosea in the land of Israel, complains against the men of his generation about this perverseness. And how fine is his mockery of those who keep the sabbath and the new moon while their eyes are not on justice and faithfulness, who say: "When will the sabbath and the new moon be gone, that we may do our work," "that we may sell grain, and set forth corn." And why do they ask this? So that after the new moon and the sabbath they can "make the ephah small," "falsify the balances of deceit," and "buy the poor for silver" (Amos 5:5, 6).⚠3 Now we have seen that this moral sickness was common to the two kingdoms together; and therefore it is no longer to be wondered at that under the reign of Ahaz, who served other gods and did according to all the abominations of the nations, and like king like people, they abandoned the qualities of the Israelite religion and "strike hands with the children of strangers" (Isaiah 3),⚠4 and that for all that they were strict about the external acts, and especially about the practice of the sacrifices (2 Kings 16:11–15).

But even in the days of the righteous king Hezekiah the disease was not truly healed. It is true that honor and praise are due to Hezekiah for removing the idols from the land and restoring the Torah to its strength, and he endeavored to raise up its study; he was the man who preserved for the last generation a precious part of the literature of the wisdom of the ancients. At his direction the words of the sages were copied and written in a book. Among the things copied at his direction were the Proverbs of Solomon, or at any rate part of them (Proverbs 25). We also find that in his days genealogical scrolls were written for the use of the chronicles (1 Chronicles 4:41), and all the deeds that he did are faithful witnesses to his effort to keep the religion of Moses on its practical side as well. But the old disease still clung to them. When Hezekiah ascended the throne of the kingdom, the first of his work was to restore to their strength the commandments and statutes that depended on the sacrificial service; he drew the priests very near and placed the charge of religion in their hands (2 Chronicles 31). But the prophet Isaiah, who lacked all confidence in the faithfulness of the priests, could not restrain himself about this, knowing how great was another work that was more necessary, for morality and justice were in a very bad state. Therefore he roused himself and rebuked the men of his generation on this matter: that they drew near to the Lord with their mouth and honored Him with their lips while their heart was far from Him, and their fear of God was a commandment of men learned by rote (ibid. 29).{v1_03_15:1} Therefore we will no longer be astonished at the sight that we see. Since it is clear that religion had different paths among the people under the reign of Hezekiah, who was at any rate righteous in his deeds, how was it possible that the ways of morality were corrupted in the terrible manner that the prophets describe in their rebukes? For indeed it was their light-mindedness in keeping the statutes, their holding to the outward act, and that their fear of God was a commandment of men learned by rote, that did all this. And the priests, and no doubt also the false prophets, strengthened their hand in this worthless thinking, and in their self-deception they thought that by this they had fulfilled their duty in keeping the religion and that it was fitting for them to obtain favor from the Lord; but offhandedly all pure faith was lost, and the words of the prophet came true: "for from the prophets of Jerusalem is profaneness gone forth into all the land" (Jeremiah 23:15). But the prophets of truth, who stood on a high level in the understanding of the statutes, rebuked the men of their generation for their error, early and persistently, and did not cease to dispute with their opponents; and they, with the small remnant that kept the way of the Lord in truth, were the bearers of religion and Torah, to keep it according to its first intention. But we cannot picture to ourselves disputes and opposition of opinions unless we determine that they had different ways in the meaning of the statutes, and this too is not possible unless we say that they were accustomed to the interpretation of the statutes.{v1_03_16:1} But we have also found particular traces that show different ways of understanding the statutes at that time, which differ from their meaning at a later time. And the rule that follows for us from these ways is the same rule that was already found in other and earlier times, namely: that in keeping the statutes they did not care to be strict about their exact form, but changed some form for the need of their hour. Thus it is told of King Hezekiah that in the first year after he came to the throne he kept the Passover, but he did not keep it at its appointed time, only in the second month. And what was the reason for his departing from the statute? Because they could not keep it at its appointed time, for the priests had not sanctified themselves sufficiently and the people had not gathered to Jerusalem. And who will not understand that the writer here gives the two causes that bring about keeping a second Passover, as written in the Torah: the first, the cause of impurity; and the second, the cause of a distant journey? Now, since this Passover was a second Passover, it is not fitting that a festival of seven days, which is the Feast of Unleavened Bread, should go with it; but it is told explicitly that the children of Israel kept at that time the Feast of Unleavened Bread seven days (2 Chronicles 31:21).⚠5 And although we cannot say that this is an explicit contradiction of the law of the Torah, for the law is entirely silent on this, yet it is clear that in the Oral Torah the law was not understood so, for according to it the rule of the second Passover is that leaven and unleavened bread are with it in the house. And it is not to be believed that Hezekiah kept the Feast of Unleavened Bread in the second month only as a make-up, because the seven-day Feast of Unleavened Bread too had not been kept at all that year; for where then did he find a make-up for a festival?⚠6 Besides this there are still in the narratives of the chronicles of the kings of Judah various traces that show us the meanings of some statutes at that time, whether in agreement or in contradiction with their meaning in the Oral Torah; but it is not our aim to point to all the details, and the general matters and the many details recorded so far are enough for us. Let us now pass from this to the time of the prophet Jeremiah, to consider the traces of tradition that were preserved in his prophecies.

The spirit of understanding that rests on the words of the prophets who preceded him is also the spirit that stirs⚠7 in the prophecies of Jeremiah. His rebukes are against the abominations of the nations, and especially against the perversity in their deeds: on the one side they acted corruptly, did abominable deeds and served other gods, and on the other side they came and bowed down to the Lord in the house upon which His name is called, and thought that by this they had done their duty (Jeremiah 7:9–10). And in such a people, which halts between two opinions and in which the service of the Lord is joined with the service of other gods, of necessity a right understanding of the faith in one God, as it was intended in the religion of Moses, had not yet taken root. In their error and the straying of their heart they held fast to outward deeds and statutes; they afflicted their souls with fasts and offered burnt offering and meal offering without number (ibid. 14:12), but they did not turn back from any of the sins of man, murder, theft and adultery, and robbing the poor, and they said: "We have not sinned" (ibid. 3:35).⚠8 And Jeremiah could not rebuke them enough for this. He spoke against the sacrificial service when it is not accompanied by the fear of the Lord and the keeping of His word in truth (ibid. 6:19–20), and against the trust in the Temple of the Lord, whose gates they entered without amending their ways to do justice and righteousness (ibid. 7:4–6). And as Isaiah complained that they honored the Lord only with their lips and not so in their heart, so Jeremiah complained before his God, saying: "Thou art near in their mouth, and far from their reins" (ibid. 12:2). And in clear words he says that only understanding and knowledge of the Lord are the purpose of religion and the statutes, and the sacrificial service is nothing but the means to attain this purpose, as is seen from the fact that Israel was not commanded in the wilderness concerning burnt offering and sacrifice, but only to hearken to the voice of the Lord (ibid. 7:21);⚠9 of necessity this is not the essence of the service but only the means. And indeed they did not offer to the Lord sacrifice and meal offering during all the forty years that they walked in the wilderness (Amos 5:25). Yet in his time his opponents were many. Even those who handle the Torah did not rise to such lofty views, and they did not know the Lord (Jeremiah 2:8); both prophet and priest went after gain and dealt falsely (ibid. 6:13). And in his rebuke the prophet thundered against these men who were wise in their own eyes, saying: "How do ye say: We are wise, and the Torah of the Lord is with us? Lo, certainly in vain hath wrought the vain pen of the scribes" (ibid. 8:8). And although today, after the thousands of years that have passed since then, we cannot know what the literature was at which the prophet aimed, this we have seen clearly: that these scribes wrote things touching on the Torah, whether they falsified the written statutes and shaped them according to their wish and purpose, or put into them intentions and meanings that they invented out of their own heart. And against this the prophet said that the pen in the hand of these scribes is a false pen, and it also made their Torah, which they call the Torah of God, false as well. Now how can we imagine such events unless we say that on both sides of the opponents the Torah was interpreted, and each of the sides chose for itself its own way in interpreting the Torah and understanding it? But even though we have seen that the outward service was evil in the eyes of Jeremiah, we will not conclude from this that he rejected the practical statutes; they too were precious in his eyes when they were done for a moral purpose. And we also see that the prophet speaks in scattered places of such statutes and warns about keeping them. And how precious is the mention of these statutes for our purpose, since some of them are statutes that are not found in Scripture, and their foundation is only in the tradition preserved for us through the prophet. Thus, when he rebukes them about keeping the Sabbath, he specifies in particular the warning that they should not carry a burden out of their houses on the Sabbath day (ibid. 18:22).⚠10 This detail is not written in the Torah of Moses nor hinted at in it, but its foundation is in a tradition from mouth to mouth, and it is suited to explain the general rule in the Torah that warns against doing any work, and it teaches us that carrying a burden out of the house is also counted as work. And perhaps this statute is already a widening of the statute of the Torah, and carrying out a burden was not forbidden by it except because it is one of the business of the weekdays, from which it is fitting to refrain, out of our duty to sanctify the Sabbath day. And that this is truly so follows for us from the words of another prophet, who prophesied not long after the time of Jeremiah: that is the exalted prophet who is not known to us by name and whose prophecies were appended to the prophecies of Isaiah. He too speaks of the holiness of the Sabbath and counts resting from pursuing one's business as one of the things necessary to the holiness of the Sabbath, saying: "If thou turn away thy foot because of the Sabbath, from pursuing thy business on My holy day ... and shalt honour it, not doing thy wonted ways, nor pursuing thy business, nor speaking thereof" (Isaiah 58:13). The meaning of these words is close to the meaning of the words of Jeremiah, for the words of both are aimed at refraining on the Sabbath from the business of the weekdays. But [Jeremiah] speaks of bringing the burden into the gates of Jerusalem and of carrying it out of the house, while [this prophet] speaks of "doing ways," in which lies the first hint of the prohibition of Sabbath limits [teḥumin] mentioned in the Talmud. But it is clear from his words that "doing ways" is not the main prohibition; he warns only to turn back the foot because of the Sabbath so as not to pursue business, and therefore his intention is only refraining from going on journeys whose purpose is to pursue business; and accordingly the words of both are equal in their inner intention. And indeed in ancient years they were not strict about walking on the Sabbath from place to place, even if the place to which one went was far, if the going was not to do the business of the weekdays but for the sake of fulfilling a commandment, such as hearing the word of the Lord. This follows for us from the story of the Shunammite woman (1 Kings 4),⚠11 whose husband said to her: "Wherefore wilt thou go to him (to the prophet) today? it is neither new moon nor Sabbath." From this it is seen that it was the custom of the good among the people to go on the Sabbath from their city to the place of the prophet. Jeremiah preserved in his prophecies several more customs that were practiced in Israel; some of them the generations after him held to, and some were lost in the course of time. A notable one among those customs is the custom of mourners for the dead. He describes the turmoil in the land of Judah and the multitude of the slain who will not be buried nor lamented: "neither shall men cut themselves, nor make themselves bald for them; neither shall men break bread for them in mourning, to comfort them for the dead; neither shall men give them the cup of consolation to drink for their father or for their mother" (Jeremiah 16:6–7). From this we learn that in his time they used to cut themselves and make baldness for the dead (Radak), and it is very far-fetched to say that he mentioned also those who acted against the law.{v1_03_18:1} And they used to break bread for the mourner to give him his meal of consolation, and gave him to drink the cup of consolation; and these customs are not hinted at in the Torah. He also preserved for us the custom of the ancients in the manner of acquisitions, to confirm every matter in buying and selling: "men shall buy fields for money" (ibid. 32; and see Kiddushin 26a). And he is the first who mentions acquisition by a deed, which he calls "the deed of purchase." From his words we see what the form of the deed was in ancient days; for when he bought a field from Hanamel his cousin, he said: "So I took the deed of the purchase, both that which was sealed, containing the terms and conditions, and that which was open; and I gave the deed of the purchase unto Baruch the son of Neriah ... in the presence of Hanamel mine uncle's son, and in the presence of the witnesses" [Jeremiah 32:11–12]. From this we understand that on the sealed one were written the terms and conditions, that is, the substance of the deed and its stipulations; but what was written on the open one is not said there, and no doubt the witnesses wrote their signatures on the open one. So too it follows from what is described in another verse (32:44), which says: "and subscribe the deeds, and seal them, and call witnesses"; so what the first verse calls "open" is described here in the words "and call witnesses" (see Bava Batra 160b). And so we learned that the buyer wrote the deed of purchase and signed it, as it says: "And I subscribed the deed, and sealed it" [Jeremiah 32:10] (and see Targum Jonathan, and Kiddushin 9a, and this requires study). Now if we look over the generations of the prophets from the days of Samuel to the days of Jeremiah, we find that from among them all clear traces of their interpretation of the statutes of religion shine forth, whether much or little. And in all these traces we have seen that there is not even one of the prophets who took the words of the statutes literally; on the contrary, all of them as one man proclaimed with all strength that in the practice of the statutes one must go down to the ultimate intention of the Torah. Now we see in this long period of time the first stage of the interpretation [midrash] of the statutes of the Torah, and therefore it is also the first period of the Oral Torah. But with its close its stage and its character are also sealed, for from then on a new stage in the understanding of the statutes of the Torah is revealed and appears before our eyes. But these two stages, the old and the new, did not join one to the other exactly; there was a time of transition between them, which was the bridge that connected the one with the other. This time of transition is the time of the exile, and it will close the first part of our account.
