v1_12_17:1) He expounds and says: If it was said to the ancients, "You shall not murder," and whoever kills a person is liable to death, I say to you that even one who provokes his fellow is liable to judgment, and whoever calls his fellow wicked, and whoever shames his fellow and calls him a fool, falls into Gehenna (Matt. 5:21–22). These are a fence for "You shall not murder"; and so the Pharisees made a fence and said: Whoever gains honour through the disgrace of his fellow has no share in the world to come (Yerushalmi Ḥagigah ch. 2; Megillah 28a). And whoever calls his fellow wicked, [the other] may go down with him into his livelihood⚠7 (Kiddushin 28a); and whoever shames his fellow in public is as if he sheds blood (Bava Metzia 58b), and it is better for him to throw himself into a fiery furnace (ibid. 59a), and he has no share in the world to come (Avot ch. 3). For "You shall not commit adultery" he makes a fence, that even one who looks at a woman is called an adulterer (ibid. 5:27); and this is a fence of the sages, that one should not look at women, nor be alone with a woman, nor talk much with her, nor converse with her in the street. And for the commandment of divorce he made a fence, that a man should not divorce his wife unless he found in her "something indecent" (ibid. 5:32), and this is the exposition of Beit Shammai (Gittin ch. 9). For "You shall not take [the name in vain]" he made a fence, that a man should not swear at all, in line with the Essenes, who refrained from swearing. And in his saying that it is fitting that speech alone should suffice, without an oath, so that it be "yes, yes" and "no, no" (ibid. 5:37), this is the teaching of the Pharisees word for word (Sifra Kedoshim ch. 8; Bava Metzia 49a). According to his saying about fulfilment, it appears that he meant that he came to add, as we have explained; and accordingly the old reading in the Talmud (Shabbat 116b), "I came not to take away [from the Torah of Moses], I came to add," is the correct one, and not as the emendation of the corrector, "and not"; and perhaps one should read "but".

v1_12_17:2) "What is hateful to you," etc., is an ancient proverb (Tobit 6),⚠8 and Hillel called it the whole of the Torah, "and the rest is commentary" (Shabbat 31a). Deeds of loving-kindness are a pillar of the world (Avot ch. 1). "You shall love your neighbour as yourself" is a great principle (Sifra Kedoshim ch. 4). "With the measure with which a man judges, they judge him" (Matt. 7:2); its counterpart: "Judge every man in the scale of merit" (Avot ch. 1), and "Whoever judges his fellow in the scale of merit is judged favourably" (Shabbat 127b). "Whoever forgives the transgressions of his brother is forgiven" (ibid. 6:14); its counterpart: "Whoever passes over his [right to] retribution, all his transgressions are passed over" (Yoma 23a). And his commandment to give charity in secret (ibid. 6:1–4) is found among the Pharisees (Ḥagigah 5a; Shekalim 5:6; Bava Batra 9b; Sifre Re'eh 116). Loving-kindness towards an enemy is found in Holy Scripture (Exodus 23:4–5; Proverbs 25), and Scripture says: "You shall not take vengeance nor bear a grudge" [Leviticus 19:18], "You shall not hate your brother in your heart" [Leviticus 19:17]. And Hillel says: "Love the creatures and bring them near to the Torah." They deliberately did not speak of this in particular, for by law no Israelite is to be an enemy to an Israelite, as it is said, "You shall not hate your brother in your heart"; and how could they command about something that is impossible according to the law?

v1_12_17:3) "[I dwell] with the contrite," etc. (Isaiah 57); "The meek shall inherit the earth" (Psalms 37); "Whoever humbles himself, the Holy One, blessed be He, raises him up" (Eruvin 13b); "humble," etc., "is a son of the world to come" (Sanhedrin 88b). "He who works righteousness shall dwell in the tent of the Lord" (Psalms 15). "Whoever has mercy on creatures receives mercy from heaven" (Shabbat 151b). "The pure of heart ascend the mountain of the Lord" (Psalms 24). "Lovers of peace are called children of the Omnipresent" (Sifre Re'eh 96). "God seeks the pursued" (Ecclesiastes Rabbah 3). "The insulted are the beloved of the Lord" (Shabbat 88b). And there is no need of proof that the belief of the Jews from of old was that the mercies of the Lord are upon the nations as upon Israel, as is explained in the chapter before this one. But just as the Jews in his time believed that the love of the Holy One, blessed be He, is special to Israel, so too was his belief (Matt. 15:26, and in many places).

v1_12_18:1) Of all these principles we find examples: Sifre Ekev 49; Mekhilta Va-yassa ch. 2; Sotah 48b; and see Beitzah 16a; Sotah 8a, and in many places; Avot 1:4;⚠9 Sotah 3b.

v1_12_18:2) He did not describe his concept of the kingdom of heaven explicitly, and he did not need to explain it, for he was speaking with Jews, and they knew that the hoped-for repair of the world under the kingdom of heaven is that the idols will be cut off, and all flesh will call upon His name, and the wicked will turn to the Lord, and all the inhabitants of the world will accept the yoke of His kingdom (the Aleinu prayer).

v1_12_18:3) "The Sabbath is handed over to you, and you are not handed over to the Sabbath" (Mekhilta Ki Tissa; Mark 2:27). "Whoever hears a little is made to hear much" (ibid. Va-yassa ch. 1; Matt. 13:12). "As I [teach] freely, so you [teach] freely" (Bekhorot 29a; ibid. 10:8). "It is enough for the servant to be like his master" (Berakhot 58b; Tosefta Sanhedrin ch. 13; ibid. 10:25). "Your deeds will bring you near and your deeds will push you away" (Eduyot ch. 4;⚠10 ibid. 12:37). "Whoever humbles himself, the Holy One, blessed be He, raises him up," etc. (Eruvin 13b; ibid. 4:15).⚠11 "The Messiah comes only when attention is diverted" (Sanhedrin 97a; ibid. 24:44). And his parable of the splinter and the beam (Arakhin 16b; ibid. 7:3), and of the householder who hired labourers (Yerushalmi Berakhot ch. 3;⚠12 ibid. 20:1). All this is a little out of much: one can show examples in the books of the Oral Torah for most of his sayings, or of those attributed to him. And needless to say, the sages did not copy his words, for we know⚠13 how careful they were not to hear a word or half a word touching on heresy. And although several of the sayings we have cited were said by later Tannaim, this is no proof at all that they are not ancient; for we find several sayings that are clearly ancient and are found in Ben Sira or in another ancient book, and they are said word for word in the name of later Tannaim, not because these founded them but because they were current in their mouths.

v1_12_18:4) Once his disciples were plucking ears of grain on the Sabbath, and the Pharisees rebuked him because he did not protest against them; and he answered them that this is permitted by law when a man has nothing to eat, and the proof is from David, who ate holy bread in time of need, and the priests do work in the Temple on the Sabbath (Matt. 12:2–5). And the sum of the matter: "The Sabbath is handed over to you, and you are not handed over to the Sabbath" (Mark 2:27). He also said that divorce is to be abolished, and gave the reason that from the beginning the Holy One, blessed be He, appointed that the bond of marriage should be a lasting bond that may never be dissolved; Moses in his time⚠14 was compelled to permit divorce to them because of their stiff-neckedness (Matt. 19:8).

v1_12_19:1) The reason they were called minim is that they called themselves "believers" [ma'aminim] (Acts 4:32, and in many places), and the Jews called them by a word that plays on that word, minim (*Musaf he-Arukh*, s.v. min),⚠15 a name by which they had also called from of old another religious sect, which believed in two powers.

v1_12_19:2) It seems to me that the words "our God, King of the universe," which are said in every blessing, were added at a later time, namely at the time when the sect of the Zealots arose and said that one must not accept the kingship of flesh and blood but only the kingship of heaven. But among the ancients the blessing was without [mention of] kingship, as in "Blessed are You, O Lord, teach me Your statutes" [Psalms 119:12]; and likewise in the Eighteen Benedictions there is no mention of kingship, nor is there any mention of it in the closing formula of each and every blessing. And therefore several sages were not strict about saying kingship.

v1_12_20:1) See what I wrote in *Bikkurim* 5624 [1864], p. 92, where I proved from the Tosefta, which says "and includes that of the minim in 'who humbles the arrogant'," that "who humbles the arrogant" is ancient and that they made an addition to it against the minim. And in Yerushalmi Ta'anit ch. 2 and Berakhot ch. 4 it says: "If a man says to you, they are seventeen, say to him: that of the minim they already fixed at Yavneh." The meaning is that "the Shoot [of David]" was late (see Numbers Rabbah and Tanḥuma Koraḥ), having been enacted after the blessing of the minim; and therefore, if the blessing of the minim had also been entirely new, they would come to only seventeen. To this he says that that of the minim was fixed at Yavneh, meaning that they fixed it in "who humbles the arrogant," and so the number comes out right. Accordingly, at first the prayer for redemption was included in the first blessing, "and brings a redeemer," etc., and in the seventh blessing, "and redeem us speedily," etc., and in the tenth blessing, "Sound the great shofar"; and the kingdom of the house of David was not mentioned. Later they included that of David in "who builds Jerusalem," that is, they added: "and speedily establish the throne of David within it"; while the main passage of the blessing was "and build it," etc., "an everlasting building," and to this the closing "who builds Jerusalem" is fitting. And at a still later time they enacted "the Shoot"; and both of these were against the minim, who said that the son of David had already come.

v1_12_20:2) See Yerushalmi and Bavli, end of Megillah. The words of the Yerushalmi: "Those who translate, 'My people, children of Israel, as we are merciful in heaven, so you shall be merciful on earth: a cow or a ewe, it and its young you shall not slaughter both on one day'" [Leviticus 22:28]. And in Leviticus Rabbah ch. 27 and Deuteronomy Rabbah Ki Tetze they expounded the reasons for these commandments thus. And see what I write below in the text, and *Tosafot Yom Tov* on Megillah ch. 5.⚠16 And R. Simeon Santa⚠17 wrote sensible words on this subject in *Bikkurim* I, p. 183.
