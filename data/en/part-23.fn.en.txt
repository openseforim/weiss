v1_12_03:1) Shem ha-meforash is like "proper name" [shem perati]. Every singling out is called perat, as in: "he specified (perat) the vow," "he specified the sin." Parash and parat sometimes interchange, as in: "until Scripture specifies (she-yifrot) for you" (Pesaḥim 22b), where the Yerushalmi reads "until it specifies (she-yifrosh)." Therefore it is possible that shem ha-meforash means the same as shem ha-meforat, and that is what we call a proper name.

v1_12_04:1) See Ḥagigah, Bavli and Yerushalmi, at the beginning of ch. 2, and Genesis Rabbah in several places in its sections, and Pirkei de-Rabbi Eliezer, and elsewhere in many places in the Gemara and in the midrash.

v1_12_11:1) See R. N. Krochmal's *Moreh Nevukhei ha-Zeman*, gate 11, and the introductions to Holy Scripture, and the recent commentators on Daniel, who have brought still further proofs.

v1_12_12:1) The wording of the verse is: "And at that time your people shall be delivered, everyone that shall be found written in the book" [Daniel 12:1]; and the commentators explained that "everyone that shall be found" refers to "your people," that is, everyone of the people who is written in the book of life. In my opinion a kaf has dropped out of the word kol because two kafs stand next to each other in the words ʿammekha kol, and one should read ke-khol ha-nimtsa katuv ba-sefer, "according to all that is found written in the book"; and he meant by it the Aramaic part of the book of Daniel, which is a book by itself, and there the matter of the kingdom of the Messiah is set out.

v1_12_13:1) This is the reading in the Gemara, but it seems to me that the version was: "and the Torah returns to its newness and is renewed for Israel," as it is in Midrash Song of Songs on "the fig tree"; and they corrected it in the Babylonian Talmud so as not to give an opponent room to say that the perfection of the Messiah will be a new Torah. Some scholars have already understood this saying in the Midrash in that direction; they are cited in the book *Foundations of the Beliefs of the Jews*⚠25 by De Wette⚠26, p. 176; but they did not understand the plain sense of the words. For this saying is directed against their statement that in the third year the Torah will be forgotten by those who study it; against this it says that in the fifth it will return to its newness, so that after the forgetting it will be as new. And it is always their way to describe love of the Torah by its being as new in the eyes of those who study it; see Sifre Va-etḥanan piska 33, and Berakhot 63b. But Jewish sages too inclined to this view, and it is said explicitly in Yalkut Isaiah remez 296 that the Holy One, blessed be He, sits (in the Garden of Eden) and expounds a new Torah that He will give through the Messiah; and without doubt the one who said that the commandments will be abolished in the time to come (Niddah 61b) meant this, and this refers to the time after the resurrection.

v1_12_16:1) Rabban Gamaliel brought the Sadducees a proof of the resurrection of the dead from the Torah, from the Prophets and from the Writings, and it is a wonder that he did not bring a proof from Daniel. Perhaps Rabban Gamaliel deliberately refused to bring a proof from a late writer like Daniel, because this was the very claim of the Sadducees, that the belief in the resurrection of the dead had been learned from the Persians; and therefore there is no proof from the late Writings.
