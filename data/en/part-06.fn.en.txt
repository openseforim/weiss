v1_04_02:1) See *Moreh Nevukhei ha-Zeman*, gate 11. In the Gemara it appears that sofer denotes one who teaches from the book, as in "the wage of a scribe and of a Mishnah teacher" (Yerushalmi Ḥagigah ch. 1). In the book *Darkhei ha-Mishnah*, p. 3, it is written that he was called "a ready scribe" because he occupied himself with the book of the Torah to interpret it; but this is not so: rather, he was called "a ready scribe" because of his speed in the art of writing, as in "my tongue is the pen of a ready scribe" (Psalms 45:2).

v1_04_03:1) See Kiddushin 30a and Yerushalmi Shekalim ch. 5. They said of Ezra: just as he was a sofer in the words of the Torah (meaning, counting the letters), so he was a sofer in the words of the sages (meaning, [arranging them in] counted sections); and see Ḥagigah 15b and Sanhedrin 106b, which derive these two senses from the verse "Where is he that counted, where is he that weighed, where is he that counted the towers?" [Isaiah 33:18].

v1_04_03:2) These are the words of the scholar Herzfeld (*Geschichte*, vol. 1, p. 132): "There is no doubt that in the exile set discourses were read before them on the days of holy convocation, from the earlier holy writings and also from those written in the exile; and it seems that most of the portions in the second part of Isaiah, etc., were written and kept for this purpose. And in the Epistle of Baruch 1:14 it says explicitly that the book which he sent to the exiles was so that they would read it, and for this reason he sent it to them, that they should read it on the Sabbaths and festivals." But from the Epistle of Baruch there is no proof, for it was written at a late time, as will be made clear further below, ch. 22.

v1_04_04:1) The sources of the book were: the words of Samuel the seer, the words of Nathan the prophet, the words of Gad the seer (1 Chronicles 29:29); the book of the kings of Judah and Israel (ibid., 2 Chronicles 16:11 and in many places); the words of the kings of Israel (33:18); the visions of Iddo the seer, Iddo the seer (9:29; 12:15); the midrash of Iddo (13:22); the words of Shemaiah (12:15); the words of Hozai? (33:19); the midrash of the book of the kings (24:27); the laments of Jeremiah (35:25); the words of Jehu the son of Hanani, which were taken up into the book of the kings of Israel (20:34); the vision of Isaiah the son of Amoz (32:33)⚠10.

v1_04_04:2) 1 Chronicles 5:1: "and because he defiled his father's couch, his birthright was given to the sons of Joseph — and the genealogy is not to be reckoned after the birthright. For Judah prevailed above his brethren, and of him came the ruler; but the birthright was Joseph's." This is an explanation of what is written in the Torah, "Reuben, you are my firstborn" etc. [Genesis 49:3], where it is not said to whom Jacob gave the birthright, and who was reckoned as firstborn, and in what respect he was reckoned as firstborn. On this the book explains that the birthright was Joseph's, and it judged so from the event before Jacob's death, when he blessed Joseph and said: "Ephraim and Manasseh, as Reuben and Simeon, shall be mine" [Genesis 48:5]; and this is why it says "to the sons of Joseph." "But not to be reckoned in genealogy after the birthright" means that the birthright was not given to him in respect of lineage and pre-eminence over all his brothers, but Judah was the one who prevailed over his brothers, and the birthright was Joseph's in respect of two tribes coming from him.

v1_04_04:3) 2 Samuel 24:1: "And again the anger of the Lord was kindled against Israel, and he moved David" etc. But in 1 Chronicles 22:1⚠11 he changed the words and wrote: "And Satan stood up against Israel, and moved David" etc. This change is without doubt according to the concept of his time, which believed in the existence of a particular Satan who incites man to evil; and the name Satan in this sense is not found in ancient books. See below, chapter 23⚠12. He also called the Temple by the name "house of sacrifice," and this too is according to the concept of his time; see above, chapter 3, in the note.

v1_04_04:4) 2 Samuel 24:3: in the mouth of Joab is put: "But why does my lord the king delight in this thing?" The reason why Joab did not agree with the king's wish is not explained there. But in Chronicles he adds the reason and says: "why should he be a cause of guilt to Israel?" [1 Chronicles 21:3]. Likewise it is not said in Samuel why David did not go to Gibeon at the time of the plague to offer sacrifices. But Chronicles says that he did not go to Gibeon because "he was terrified because of the sword of the angel of the Lord" [1 Chronicles 21:30]. Likewise, in the service of the Levites it is said that they shall serve from twenty-five years old to fifty years old (Numbers 8:24)⚠13, and elsewhere it is said from thirty years old to fifty (ibid. 4:20)⚠14; in any case they were not to be less than twenty-five years old for the service. And so it was until the days of David (1 Chronicles 23:3), but at the end of his days it was not so. The author of the book of Chronicles relates that they were counted from twenty years old, and adds the reason for the matter: for David said, "The Lord God of Israel has given rest to his people, and he dwells in Jerusalem for ever; and also the Levites shall no more carry the tabernacle" etc. (ibid. 23:24–26). By this the reason appears why David set aside the commandment of the Torah: because it was no longer needed for his time. See on this the glosses of Naḥmanides on the *Sefer ha-Mitzvot*, root 3.

v1_04_04:5) The book⚠15 was composed about three generations after Ezra. See Zunz, p. 31; and in the *History of the Jews* by Dr. Herzfeld, Excursus 2; and in the introductions to the Holy Scriptures.

v1_04_05:1) We have already said before this that the book of Ezra as it lies before us was composed some generations after Ezra's time. But it is clear that it contains parts that Ezra himself wrote, which the author took up into his book in their own writing and language; and one of these parts is chapters 8–9, in which Ezra speaks in the first person. Likewise in the book of Nehemiah: though it too was composed by a later author, most of its parts were nevertheless written by Nehemiah's hand, and one of them is this document of the making of the covenant. Whoever wishes to understand the matter should consult Zunz, pp. 12 to 35, and Herzfeld, vol. 1, p. 306 and following.

v1_04_05:2) See Kiddushin (68b), and Maimonides, Laws of Forbidden Relations 12:1, and the *Tur*, section 44, and in the book *Mevo ha-Talmud* by R. Chajes, who decided that Ezra decreed against marriage with the other nations; and this is not correct.

v1_04_05:3) In the Yerushalmi, Shevi'it 6:1 and Kiddushin 1:9 and in many places, they said that some of the commandments listed there they took upon themselves of their own accord; and for one of them the sense of the verse is also so, for it says: "And we laid upon ourselves commandments, to give the third part of a shekel yearly" [Nehemiah 10:33]. And in the Gemara they said so also of the commandment of the tithes. But even if it be as they say, that they took it upon themselves of their own accord, it is at any rate clear that they took it upon themselves with all its judgments, statutes and conditions as stated for this commandment in the Torah; or, as this commandment was understood by them, so they took it upon themselves. And they said explicitly there: "to observe and do all the commandments of the Lord our Lord" [Nehemiah 10:30], and it goes on to list what the commandments are.

v1_04_06:1) It is a known and widespread matter that buying and selling on the Sabbath is forbidden by rabbinic law, as a decree lest one write; and so the codifiers wrote as a matter of course. And in Beitzah (27b): R. Judah said in the name of Samuel: one may not fix prices at the outset for a beast on a festival; and Rashi explained: for buying and selling on the Sabbath and festival is forbidden (read: Ezra forbade it) in the book of Ezra. And they said (ibid. 37a): one may not consecrate etc., a decree on account of buying and selling, which is forbidden by Scripture: "nor pursuing your business" etc. [Isaiah 58:13]; or else, one may come to writing etc.; and if you say, this is a decree upon a decree, it is all one decree. And R. Y. P.⚠16 remarked on this contradiction. Now even for Rashi it was not settled that buying and selling is forbidden by way of a decree, and according to the first explanation it is from the words of tradition. And he was forced to explain that the prohibition already existed before Ezra, as we have proved in the text from Scripture. And that he brings a proof there (27b) from Ezra is because he said that buying and selling on the Sabbath and festival is forbidden; and the festival is not mentioned in Isaiah, but is stated very plainly in Ezra⚠17, where it says: "we will not take from them on the Sabbath or on a holy day" [Nehemiah 10:32]. The main point is that this prohibition was then held to be among the prohibitions of the Torah, and is not a decree. And it is clear to me that for all the ancients buying and selling was a Torah prohibition, as we have shown from the verses. Nor do we have from the Talmud any explicit proof against this.

v1_04_06:2) If we examine closely the verses in the Torah that speak of the gifts to the priests and the Levites, we find in all of them that it says explicitly and clearly to whom they shall belong as an inheritance, and it uses the words "shall be yours," "I have given them to you," "shall be yours," "I have given you," "I have given as an inheritance." These expressions are clear and leave no room for two meanings, and we know clearly to whom the gifts shall belong as an inheritance. But the verse on the matter of the tithe of the tithe is obscure, and we can interpret it in two senses. In saying "and you shall give of it the Lord's offering to Aaron the priest" [Numbers 18:28], it is possible that it meant that the giving shall be to Aaron so that it shall be his inheritance; and it is also possible to interpret that the giving shall be to the priest but it shall not be his inheritance, only that the tithe shall be given into his hand and from his hand reach the treasury of the Temple. And this second interpretation agrees with what they took upon themselves in the days of Ezra with regard to the tithe of the tithe.
