v1_09_22:2) R. Z. Frankel, in *Darkhei ha-Mishnah* p. 53 note 8, counts Yoḥanan ha-Ḥoroni among the disciples of Shammai and does not count R. Zadok at all. But this is not so. For these are the words of the Gemara: "R. Eleazar ben Zadok says: When I was studying Torah with Yoḥanan ha-Ḥoroni, I saw that he ate dry bread with salt in years of drought. I came and told Father. He said to me: Take him olives. I took them to him; he saw that they were moist. He said to me: I do not eat [moist] olives [so the reading of the Tosefta]. I came and told Father. He said to me: Go, tell him: the jar was pierced, only the lees had stopped it up. And although he was a disciple of Shammai, he acted only according to the words of the House of Hillel." R. Z. Frankel thought that the statement "although he was a disciple of Shammai" refers to Yoḥanan, and this against the opinion of Tosafot s.v. amar. But in the Tosefta it is proved explicitly that this statement refers to R. Zadok, for there the reading is: "R. Eleazar ben Zadok said etc. He (his father) said to me: Go, tell him, it was pierced according to the words of the House of Hillel, only the lees had stopped it up; to inform you that he ate ordinary food in purity; for although he was among the disciples of Shammai, he behaved only according to the House of Hillel." And so it is in Tosefta Eduyyot ch. 2. From this wording it is proved clearly that "to inform you" refers to R. Zadok, and this is the meaning: Yoḥanan ha-Ḥoroni, when he saw the olives moist, feared that they had become impure, for he thought that R. Zadok surely behaved like his teachers, the House of Shammai; but he (Yoḥanan), who was of the House of Hillel, feared this. [R. Zadok] sent him word not to fear, since it was pierced according to the words of the House of Hillel. On this they added in the baraita that there is no wonder at what R. Zadok did according to the House of Hillel, for although he (R. Zadok) was a disciple of Shammai, he acted only according to the words of the House of Hillel. This is the meaning of the Tosefta without any doubt, and this is also the meaning of the words of the Gemara, and so Tosafot understood it there.

v1_10_02:1) There we learned: Bava ben Buta used to offer a voluntary suspensive guilt-offering every day, except the day after Yom Kippur. One day he said: By this Temple! If they let me, I would bring one; but they say to me: Wait until you come into a case of doubt. Here we have before us an event that took place: Bava ben Buti offered a voluntary suspensive guilt-offering every day, and the priests in the Temple offered it, except on the day after Yom Kippur, when they told him to wait. On this R. Eliezer relied, and established the halakhah that a man may offer a suspensive guilt-offering voluntarily every day. But the Sages disagree and say that a suspensive guilt-offering is brought only for a matter whose deliberate transgression incurs karet. How then will they account for that event? Surely they did not deny R. Eliezer, and admitted that the event happened so; and if it was so, are not their words contradicted? Necessarily, even if by the strict line of the law it is as the Sages said, in practice they did not act so while the Temple stood.

v1_10_10:1) *Darkhei ha-Mishnah*, p. 53, attributes the decree of the eighteen matters to the time of the beginning of the founding of the Houses. Graetz, vol. 3, note 26, decided that it was twenty-four years before the destruction. His proofs: (a) From the words of R. Eliezer and R. Joshua in the Talmuds and in the Tosefta it appears that they speak as of a thing that happened recently. To this proof we have nothing to answer here; we will only say that to us the opposite appears, and who can vouch for the matter? (b) In 'Avodah Zarah (35a) R. Ishmael asked R. Joshua: Why did they forbid the cheese of gentiles for benefit? And R. Joshua diverted him to another matter. 'Ulla said, etc.: It is a new decree. And the reading of the Yerushalmi is: because they forbade it recently. This is Graetz's proof of the youth of the decree. But let us look at the context of the words in full: it is a new decree, and one does not cast doubt on it. And it concludes: for when they decree a decree in the West, they do not reveal its reason for twelve months. So according to the Talmud it was decreed in the days of R. Joshua and R. Ishmael and had not yet stood twelve months. Now R. Ishmael was a child at the time of the destruction, and necessarily this was a long time after the destruction; so how can we take from this a proof about the decree of the eighteen matters? But it is clear that already for the Amoraim the matter and its time were hidden. And in the Yerushalmi he said: because they forbade it recently, and R. Ishmael was young. This too hides the matter: if he diverted him to another matter because R. Ishmael was young, why does R. Joshua call R. Ishmael "Ishmael my brother"? And why did he begin to give the reason, and not divert him to another matter at once? Rather, certainly the reason was hidden from R. Joshua, and when R. Ishmael asked him he searched for the reason; but R. Ishmael answered him and did not accept the reason, and his replies prevailed; therefore he diverted him to another matter. Therefore the words of the Talmud here are no proof at all, and we have only the words of the Mishnah, and they indicate that the ordinance is old. (c) One of the decrees, in the opinion of R. Simeon bar Yoḥai, is their gifts. And according to what Josephus relates, Eliezer ben Hananiah decreed not to take sacrifices from foreigners, and this was at the end of the days of the Temple; therefore all eighteen matters were so too. This would be a proof if we knew for certain that their gifts are among the eighteen matters. But we can reverse the proof and say that the surmise of R. Simeon bar Yoḥai is refuted and their gifts are not among the eighteen matters, because by the words of Josephus Eleazar decreed this, and not earlier, in the days of Hananiah. But Graetz proves that the reading of the Mishnah is corrupt, and reads Eleazar ben Hananiah instead of Hananiah ben Hezekiah. And he wrote that this reading lay before the author of *Seder ha-Dorot*. This is an error. For under the entry Hananiah he wrote in the name of *Yuḥasin*: "Not all of it, as appears in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah, for R. Judah ben Shammua was a disciple of R. Meir, and he is in *Megillat Ta'anit*; and in the first chapter of Shabbat and at the end of *Megillat Ta'anit*: for the company of R. Eleazar ben Hananiah ben Hezekiah ben Garon wrote *Megillat Ta'anit*." Graetz took from his words "and in the first chapter of Shabbat etc." that he read Eleazar ben Hananiah in the Mishnah; but the abridgment, in which *Seder ha-Dorot* abridged the words of *Yuḥasin*, misled him. These are the words of *Yuḥasin*: "And it seems to me that he did not write all of it, as appears in the first chapter of Rosh Hashanah, for Judah ben Shammua, a disciple of R. Meir, abolished the persecution, and that is in *Megillat Ta'anit*. And likewise ch.⚠16 (that is, Hananiah ben Hezekiah) the contradictions of Ezekiel etc., and he (Hananiah ben Hezekiah) is remembered for good, as stated in the chapter Ha-Tekhelet and in the first chapter of Shabbat (and so it is indeed there). And at the end of *Megillat Ta'anit*, for [read: he wrote] the company of R. Eleazar ben Hananiah ben Hezekiah ben Garon wrote *Megillat Ta'anit*." These are the words that *Seder ha-Dorot* copied in brief. So where do we get that he read R. Eleazar ben Hananiah in the Mishnah? He further wrote that all the old sources know only Eleazar ben Hananiah, and he relied on the Mekhilta, portion Yitro, and on the Sifre at the end of portion Ki Tetze. I do not understand this proof: because we found two expositions from Eleazar ben Hananiah, are we obliged to emend the books that cite Hananiah ben Hezekiah? Is it something ugly that Rav said "Hananiah ben Hezekiah" in three places? And in the Mishnah and in the baraita of Shabbat it is always Hananiah ben Hezekiah; so why should we emend all these on account of a corrupt reading in the Sifre? For the baraita of the Sifre is cited in Menaḥot (45a), and in the name of Hananiah (and so also is the reading of the Yalkut), and Tosafot s.v. *ki* read Hananiah in the Sifre as well. And his proof from *Halakhot Gedolot* is no proof, for these are the words of *Halakhot Gedolot*: "And they wrote *Megillat Ta'anit* in the upper chamber of (Eleazar ben) Hananiah ben Hezekiah ben Garon when they went up to visit (the court after them stood up and hid it away)." Now what stands between the two half-circles is not from *Halakhot Gedolot*; it is a gloss following the commentator on *Megillat Ta'anit*. And now, who would plaster and who would pound⚠17 to emend the Mishnah, the baraita and the Gemara because of the error of the commentator on *Megillat Ta'anit*? He brought a further proof that the eighteen matters were decreed at the end of the Temple, in the days of Josephus: for he relates that the Jews in Syria and Caesarea sent to John of Gischala to send them Israelite oil from Galilee, because they were forbidden to eat the oil of gentiles. And how had they done until then? It must be that until that day it was not forbidden. But what proof is this? They asked for pure oil because it had already run out of their vessels, and they feared they would be forced to eat forbidden oil. And there is a hint that the decree on oil is old: as it is taught (Yerushalmi Shabbat 1:4): R. Joshua said in the name of R. Eleazar ben Zadok: I have received a tradition that any decree that the court decrees on the public, and the majority of the public did not accept it, is not a decree; they examined and found the decree on oil. Now R. Eleazar ben Zadok lived in the days of the Temple and after it, and when he says "I have received a tradition" it implies that he speaks of a tradition from an early time. And he says that those from whom he received it examined and found; this language proves that the meaning is an early decree. In sum, all his proofs have fallen into the pit and have no support whatsoever.
