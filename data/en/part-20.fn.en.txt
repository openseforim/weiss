v1_10_11:1) In Horayot (3a) they said: Our rabbis relied on the words of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel and R. Eleazar ben Zadok, who used to say: one does not decree, etc. And R. Joshua b. B.⚠12 speaks of this as of a received tradition; and see Tosefta Sotah ch. 15, and the reading needs study. And the proof that the Rabban Gamaliel of the incident is Rabban Gamaliel the Elder: R. Joshua and R. Papias testify in 'Eduyot ch. 7 that one intercalates on condition, and this incident is cited there and is part of the testimony; and perhaps because of it the halakhah was established that one intercalates the year on condition.

v1_10_12:1) In the Gemara they attributed this incident to Rabban Gamaliel of Yavneh, "after they removed him" from the patriarchate, as Rashi and Tosafot explain; but it is not so, as I wrote in the text. And R. Hirsch Chajes, in the book *Darkhei Hora'ah*, explained "after they removed him" as "after they had intercalated the year," referring to what is told, that once Rabban Gamaliel was not present at the intercalation of the year and his colleagues intercalated it on condition that the Patriarch would approve; therefore he wrote "before my colleagues." But why did he write "before me," when it was not so? And see the Yerushalmi, which cites the text in the name of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel, and in it is written "before me and before my colleagues." And the words of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel are a narrative, to make known how the ancients used to write in the time of the Temple. For so we read in the Yerushalmi and in the Tosefta: R. Yannai says in the name of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel, who used to say: "The lambs are tender, etc." With this the words of the rabbi mentioned have no basis. But however that may be, it is clear that this Rabban Gamaliel is Rabban Gamaliel the Elder. I will further remark on what Jost wrote, vol. 1, p. 382, attributing the halakhah "one may make a kid roasted whole on the nights of Passover" (Beitzah 22b) to Rabban Gamaliel the Elder. Whoever looks there carefully at the arrangement of all the mishnayot will find that it is not so, and there is no need to elaborate.

v1_10_12:2) The saying in Pesaḥim is certainly by Rabban Gamaliel the Elder, for the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel refer to it; whoever looks there will see that all the words of the Mishnah up to the dispute of the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel are the words of Rabban Gamaliel. See there, for I have been brief.

v1_10_12:3) One should remark here on the incident of R. Ḥanina ben Ḥanina, whose father vowed him a Nazirite and brought him before Rabban Gamaliel (Nazir 29a): this too was Rabban Gamaliel the Elder, for we do not find that people took Nazirite vows after the destruction, because of impurity and the lack of sacrifices. But Maimonides, *Hilkhot Nezirut* 2:21, ruled that one takes Nazirite vows in the present time; and Rabad objected to him and wrote that there is no difference between the Land of Israel and outside the Land, and it is forbidden to take a Nazirite vow anywhere. If so, according to Maimonides it is possible that this was in the days of Rabban Gamaliel of Yavneh. But from the Tosefta of Niddah ch. 5 and from the Yerushalmi of Nazir it appears that it is not so, for there it is said: when the boy answered Rabban Gamaliel with sensible words, Rabban Gamaliel kissed him on his head and said: I am sure that he will be a teacher of rulings in Israel. And on this R. Eleazar ben Zadok said: I saw him, that he was a teacher of rulings in Israel. This language proves that he speaks of an early time, and not of the time of the second Rabban Gamaliel, who was his contemporary and younger than he in years.

v1_10_13:1) See Tosefta, end of Eduyot:⚠13 "if his disciples were forgotten, he is called Rabban." And see *Darkhei ha-Mishnah*, p. 58, who wrote as follows: "It seems (that for this reason he was called Rabban) because he was the first who sat alone at the head of the Sanhedrin, without an av beit din beside him, and therefore they called him Rabban, that is, 'our master,' for to him alone the lordship came." This is refuted by Antigonus of Sokho and by Simeon son of Hillel, and there is no need to elaborate.

v1_10_14:1) In Avot de-Rabbi Natan, ch. 38,⚠14 it is said of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel the Elder that he was killed; in Semaḥot this is told in another way, and there it adds: "when the report came to R. Akiva." Necessarily this is not Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel the Elder. In the Mekhilta, Mishpatim 18, "R. Simeon" is mentioned without further specification, and that is the correct reading; and see there in our work *Middot Soferim*.

v1_10_14:2) Most of them are testimonies; see Pesaḥim 1:6, Zevaḥim 14:2,⚠15 Shekalim 4:4, Eduyot 2:3, Parah 3:1, Menaḥot 6:1, Nega'im 1:4, Yoma (39a).

v1_10_14:3) It is self-evident that one who takes upon himself a debt or a lien confirms his undertaking in his own handwriting, just as the husband writes the get, as it says "and he shall write her a bill of divorcement" [Deuteronomy 24:1]: for the husband is not obliged to divorce, and in divorcing he takes an obligation upon himself, and by law he must write the get. And in Jeremiah (32:10) it says "and I wrote it in a book": so the buyer wrote, although it is the seller who takes an obligation upon himself and withdraws from his property. The buyer needs proof of this withdrawal by the seller, and for this it will not help if the buyer writes; nevertheless Jeremiah wrote the deed. Moreover, this was made a statute: "Men shall buy fields for money, and write them in a book, and seal them, and call witnesses" [Jeremiah 32:44], and it does not specify who shall write; necessarily it does not matter who writes, and the main thing is the testimony of the witnesses. Now this is the basis for the sages who permitted the matter. For this was a small village with no fixed court, and villagers are not skilled in writing; and what could the old man do but write it himself? Therefore the sages too were compelled to depart from the custom.
