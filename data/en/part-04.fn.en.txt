v1_03_14:1) In Kings it is told that Uzziah became leprous at the end of his days. But Chronicles adds that Uzziah burned incense on the altar of incense, and because of this the priests contended with him, and he was punished for it and struck with leprosy. What is clear in this story is that he really burned incense and the priests contended with him over it, and the writer of the book connected the cause of his leprosy with this, as is the way of this writer always to add things of his own, especially where he wants to magnify the standing of the priests and the Levites, for this is among the purposes of his book.

v1_03_14:2) Isaiah chapter 1 was composed in the days of Ahaz, according to the opinion of De Wette, Gesenius and Rosenmüller, at the time when Rezin king of Aram and Pekah son of Remaliah fought against the kingdom of Judah. In verses 2, 3 and 21 it appears that the prophet complains that they turned their way from good to evil, and during the time of Isaiah's prophecy this was so only in the days of Ahaz. So too "Your country is desolate; your cities are burned with fire" etc. "And the daughter of Zion is left as a booth in a vineyard" etc. [Isaiah 1:7–8] fits the time of Ahaz, in relation to the war of Aram and Israel (2 Kings 16:5), which passed through and swept over all the land of Judah, and they came as far as Jerusalem; no doubt they conquered the other cities, and it is right to say "And the daughter of Zion is left" etc. And in 2 Chronicles 28 it is told that they made a great breach among the people of Judah and carried away captives from it. At that time the Edomites too fought against them and carried away captives, and likewise the Philistines, and in all these wars Ahaz was humbled (ibid. 17, 18). Therefore the prophet's words "Your country is desolate" are right. As for the religious state in the time of Ahaz, it has already been told in the text that he was strict about the act of sacrifice, as is said at length in Kings there; and although in Chronicles (2 Chronicles 29:6) it is told "that they turned away their faces from the habitation of the Lord ... and have not burned incense nor offered burnt offerings in the holy place" [2 Chronicles 29:6–7], we must rely only on the book of Kings, which is the main source. Likewise, what the prophet complains of here, the lack of justice (v. 17) and the violence of the princes (v. 23), the wrong against the orphan and the widow, who are the poor of the people (ibid.), he complains of the same in the prophecy of chapter 3:13, 14, 15. And chapter 3 is without doubt from the time of Ahaz. For the prophet's saying "For they are replenished from the east" etc. (verse 7),⚠13 and all the other verses that speak of idolatry, fit neither the time of Uzziah nor Jotham nor Hezekiah. But in the first years of his reign, when the state of the people was still one of temporal prosperity, in whose wake came the inclination to luxuries and to human pleasures that is described there at length (verses 16, 18–23). In general, all the words said in chapters 2 and 3 are fit to be said only of Ahaz and the state of his time. This is discussed at length in the commentaries on the book of Isaiah by the sage Shadal [S. D. Luzzatto] and by Christian scholars, the best of whom assign this prophecy to the time of Ahaz; see Gesenius, Rosenmüller, Hitzig, Ewald, Knobel.

v1_03_15:1) Isaiah prophesied the prophecy in chapter 29 in the days of Hezekiah. For in verse 15 he speaks of those who hide counsel from the Lord and whose works are in the dark, and this is aimed at those who sought to make a covenant with Egypt and to lean on it. For from the time of Ahaz, Judah was a tributary vassal to the king of Assyria (2 Kings 16:5);⚠14 Hezekiah rebelled against him and did not serve him (ibid. 18:7), and it appears that he allied himself with Egypt and leaned on it (ibid. 21). This alliance was evil in Isaiah's eyes (Isaiah 30:1; 31:1), and verse 15 is aimed at it. Especially since the whole prophecy is aimed at the siege that would come soon by the king of Assyria. All the commentators have already explained this prophecy as referring to Hezekiah; see Ibn Ezra, and the commentary of Luzzatto, Gesenius, Drechsler,⚠15 Knobel.

v1_03_16:1) Isaiah himself described such a dispute between the prophet of truth and the false prophets and the priests, in what he said (Isaiah 28:9–13): "Whom shall one teach knowledge? And whom shall one make to understand the message?" etc. The meaning is that Isaiah described the mockery of the false prophets against him and put these words in their mouth: "Whom shall one teach knowledge" etc., "them that are weaned from the milk" etc.; that is: whom does this prophet (Isaiah) want to teach the Torah of the Lord, as if we were weaned from milk, who do not know and do not understand, that he always wants to teach us and gives "precept by precept, line by line, here a little, there a little"? Their meaning is that the prophet's instruction in the statutes is nothing but an addition to the statute that he gives from his own mind: to every precept he adds a precept, and to every line a line, and in every place he finds occasion to add things from his own mind, here a little and there a little. This was a strange thing to this people, and it seemed to them as if he spoke "with stammering lips and with another tongue" when he told them that by keeping this "precept by precept" and "line by line" they would attain "the rest" and "the refreshing" for their weariness, which in their opinion is not so; therefore they would not listen to the words of the prophet. And on this the prophet concluded: indeed so it will be for them, and the word of the Lord will be to them only "precept by precept," and not truly the word of God, so that they will be punished. And he described the snaring and the breaking that come upon them in consequence of this view, that they think of what is truly the word of the Lord that it is only "precept by precept" that the prophet gives from his own mind, as though they [the precepts] came of his own will and desire.⚠16

v1_03_18:1) So Radak wrote in his commentary, because he wanted to cover for the prophet, so that we should not think that the custom of cutting oneself and making baldness for the dead was not evil in his eyes. But one who inquires into antiquities has only to judge by the plain sense and meaning of the deeds set before him. And whoever looks at the prophet's words without partiality will find that the truth is that they used to cut themselves and make baldness for the dead, not because they learned this from the nations, but it was a custom that had taken root among them too from of old. In the Torah we find a warning to the priests "that they shall not make baldness upon their head ... nor make any cuttings in their flesh" (Leviticus 21:5), and according to the masters of the Talmud this is not about the dead (Sifra, Emor 1); and of Israel it is only said: "Ye shall not make any cuttings in your flesh for the dead" (ibid. 19:28). Whether cutting and gashing are one or different in their concept we do not know, but at any rate they are close. Gashing was the practice of the priests of Baal and his worshippers (1 Kings 18:28), and in the opinion of the masters of the Talmud gashing was only a scraping of the skin on the surface, for they said that gashing is possible both by hand and with an instrument (Makkot 23a),⚠17 and by hand one cannot make a deep wound in the flesh. Indeed, in Deuteronomy the Israelites too were warned not to cut themselves nor make baldness for the dead (Deuteronomy 14:1), and the reason is given there: "For thou art a holy people unto the Lord thy God," so that it is fitting for you to conduct yourself in the measure of holiness like the priests (and see the next chapter). But in the days of Jeremiah they did not practice so, and this statute had not taken root in the nation. Therefore the prophet too speaks of making baldness and gashing for the dead as if it were a custom of Israel. And even the good among them did so, as is seen from the men who came from Samaria and Shiloh to bring meal offering and frankincense to the house of the Lord: when they heard of the destruction they shaved their beards, rent their clothes and cut themselves (Jeremiah 41:5).
