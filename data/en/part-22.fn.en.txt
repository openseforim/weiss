v1_11_13:1) Herzfeld, vol. 1, 318, is in doubt as to who copied from whom; but it seems to me that in the Book of Baruch it is a copy. Right at the beginning of the prayer (1:15) it uses the saying "To You, O Lord, belongs righteousness, and to us shame of face" (Daniel 9:7); it abridged Daniel's prayer and omitted some of the sayings in verse 7. And from verse 8 it took the saying "also to the inhabitants of Judah and Jerusalem, to our kings and our princes, to the priests and to the prophets," and expanded and changed the wording. In verse 16 Baruch speaks with the Lord in the second person, and at once in verse 17 in the third person: "and we did not hearken to His voice, to keep His commandments and His statutes that He commanded us." This is because he kept the wording of the verse in Daniel (9:10), and did not take care that in Daniel the third-person speech is right, since Daniel began in verse 9 to speak in the third person; but the author of the Book of Baruch omitted verse 9, and therefore it is strange that he spoke in the third person in verse 17. This is only because he kept the wording that he copied. In chapter 2:17 he says, in the wording of Daniel (9:18), "Incline Your ear and hear, open Your eyes and see." In Daniel the words "open and see" fit, being directed at "our desolations and the city"; but the author of the book completes the saying with the verse from Psalms, "for in death there is no remembrance of You" [Psalms 6:6], and for this the words "open and see" are strange and forced. Therefore it seems that it is only a copy.

v1_11_16:1) The explanation of the words 'from then on' is: from the writing of Ben Sira on. R. Ẓevi Graetz, in his commentary on the book of Ecclesiastes, in the appendices at the end of the book, p. 166, explains 'from then on' as from the assembly in the house of Ḥananiah ben Hezekiah, or from the day on which R. Eleazar ben Azariah was appointed Nasi, when they also spoke about certain books with regard to the defilement of the hands (Yadayim 3:5). He relies on the Yerushalmi, Sanhedrin 10:1: 'R. Akiva says: Also one who reads in the external books has no share in the world to come, such as the books of the minim; but the book of Ben Sira and the book of Ben La'anah and the books of Homer and all the books written from then on, one who reads in them is like one who reads a letter.' His intention is that here 'from then on' necessarily does not refer to the writing of the books, for these three kinds of books were not written at one time; therefore 'from then on' must refer to the time of the assembly. But I cannot decide, for he brings us a reading in the Yerushalmi that I did not find there; the reading before us is: 'has no share in the world to come, such as the book of Ben Sira and the book of Ben La'anah; but the books of Homer and all the books written from then on ...', and according to this reading 'from then on' refers to the writing of the books of Homer. That scholar strove there to prove that the book of Ecclesiastes was written at the end of the days of Herod, and that in the upper chamber of Ḥananiah ben Hezekiah the House of Shammai and the House of Hillel disputed whether to attach it to the holy writings and whether it defiles the hands, and the matter was not settled until the day on which they seated R. Eleazar ben Azariah in the academy. I must apologize for not heeding his decision, for there is much to question in his proofs, and especially regarding several readings that are not found in our books. There is no room here to go on at length, but I will bring the main reason that moved me to depart from his decision: namely, that I found that Hillel quoted a verse from the book of Ecclesiastes (Tosefta Berakhot end of ch. 1). Thus in the days of Hillel it was already held to be one of the holy writings. And even before Hillel, Simeon ben Shetaḥ quoted (Yerushalmi Berakhot 7:2, and in many places) two verses from the book of Ecclesiastes: 'In the shadow of wisdom, in the shadow of money,' and 'the advantage of knowledge is that wisdom gives life to those who have it' [Ecclesiastes 7:12]. Therefore what they disputed, whether Ecclesiastes defiles the hands, does not mean whether to attach it to the holy writings, for it had long been among the Writings; rather, they wished to store it away, as they wished to store away the book of Ezekiel.

v1_11_18:1) As: 'and they shall spread the garment' [Deuteronomy 22:17] — this is a parable (Mekhilta Mishpatim ch. 6, 13); R. Ishmael interpreted three things by way of parable. 'Nor put a stumbling block before the blind' [Leviticus 19:14] — before one who is blind in a matter (Sifra Kedoshim ch. 2). 'And honor the face of the old man' [Leviticus 19:32] — this is one who has acquired wisdom (Kiddushin 32b); and the Targum⚠9 of 'you shall rise up before the hoary head' [Leviticus 19:32] is 'before one who is learned in the Torah,' and its parallel is in the book of the Wisdom of Solomon (14:8–9)⚠10. 'A just hin' [Leviticus 19:36] — that your yes be just and your no be just (Bava Metzia 49a). 'Do not profane your daughter to make her a harlot' [Leviticus 19:29] — this is one who marries his daughter to an old man (Sanhedrin 47a)⚠11. 'You shall not eat with the blood' [Leviticus 19:26] is interpreted for several matters by way of parable (Sanhedrin 63a); and likewise 'you shall not plant for yourself an Asherah' [Deuteronomy 16:21], 'you shall not remove your neighbor's landmark' [Deuteronomy 19:14], and many more like these.

v1_11_18:2) 'And behold, a vine was before me' [Genesis 40:9] — this is the Torah (Ḥullin 92a). 'And the Lord showed him a tree' [Exodus 15:25] — He showed him words of Torah, which are compared to a tree (Mekhilta Va-Yassa ch. 1). 'Go forth and behold, O daughters of Zion, King Solomon' [Song of Songs 3:11] — the children who are distinguished, the King to whom peace belongs (end of Ta'anit; Sifra; Seder Olam). There is no need to go on at length with examples, for all the books of the Oral Torah are full of this kind. The Song of Songs is interpreted entirely by way of parable, and they said of it that it is the most excellent of songs and is holy of holies.

v1_11_18:3) The days of consecration are called 'a sweet savor' because the Holy One, blessed be He, acquired a friend for Himself and makes a festive day for Him (Sifra Tzav). 'Lest the land fall into harlotry' [Leviticus 19:29] — because he has intercourse with many women, etc. (Tosefta Kiddushin ch. 1). Why did He put the taking of the Passover lamb first, etc. (Mekhilta Bo ch. 5). 'Both man and beast' [Exodus 12:12] — the one who was first in transgression, with him the punishment begins (ibid. ch. 7; Sotah 9a). 'Bring before Me the omer so that the grain may be blessed, and the two loaves so that the fruits of the tree may be blessed' (Tosefta Rosh Hashanah ch. 1; Sukkah ch. 3; and the Gemara, Rosh Hashanah 16a). Rabban Yoḥanan ben Zakkai loved to give reasons for the commandments (see Tosefta Bava Kamma ch. 7, and Mekhilta Mishpatim ch. 12; Bava Kamma 79b; Kiddushin 22b, and the Yerushalmi there). 'Why did the Torah warn concerning the stranger,⚠12 etc.' (Bava Metzia 59b). The meal-offering of the sotah is of barley, because her deed was the deed of a beast, etc. (Sotah 14a); and see further there (8b). Some translators were accustomed to give reasons for the commandments in their translations (Yerushalmi Berakhot 5:3). In a later time it was the manner of several sages to expound the reasons of Scripture (Kiddushin 65b⚠13, and in many places). In several places in this work of ours we have noted that this was the custom of our ancients. See *Me'or Einayim* ch. 6, which brings several reasons of Scripture given by the masters of midrash that agree with the interpretations of Philo.
