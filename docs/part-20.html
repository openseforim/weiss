The Patriarchs Simeon, Gamaliel, Simeon, and the sages of their generation.

הנשיאים, שמעון, גמליאל שמעון וחכמי דורם.

3

After the death of Hillel his son Simeon rose to the patriarchate (Shabbat 15a). Of the events of his life nothing at all has been made known to us, and no halakhot are mentioned in his name. After him his son Rabban Gamaliel the Elder rose to the patriarchate. The first days of his patriarchate were evil, and the violence of the Roman governors lay heavy on the people. As at every time and in every generation, when the rod of the oppressor rests upon the lot of Israel and they despair of help from man, they lift their eyes on high and hope that signs and wonders will be done on the earth, that the Lord will send His angel and redeem them from the hand of one stronger than they [Jeremiah 31:11], so it was in the days of Rabban Gamaliel the Elder: the measure of evil was full, and their hope for the day the redeemer would come was their thought all day long. Then a sect arose among the Jews, and they set their eyes [on it], ⚠1 believing that its founder was their redeemer. One of those who spread its teachings among the nations was a sage of the Jews called Saul, who was one of the disciples of Rabban Gamaliel; he himself boasts that he sat before Rabban Gamaliel and dusted himself in the dust of his feet (Acts 22:5); ⚠2 and when some members of the sect stood trial before the judges, Rabban Gamaliel too was one of the judges. In those days the Sanhedrin went into exile and sat in the shops [Ḥanuyot], and capital cases ceased (Shabbat 15a). In the days of Rabban Gamaliel an evil decree was decreed against Israel. The emperor Tiberius died and Caligula became emperor; his heart grew proud and he made himself a god, and he commanded that the Jews set up his image in the Sanctuary. The report reached Jerusalem on the eve of the first festival day of the Feast [of Tabernacles], and their joy was turned to mourning. One of the sages, Simeon the Righteous by name, said to them: Keep your festivals in joy, for the emperor's counsel will not stand. And so it was: before the decree was carried out, Caligula was killed and his counsel was frustrated (Megillat Ta'anit ch. 11). After his death Claudius became emperor. Agrippa, Herod's grandson, who had helped to bring the rule round to him, was beloved of the new emperor, so that he made him king over Judea. He was a wise and righteous king. The days of his reign were good for Israel, and nevertheless one of the sages, Simeon by name — without doubt the Simeon the Righteous mentioned above — offended his honor and said in the assembly of the people that he was not fit to enter the Sanctuary, because he did not truly fear God. For he took it ill that Agrippa had built theaters in Caesarea after the custom of the Romans, and besides this he despised him because he was of Edomite descent (Antiquities book 19, ch. 6); but the other sages did not do so. Once Agrippa read the king's portion at the Assembly [Hakhel], and when he reached "you may not set a foreigner over you" [Deuteronomy 17:15] he wept, remembering his origin; and they said to him: Do not fear, Agrippa, you are our brother, you are our brother (Sotah 7:8). These events shed light on the views of the sages of that time. Agrippa built theaters after the custom of the gentiles, and the sages did not oppose him. And even Simeon, the opponent, in the end, when he was summoned at the king's command to Caesarea and saw what the theaters were, admitted himself that he had erred in thinking that these customs were against the religion, and he retracted. From this it appears that such prohibitions were not in their eyes prohibitions in themselves, but depended on place and time; and the strictness to keep away or to draw near always followed their situation toward the nations. Only by this do we understand why several decrees were decreed twice, even three times, at different times, as we saw in the decree on the impurity of the land of the gentiles and on glass vessels, and in the decree not to learn Greek wisdom. And when they decreed, it was a rule with them that one does not impose a decree on the public unless the majority of the public can bear it ('Avodah Zarah 36b, and parallels; Tosefta Sanhedrin ch. 2). For this reason the ordinance was made that no decree or ordinance has binding force unless the Patriarch agrees to it. Once Rabban Gamaliel went to the governor in Syria and was delayed in coming, and the time pressed to intercalate the year; his colleagues intercalated it on condition that the Patriarch Rabban Gamaliel would approve (Sanhedrin 11a, and parallels). This ordinance was a need of their time. For since the disciples of the Houses multiplied, the disputes multiplied, and the House of Shammai decreed decrees without end. The people did not follow them, for they could not bear the burden of the decrees. Therefore they ordained that one may not decree except with the consent of the Patriarch. In particular, one does not intercalate the year or perform the needs of the public except on condition — that the Patriarch approves. And why so? — so that the public will accept them (Tosefta there, ch. 2).1

נגזרה גזרה רעה על ישראל. הקיסר טיבריאוס מת ונתקסר קליגולא, ורם לבבו ועשה עצמו אלהות, וצוה שהיהודים יעמידו צלמו בהיכל. השמועה באה לירושלים ערב יו"ט הראשון של חג, ונהפכה שמחתם לאבל. אמר להם אחד מן להסב אליו המלוכה, היה אהוב לקיסר החדש עד כי המליכו למלך על יהודה. הוא היה מלך נבון וצדיק. ימי ממלכתו היו טובים לישראל, ובכל זאת אחד מן החכמים, שמעון בשמו — בלי ספק שמעון הצדיק הנזכר — פגע בכבודו ואמר בקהל עם כי איננו ראוי ליכנס למקדש כי אינו ירא אלהים באמת. כי רע עליו המעשה אשר אגריפס בנה תיאטראות בקסרי כמנהג הרומיים, ומלבד זה מאס בו יען שתולדתו מן האדומיים (קדמוניות סי"ט פ"ו), אבל שאר החכמים לא כן עשו. ופעם אחת קרא אגריפס בהקהל את פרשת המלך וכהגיעו ללא תוכל לתת עליך איש נכרי בכה בזכרו את מולדתו, ואמרו אליו: אל תתירא אגריפס אחינו אתה אחינו אתה (סוטה פ"ז מ"ח) המאורעות האלה יפרשו אור על דעות חכמי הזמן ההוא. אגריפס בנה תיאטראות כמנהג הגוים והחכמים לא התנגדו לו. וגם שמעון המתנגד, באחרונה כאשר נקרא במצות המלך לקסרין וראה את מעשה התיאטראות, הודה בעצמו כי שגה לחשוב כי המנהגים האלה הם נגד הדת וחזר בו. מזה נראה כי איסורים כאלה לא היו בעיניהם איסורים מצד עצמם רק היו תלוים במקום ובזמן. והקפידה לרחק או לקרב באה תמיד לרגלי מצבם נגד האומות. ורק בזה נבין אשר כמה גזירות נגזרו פעמים גם שלש בזמנים שונים כמו שראינו בגזרת טומאת ארץ העמים ולכלי זכוכית, ובגזרה שלא ללמוד חכמת יונית: וגם כשגזרו היה כלל אצלם שאין גוזרים גזירה על הצבור כשאין רוב הציבור יכולים לעמוד בה (ע"ז ל"ו: וש"נ תוספ' סנהדרין פ"ב). ומטעם זה נעשה התקנה שכל גזירה או תקנה אין לה כח המאסר כי אם כשהנשיא מסכים על ידה. ופעם אחת הלך ר"ג אצל השלטון שבסוריא ושהה לבא ודחק הזמן לעבר השנה, ועברוה חבריו בתנאי שירצה הנשיא ר"ג (סנהדרין י"א. וש"נ) והתקנה הזאת היתה צורך לזמנם. כי מעת שרבו תלמידי הבתים רבו המחלוקות וב"ש גזרו גזרות לאין קצה. העם לא נמשכו אחריהם כי לא יוכלון שאת משא הגזרות. לכן תקנו שאין לגזור כי אם בהסכמת הנשיא. וביחוד אין מעברים השנה ואין עושין צרכי צבור אלא על תנאי — שירצה הנשיא ולמה זה? — כדי שיקבלו הצבור עליהם (תוספ' שם פ"ב)1.

These are the ordinances attributed to Rabban Gamaliel: (a) Witnesses who had seen the new moon on the Sabbath did not move from their place all day ... and had only four cubits; Rabban Gamaliel ordained that they should have two thousand cubits in every direction (Rosh Hashanah 23b). (b) He ordained that a woman may be permitted to marry on the testimony of one witness (Yevamot 115b). (c) At first, one who divorced through an agent could cancel the bill of divorce before a court of three, and not in the presence of the agent; Rabban Gamaliel ordained that he may cancel it only in the presence of the agent (Gittin 32b). ⚠3 (d) He ordained that the name of the man and of the woman, and every name and by-name they have, be written in the bill of divorce (there, 34b). (e) He ordained that a woman who comes to collect her ketubbah from the property of orphans shall vow on whatever they wish that she has not received her ketubbah (there). (f) He ordained that the witnesses sign the bill of divorce, and the witnesses of delivery do not suffice (there, and in the Gemara 36b). These ordinances he made for the sake of the good order of the world [tikkun ha-olam]. And it appears from this that where something was required for the good order of the world he departed from what was received, even when the received tradition was founded in the Torah. Therefore he permitted a woman presumed to be a married woman [to marry again] on the testimony of one witness, although the Torah said, "at the mouth of two witnesses shall a matter be established" [Deuteronomy 19:15]. And he ordained that cancelling the agency would not avail to cancel the bill of divorce, although by law the bill is void. His ordinance concerning the witnesses of the new moon also does not agree with the tradition: for according to the tradition they have only four cubits, so how did Rabban Gamaliel give them two thousand cubits? It is only because he went down to the depth of the Torah's intent, whose purpose is the good order of the world; and so, when the good order of the world compelled going one step further than the Torah had gone, whether because of a change of circumstances or of the times, he did not hesitate and went, after the manner of his grandfather Hillel. We also find ordinances made for the good order of the world, but we cannot prove that they came from the court of Rabban Gamaliel (Gittin 40b, 45a, and elsewhere), since they may have been ordained after the destruction. But one of them appears to be from the time of the Temple, and perhaps Rabban Gamaliel was the one who ordained it. It is this: one who sold his field to a gentile, and an Israelite then bought it back from him — the buyer brings first fruits, for the good order of the world (Gittin 47b). And perhaps we may attribute to Rabban Gamaliel the ordinances made to remove enmity between Israel and the gentiles, such as: one does not prevent the gentile poor from gleanings, the forgotten sheaf and the corner of the field, for the sake of the ways of peace (there, 53); ⚠4 and one strengthens their hands in the Sabbatical year, greets them, supports their poor, visits their sick and buries their dead (there, 61a; Graetz). We may attribute all this to Rabban Gamaliel because we know that King Agrippa too, who loved him, strove to keep peace between Jews and gentiles and did good to the gentiles as well (Antiquities book 19, ch. 7); and at that time the Greeks living among them complained that the Jews did not care for the gentile poor when they distributed the charity fund to the poor every day (Acts 19); ⚠5 therefore, because of the enmity, they ordained that their poor be supported. Sometimes Rabban Gamaliel is mentioned without qualification, and he is Rabban Gamaliel the Elder. Once Rabban Gamaliel was sitting on the steps on the Temple Mount, and that scribe Yoḥanan was standing before him, with three cut sheets lying before him. He said to him: Take a sheet and write: "To our brothers, the people of the Upper South, and to our brothers, the people of Lower Galilee: may your peace increase forever. We inform you that the time of removal [of tithes] has come, etc." And write: "To our brothers in Babylonia and to our brothers in Media and to all the rest of the exile of Israel: may your peace increase. We inform you that the pigeons are tender and the lambs are thin, ⚠6 and the time of spring has not come, and the matter seemed good to me and to my colleagues, and I have added thirty days to this year" (Sanhedrin 11b; Tosefta ch. 2, and Yerushalmi there, and Ma'aser Sheni ch. 5).2 Only in the time of Rabban Gamaliel the Elder was there a reason to intercalate the year on account of pigeons and lambs, for they needed pigeons for the bird-offerings of women after childbirth and lambs for the Passover. Of halakhot we have from him only three (Orlah 2:12; Pesaḥim 116b; Bekhorot 38a),3 and there are rulings from him (Pesaḥim 74a, 88b; Tosefta 'Avodah Zarah ch. 3), and in all of them he inclines to leniency. Some customs of his household have also reached us (Sheqalim 3:3; 6:1). And it is a wonder that, with his inclination to leniency, he was strict in the laws of impurity (Berakhot 38a). Once he said to the priest who was sprinkling the water of purification: Draw back, lest you become impure (Tosefta Parah ch. 11), although according to the tradition water of purification that has done its duty does not make impure (Parah 12:4). In none of these is he mentioned with the title "the Elder," but from the nature of the matters it appears that he is none other than Rabban Gamaliel the Elder.4 Besides all these, a moral saying of his has been preserved (Avot ch. 1): "Provide yourself with a teacher, and remove yourself from doubt, and do not tithe much by guesswork." He was the first whom they called by the honorific title "Rabban," and we do not know the reason;5 perhaps there was no particular reason for it, but because he was beloved in the eyes of all and greatly honored, the people did not mention his name without this honorific, and from then on this title became a title proper to all the Patriarchs. To the honor in which they held him testifies what they said of him: "When Rabban Gamaliel the Elder died, the honor of the Torah ceased" (Sotah end of ch. 9). But their other saying too, "When he died, purity and abstinence ceased," shows how well the honor they gave his name befitted him.

ע"ד. פ"ח: תוספ' ע"ז פ"ג) ובכלן נוטה לקולא. וכן הגיעו אלינו מקצת מנהגי ביתו (שקלים פ"ג מ"ג פ"ו מ"א). ודבר פלא הוא שעם נטייתו לקולא היה מחמיר בדיני טומאה (ברכות ל"ח.). ופעם אחת אמר לכהן המזה מי חטאת פרוש לאחוריך שמא תטמא (תוספתא פרה פי"א) אף שלפי הקבלה, מי חטאת שעשו מצותם אינם מטמאים (פרה פי"ב מ"ד). בכל אלה לא נזכר בתואר "הזקן" אבל ממהות הדברים נראה שאינו אלא ר"ג הזקן ולא אחר4. מלבד כל אלה נתקיים ממנו מאמר מוסרי (אבות פ"א) עשה לך רב והסתלק p. 179 מן הספק ואל תרבה לעשר אומדות. הוא היה הראשון אשר כנוהו בתואר הכבוד "רבן" ולא ידענו הסבה5 ואולי לא היה לזה סבה מיוחדת אך מהיותו אהוב בעיני כל ומכובד מאד ע"כ לא היה העם מזכירים שמו בלא כנוי הכבוד הזה, ומאז היה הכנוי הזה תואר מיוחד לכל הנשיאים. על הכבוד שכבדוהו יעיד מה שאמרו עליו: משמת ר"ג הזקן בטל כבוד התורה (סוטה ספ"ט). אבל גם מאמרם שאמרו: משמת בטלה טהרה ופרישות הוא עד כי יאות לו הכבוד שנתנו לשמו.

Just as at his death the glory of the Torah ceased, so too at that time the tranquility of the people ceased. For the rule of Agrippa, which had prospered in all its affairs, did not last more than five years, and when he died, ruin upon ruin came upon them. The Roman procurators oppressed the people, and the land was filled with violence because of them, and the enmity of the nations around them grew. This terrible state of affairs was the reason that they could no longer bear the oppression, and almost the majority of the people became zealous as one man to stand against their oppressors. Whereas formerly the Zealots had been only a small sect, now the hand of the majority of the people was firmly with them. Nor should we wonder at this. When we see how many tens of thousands of innocent people were killed and how many were sold as slaves, who then could see this violence and not have his heart grow hot? In all the hardships that had befallen them before, we saw that the house of the Patriarchs restrained itself and bore it, because it had been laid upon them [Lamentations 3:28]; now even the Patriarch could no longer hold back. For about eighteen years before the destruction of the Temple, Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel the Elder was Patriarch over Israel (Josephus, Life 38), and he led with his own hand the last great revolt against the Romans. The great assembly at which the sages gathered in the house of Hananiah ben Hezekiah was in the time of the patriarchate of R. Simeon. In that assembly the hand of the School of Shammai was uppermost and they outnumbered their opponents, and this was not by chance. For since most of the matters debated there were directed against closeness to the gentiles, and their decrees had the purpose of keeping them at a distance, many of the School of Hillel also agreed with the School of Shammai in this matter. Indeed, even the Patriarch R. Simeon, who was head of the School of Hillel, sided with the School of Shammai in this respect. And so we understand why at that gathering the School of Shammai outnumbered the School of Hillel. But the School of Shammai sought to decree also on matters that had no relation to keeping apart from the gentiles, and in these the School of Hillel stood by their opinion, and therefore the matters were not concluded. R. Simeon did not depart, in the conduct of his patriarchate, from the ways of his fathers. Just as Hillel, his father's grandfather, enacted the prozbul and uprooted a matter of Torah for the need of his time, and just as his father did not hesitate to uproot received laws even when they had been held to be in the category of Torah law, so did he. Once the price of pairs of birds in Jerusalem stood at golden denarii. Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel said: By this Temple! I will not lodge tonight until they are at [silver] denarii. He entered the court and taught: A woman who has five certain births [owing offerings] brings one offering and may eat of the sacrifices, and the rest are not an obligation upon her. Here he made an enactment against the law of the Torah, because he saw that they refrained from bringing [offerings] on account of the high price; therefore he was lenient and went beyond the line of the law (Keritot 8a, and Rashi's commentary). Likewise his judgment was like Hillel's judgment regarding the Sadducees, and he made it known that the Sadducees are not to be held as gentiles but are like full Israelites, even though they reject the tradition (Eruvin 6:1). In this too he followed his fathers fully: that one does not lay upon the people decrees that the majority of the community cannot bear (Tosefta Sanhedrin 2). No halakhot are mentioned in his name, but it is possible that there are halakhot said in the name of "Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel" without further specification that were his, just as we saw earlier that matters said of "Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel" without further specification refer to Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel the Elder. Aggadot are told about him, but they are of no use for our purpose (Sukkah 53b; Avodah Zarah 20a). And a moral saying of his has survived (Avot 1): "All my days I grew up among the sages, and I found nothing better for the body than silence. Not study is the main thing but the deed. And whoever multiplies words brings on sin." The saying "not study is the main thing but the deed" seems to be directed against the teachings of the sect that annulled the practical part of the Torah, so that according to its teachings the Torah has no purpose except on the side of study and not on the side of action. This opinion spread also among some Jews in Alexandria, and Philo protested against it (in the book On Abraham, p. 40); and the saying of Rabban Simeon ben Gamaliel was directed against this. Of the year of his death nothing is known to us, and what is told in the aggadah, that he was killed by the government, has no foundation;6 but this is clear, that he died before the destruction. All the days that he sat at the head, his word was holy to all the members of his people, and even his opponents gave him honor and praised him, for he was a man full of the spirit of wisdom and knowledge, the spirit of counsel and might [Isaiah 11:2]; and had his counsel been followed at all times, there would then have been a future and a hope of prevailing over the Romans (Josephus, Life, ch. 38).

כאשר במותו בטל כבוד התורה כן גם אז בטלה שלות העם. כי ממשלת אגריפס אשר היתה מצליחה בכל עניניה לא התמידה יותר מחמש שנים. וכאשר מת בא עליהם שבר על שבר. נציבי רומי עשקו את העם ומלאה הארץ חמס מפניהם, ואיבת הגוים סביבותיהם רבתה. המצב האיום הזה היה סבה כי לא יכלו כלכל עוד העושק וכמעט התקנאו רוב העם באיש אחד לעמוד נגד נוגשיהם. ותחת אשר מלפנים הקנאים לא היו רק כת קטנה עתה יד רוב העם תכון עמם. ולא נפליא על זה. אם נראה כמה רבבות נקיים נהרגו וכמה נמכרו לעבדים, ע"כ מי זה יכול לראות את החמס הזה ולא יחם לבבו? בכל התלאות אשר מצאום לפנים ראינו כי בית הנשיאים התאפק וסבל כי נטל עליהם, עתה גם הנשיא לא יכול לעצור עוד. כי כמו י"ח שנה לפני חרבן הבית היה רבן שמעון ב"ג הזקן לנשיא על ישראל. (יוסיפון קורות חייו 38) והוא נהג בידו את המרידה הגדולה האחרונה נגד הרומיים. הועד הגדול אשר נתועדו החכמים בבית חנניה ב"ח היה בזמן הנשיאות של ר"ש. ובועד ההוא היתה יד ב"ש על העליונה ורבו על מנגדיהם, ולא היה זה במקרה. אמנם יען כי רוב הענינים אשר נשאו ונתנו עליהם היו מכוונים נגד הקירוב אל הגוים וגזירותיהם היו לתכלית הרחקתם. ובדבר הזה הסכימו גם רבים מב"ה עם ב"ש. הלא גם הנשיא ר"ש שהיה ראש לב"ה היתה ידו עם ב"ש בבחינה זו. וע"כ נבין למה במעמד ההוא רבו ב"ש על ב"ה. ואמנם שב"ש בקשו לגזור גם על דברים אשר אין להם יחוס אל ההתרחקות מן הגוים ובאלה עמדו ב"ה על דעתם וע"כ לא נגמרו הדברים. ר"ש לא נטה בהנהגת נשיאותו מדרכי אבותיו. כמו שהלל זקנו של אביו תקן פרוזבול ועקר דבר תורה לצורך זמנו, וכמו שאביו לא חש לעקור דינים מקובלים אף כשהוחזקו בגדר דבר תורה כן עשה הוא. ופעם אחת עמדו קנים בירושלים בדינרי זהב אמר רשב"ג המעון הזה לא אלין הלילה עד שיהיו בדינרין נכנס לבית דין ולמד: האשה שיש עליה חמש לידות ודאות מביאה קרבן אחד ואוכלת בזבחים ואין השאר עליה חובה. הנה עשה תיקון נגד דין התורה מפני שראה שנמנעו מלהביא מפני היוקר ע"כ היקל ונטה משורת הדין (כריתות ח'. ובפרש"י) וכן היה משפטו כמשפט הלל בבחינת הצדוקים ופרסם שאין להחזיק את הצדוקים כנכרים אלא הם כישראל גמורים אף שממאנים בקבלה (ערובין פ"ו מ"א). גם בזה מלא אחרי אבותיו שאין להעמיס על העם גזרות מה שאין רוב הצבור יכולים לעמוד בהם (תוספתא סנהדרין פ"ב). הלכות לא נזכרו משמו אבל אפשר כי יש הלכות שנאמרו בשם רשב"ג סתם שהי' ממנו, כמו שראינו לפני זה ענינים אמורים על רשב"ג סתם ומכוונים על רשב"ג הזקן. יש מסופרות עליו הגדות אך אין בהן תועלת לתכליתנו (סוכה נ"ג: ע"ז כ'.). ונתקיים לנו ממנו מאמר מוסרי (אבות פ"א) כל ימי גדלתי בין החכמים ולא מצאתי לגוף טוב משתיקה. ולא המדרש עיקר כי אם המעשה. וכל המרבה דברים מביא חטא. המאמר לא המדרש עיקר כי אם המעשה נראה שהוא מכוון נגד לימודי הכת p. 180 אשר בטל את חלק המעשי מן התורה, עד שלפי לימודיו אין עוד תכלית לתורה כי אם מצד הלמודי ולא מצד המעשה. הדעה הזאת נתפשטה גם אצל קצת יהודים באלכסנדריא. ופילון התרעם נגדה (בס' מאברהם צד 40) ונגד זה כוון מאמרו של רשב"ג. משנת מותו לא הודע לנו מאומה ומה שמסופר בהגדה שנהרג מהממשלה אין לו יסוד6 אבל זה ברור כי מת קודם החרבן. כל הימים אשר ישב לראש היה דברו קדש לכל בני עמו וגם מנגדיו נתנו לו כבוד ויהללוהו כי היה איש מלא רוח חכמה ודעת רוח עצה וגבורה ולו נעשתה עצתו בכל עת כי אז היה אחרית ותקוה להתגבר על הרומיים (יוסיפון קורות חייו פ' ל"ח).

6

During the patriarchate of Rabban Gamaliel and of R. Simeon his son, the School of Shammai and the School of Hillel were at their height. Of the individual sages of the Schools only a few are mentioned. One was R. Ḥanina, the deputy of the priests. At first he was one of the lovers of peace, perhaps in the days of Rabban Gamaliel. He warned that one should pray for the welfare of the government, for were it not for fear of it, men would swallow each other alive (Avot 3:1). But the times changed, and he too changed his mind. From the time the violence of the Romans passed all bounds, he turned to hate them with utmost hatred, and his hand was with the Zealots, in agreement with the Patriarch R. Simeon. He protested against the nobles and the rulers, "these are the worn-out ones of Judea," who threw off from themselves the yoke of the Holy One, blessed be He, and set over themselves the yoke of flesh and blood (Avot de-Rabbi Natan 20). From these words of his it seems that he held the basic opinions of the Zealots, who said that Israel should not submit itself to a king of flesh and blood, for its King is the Lord its God, and woe to the man who sets a human being as master over himself and bears the yoke of flesh and blood. The later [sages] told of his customs and of the custom of those who traced their descent to his house (Shekalim 6:1); from this we learn that he was head of a priestly family in the time of the Temple (Darkhei ha-Mishnah). He likewise told that his father used to push away priests with blemishes from offering (Tosefta Zevaḥim 9); and he lived also after the destruction. When the Temple was destroyed he permitted those obliged to immerse to refrain from immersing on the Ninth of Av, and said: The house of our God is worth losing one immersion a year for its sake (Ta'anit 13a). He preserved for us many laws on the customs of the priests in the service of the sacrifices.7 He gave one testimony on monetary matters. He testified about a small village beside Jerusalem in which there was an old man who used to lend to the villagers and write [the bond] in his own hand while others signed, and the case came before the sages and they permitted it (Eduyot 2:3). No doubt the custom was the reverse: the borrower wrote the bond, and when he was not skilled in writing it was written at his word in court.8 But above all we see that R. Ḥanina strove to preserve the memory of the customs of the service in the Temple. And he was not alone in this effort. One of the sages of his generation, a contemporary of Rabban Gamaliel the Elder, also collected an order of halakhot on the service of the Day of Atonement; this was Simeon of Mizpah. Some attribute to him an order of halakhot on the laws of the daily offering, and perhaps both were of his founding (Yoma 14b). Surely he too lived after the destruction, for in the time of the Temple there was no need for this, since in the time of the Temple the sages did not need to occupy themselves with the laws of sacrifices, which were entrusted to the priests. He lived in the time of Rabban Gamaliel the Elder, and he asked Rabban Gamaliel a halakhah which he did not know, and Naḥum the scribe informed them of his tradition from R. Measha, who received it from his father, who received it from the Pairs, who received it from the prophets: a halakhah [given] to Moses at Sinai (Pe'ah 2:6). The second to him was Abba Yose ben Ḥanan, who also handed down customs of the Temple service (Tosefta Yoma 5, Sukkah 4). He protested against the high priests of his day (Pesaḥim 56a; ⚠7 Tosefta Menaḥot 13). From this we see that he lived in the time of the Temple and after it; and we have nothing more from him except one saying, and that not in halakhah (Tosefta, end of Eruvin).9 His companion or disciple was Abba Saul ben Batnit (Pesaḥim, ibid.). He was a shopkeeper in Jerusalem (Tosefta Beitzah 3), conducted himself with piety, was strict with himself in resting on the Sabbath and festivals (ibid., in the Mishnah, 3:5), ⚠8 and went beyond the line of the law in the measure of justice and uprightness (Babylonian Talmud, ibid. 29a); but in matters of impurity he was not so particular (Shabbat 24:5). Beyond this nothing of his history has come down to us, and we do not know whether he lived on until after the destruction. Besides these, other sages are mentioned by name who lived at this time: Judah ben Petiri (Tosefta Sotah 5, 6), Judah ben Jacob of Beit Guvrin, Jacob ben Isaac of Beit Gufnin (Tosefta Oholot 18), Ben Bukhri (Shekalim 1:4), Simeon the Modest (Tosefta Kelim 1), and others besides. But their sayings are few, and there is no use for our purpose in mentioning them. There are also other sages who lived in the last generation before the destruction, but the main work they did for the good of the nation came afterwards; therefore it is fitting to set aside a place for the memory of their work in the first generation after the destruction. Here we will only mention some more sages who had a special office, and some of the priests noted for wisdom and fear of sin.

במשך זמן הנשיאות של ר"ג ור"ש בנו היו בית שמאי ובית הלל בתקפם. מיחידי חכמי הבתים נזכרו רק אחדים. אחד היה רבי חנינא סגן הכהנים. הוא היה מאוהבי השלום בראשונה ואולי בימי ר"ג. והזהיר להיות מתפללים בשלומה של מלכות שאלמלא מוראה איש את רעהו חיים בלעו (אבות פ"ג מ"א). אבל הזמנים השתנו וגם הוא שנה דעתו. מעת אשר חמס הרומיים עבר גבול נהפך לשנוא אותם תכלית שנאה, ותהי ידו עם הקנאים מסכים עם הנשיא ר"ש. הוא התרעם נגד החורים והנגידים הם הבלאות שביהודה שפרקו עולו של הקב"ה מעליהם והמליכו עליהם עול בשר ודם (אבות דר"נ פ"כ) מדבריו האלה נראה כי החזיק יסוד דעות הקנאים שאמרו: שלא יכנע ישראל את עצמו למלך בו"ד כי מלכו ה' אלהיו ואוי לגבר אשר שם אדם אדון לו וסובל עול בשר ודם. האחרונים ספרו ממנהגיו וממנהג המתיחסים אל ביתו (שקלים פ"ו מ"א), מזה נשמע כי היה ראש בית אב בזמן הבית (דה"מ) וכן ספר שאביו היה דוחה בעלי מומים מלהקרב (תוספ' זבחים פ"ט), וחי גם אחר החרבן. כשחרב הבית התיר לחייבי טבילה להמנע מלטבול בט' באב ואמר כדאי בית אלהינו לאבד עליו טבילה פעם אחת בשנה (תענית י"ג.). הוא קיים לנו הרבה דינים במנהגי הכהנים בעבודת הקרבנות7. עדות אחת העיד בממון. הוא העיד על כפר קטן בצד ירושלים והיה בו זקן אחד והיה מלוה לבני הכפר וכותב בכתב ידו ואחרים חותמים ובא מעשה לפני חכמים והתירו (עדיות פ"ב מ"ג), ובלי ספק היה המנהג להפך שהלוה היה כותב את השטר וכשאינו בקי בכתיבה נכתב על פיו בב"ד8. אך ביחוד ראינו כי ר"ח השתדל לקיים זכרון מנהגי העבודה במקדש. ולא היה יחיד בההשתדלות הזאת. אחד מחכמי דורו ובן דורו של ר"ג הזקן גם הוא קבץ על יד סדר הלכות בעבודת יום הכפורים. והוא היה שמעון איש המצפה. ויש שיחסו לו סדר הלכות בדיני תמיד ואולי זה וזה היה מיסודו (יומא י"ד:). וודאי גם הוא היה חי גם אחר החרבן שבזמן הבית לא היה צורך לזה כי בזמן הבית לא הוצרכו החכמים להתעסק בדיני קרבנות שנמסרו לכהנים. הוא היה חי בזמן ר"ג הזקן, ושאל מר"ג הלכה ולא ידעה, ונחום הלבלר הודיע להם קבלתו מרבי מיאשה שקבל מאביו שקבל מהזוגות שקבלו מהנביאים הלכה למשה מסיני (פאה פ"ב מ"ו). והשני לו: אבא יוסי בן חנן גם הוא מסר מנהגי עבודת המקדש (תוספתא יומא פ"ה סוכה פ"ד). הוא היה מתרעם נגד הכהנים p. 181 הגדולים שבימיו (פסחים נ"ו. תוספ' מנחות פי"ג). מזה ראינו שחי בזמן הבית ולאחריו ואין לנו ממנו עוד כי אם מאמר אחד ואינו בהלכה (תוספתא סוף ערובין)9. חברו או תלמידו היה אבא שאול בן בטנית (פסחים שם) והיה חנוני בירושלים (תוספתא ביצה פ"ג) ונהג בחסידות והחמיר על עצמו בשביתת שבת ויו"ט (שם במשנה פ"ג מ"ה) ויצא במדת הצדק והיושר לפנים משורת הדין (בבלי שם כ"ט.) אבל בעניני הטומאה לא היה מקפיד כל כך (שבת פכ"ד מ"ה). יתר על זה לא נמסר לנו מקורותיו ולא ידענו אם האריך ימים עד אחר החרבן. מלבד אלה נזכרו עוד חכמים אחרים בשם שחיו בזמן הזה. יהודה בן פטירי (תוספ' סוטה פ"ה פ"ו), יהודה בן יעקב מבית גוברין, יעקב בן יצחק מבית גופנין (תוספ' אהלות פי"ח) בן בוכרי (שקלים פ"א מ"ד), שמעון הצנוע (תוספ' כלים פ"א) ואחרים עוד. אבל מאמריהם מעטים ואין תועלת לתכליתנו בזכרונם. ועוד יש חכמים אחרים אשר היו בדור האחרון שלפני החרבן אך עיקר פעולתם אשר פעלו לטובת האומה היה אחרי כן ע"כ ראוי ליחד מקום לזכרון פעולתם בדור הראשון אחר החרבן, ורק נזכיר עוד איזה חכמים אשר היה להם פקידה מיוחדת, ואיזה מן הכהנים הרשומים בחכמה וביראת חטא.

Two sages noted for a special office were Admon and Ḥanan. They lived in the last half century before the destruction and were appointed to be "judges of robberies" (dayyanei gezelot) (Ketubbot 13, and in the Yerushalmi) or "judges of decrees" (dayyanei gezerot) (Babylonian Talmud, ibid.). What was the nature of these judges? The Talmud says that they were so called because they were appointed over robberies (Yerushalmi), and that they issued decrees concerning robbery (Babylonian Talmud). Or perhaps [they were so called] because they were appointed to judge matters of damages, small injuries and ordinary quarrels, and their purpose was to keep dealings between a man and his neighbor straight and to keep harm away from civil peace. To attain this purpose they laid down general statutes, and the details were given to the judge, to judge as he saw fit according to place and time; and at times he would issue decrees not according to the law but in order to uphold civil peace. This is the meaning of "judges of decrees": they were appointed over this part of the laws whose outcomes are mostly decrees (Darkhei ha-Mishnah, 61). However, among all the legal matters handed down in the name of the judges of decrees there is not one that relates to robberies or injuries or other damages, great or small; all of them are monetary cases, which they judged by reasoning, and some of them on the foundations of the received halakhah. Therefore they all fall within the category of monetary law, not of the laws of damages and fines. Yet one thing that is told about them gives evidence of their nature. The decree-makers in Jerusalem took their wages from the funds of the Temple chamber (Shekalim 7a). It appears from this that they were appointed by the public to be available at all times to judge between a man and his neighbor, and received their wages from the public. Although by law judges should not take a fee for judging, in such a case, where they are appointed by the public and devote all their time to their office, they did not hesitate to take a wage, and they took it lawfully (Ketubbot 105a, Tosafot). Such courts had the power to compel [people] to be judged before them and to carry out the verdicts issued by them, because the public had accepted them over itself (Sanhedrin 21). The verdict issued by a court was called "a deed of the court" (ma'aseh beit din). In the Land of Israel such a deed was called gezilei ha-dayyanim, and in Babylonia ashlei ha-dayyanim or ishrei ha-dayyanim. Both are one, and the difference between them lies in a difference of pronunciation (dialect); such an interchange between resh and lamed is very common. Now just as the Babylonians said ashlei in place of ishrei, so the same interchange occurred in the Land of Israel: some said gezilei ha-dayyanim (Yerushalmi Bava Metzi'a 1:8), and they also called them gezirei ha-dayyanim (Yerushalmi Mo'ed Katan 3:3). And so it appears that the judges appointed for this by the public, who had the power to compel the carrying out of their gezirei dinim or gezilei dinim, were called dayyanei gezerot or dayyanei gezelot. Two halakhot remain from Ḥanan. The first: a woman whose husband went abroad and who comes to be maintained from his property need not take an oath that she has not seized anything of his; but if it later becomes known that he has died and she comes to collect [her ketubbah], she must take an oath (Ketubbot 3:1). ⚠9 The basis of this ruling is the enactment that a woman who collects from the property of orphans may collect only with an oath (ibid. 87a). In his days they did not refrain from making her swear, as they did in the days of Rabban Gamaliel the Elder (Gittin 34b); if so, Ḥanan preceded Rabban Gamaliel. Ḥanan judged by reasoning that in a claim for maintenance she does not swear, and he was not concerned that she might have seized something against it, since maintenance is a thing without a fixed amount; but against the ketubbah he was concerned. The second: if a man went abroad and someone stepped forward and maintained his wife, he has lost his money (Ketubbot 13:2). This is founded on the rule that if one pays off his fellow's debt, not as his fellow's agent but of his own will, his fellow is not obliged to repay him. This rule is hinted at in another halakhah, concerning one who is forbidden by vow to benefit from his fellow, that [the fellow] may pay his shekel for him and pay his debt (Nedarim 33a). We do not know which of these two halakhot was based on the other, whether Ḥanan's came first or the reverse. In any case we see that these judges judged according to the foundations of the Oral Torah and not according to their own estimate. This becomes still clearer when we pay attention to the words of Admon, who said seven things (ibid.), all of them founded on the foundations of the statute of the Torah; and he errs who says that these judges were only a court of laymen and were not of the sages (Jost, p. 285). Two more sages are mentioned with the title "decree-makers." The first is Ḥanan the Egyptian, and we have from him only one halakhah (Yoma 63b, and parallels). The second is Naḥum the Mede. Later scholars have cast doubt on this, and rightly, for Naḥum was not in the court of Admon and Ḥanan, since those two sages lived before the time of Rabban Gamaliel, while Naḥum lived long after the destruction, as will be explained. Nevertheless it is possible that he was one of the judges of decrees at some other time, perhaps at the end of the days of the Temple. We have six halakhot from him (Shabbat 2:1; Bava Batra 5:2; Avodah Zarah 7b); concerning three of them the sages said to him: the matter was forgotten and not said. We also find four more halakhot that may be attributed to him though they are not mentioned in his name (Tosefta Bava Batra 9), all of them in monetary law. After the destruction, Nazirites came to him from the Diaspora and found the Temple destroyed. Naḥum the Mede said to them: Had you known that the Temple would be destroyed, would you have vowed? They said to him: No. And Naḥum released them (Nazir 5:5). In this too the sages of his generation did not agree with him. And one of the sages of his own land reproached the sages for their opposition, saying that this was only because the sages of the Land of Israel were grudging toward the learning of sages from another land (Avodah Zarah 7b).

שני חכמים הרשומים בפקידה מיוחדת היו אדמון וחנן, חיו בחצי המאה האחרונה קודם החרבן ונתמנו להיות דייני גזלות (כתובות פי"ג ובירושלמי) או דייני גזרות (בבלי שם). מה טיבם של הדיינים האלה? אמרו בתלמוד שנקראו כן יען שהיו ממונים על הגזלות (ירושלמי) והיו גוזרים גזרות על הגזל (בבלי). או אולי מהיותם ממונים לדון בעניני נזקין וחבלות קטנות ודברי ריבות הרגילים ותכליתם להישיר משא ומתן בין איש לרעהו ולהרחיק הנזק משלום המדיני. ולהשיג התכלית הזאת יסדו חקים כלליים והפרטים נתנו לדיין לדון כפי ראות עיניו לפי המקום והזמן ויגזור לפעמים גזרות לא לפי הדין אלא כדי להחזיק שלום המדיני, וזה ענין דייני גזרות, שממונים על החלק הזה מן הנימוסים אשר תוצאותיהם לרוב גזירות (דה"מ 61). אמנם בין כל הענינים הדיניים אשר נמסרו על שם דייני גזרות אין גם אחד אשר יש לו יחוס אל גזלות או חבלות או שאר נזקים גדולים או קטנים, אלא כלם הם דיני ממון אשר דנו עליהם בסברה ובקצתם ע"פ יסודי ההלכה המקובלת, וע"כ כלם נופלים בגדר דיני ממון לא דיני נזקין וקנסות. אמנם דבר אחד המסופר על אודותם יתן תעודה על טיבם. גוזרי גזרות בירושלים נטלו שכרם מתרומת הלשכה (שקלים ז'.). ונראה מזה שנפקדו מהצבור להיות נועדים בכל עת לדון ולשפוט בין איש ובין רעו וקבלו שכרם מהצבור. אף שמן הדין לא יטלו הדיינים שכר לדון, אך באופן הזה שממונים מהצבור ומקדישים כל זמנם לפקידתם לא חשו ליקח שכר ולקחוהו כדין (כתובות ק"ה. תוס') ובתי דין כאלה היה להם כח להכריח לדון לפניהם ולקיים פסקי הדין היוצא מלפניהם יען שקבלום הצבור עליהם (סנהדרין כ"א). הפסק היוצא מב"ד היה נקרא מעשה בית דין, ובארץ ישראל קראו מעשה כזה בשם: גזלי הדיינים. ובבבל אשלי הדיינים או אישר הדיינים, ושניהם אחד וחלופם מונח בחלוף המבטא (דיאלעקט) וחלוף כזה בין רי"ש ולמ"ד מצוי מאד. ועתה כמו שהבבליים אמרו: אשלי במקום אישר כמו כן היה החלוף הזה בארץ ישראל שקצתם אמרו: גזלי הדיינים (ירושלמי ב"מ פ"א ה"ח) וקראום גם גזרי הדיינים (ירושלמי מ"ק פ"ג ה"ג), ועתה נראה שהדיינים הממונים על זה מהצבור, והיה להם הכח להכריח על קיום גזרי דיניהם או גזלי דיניהם, נקראו בשם דייני גזרות או דייני גזלות. מחנן נשארו שתי הלכות. האחת: אשה שהלך בעלה למדינת הים ובאה ליזון מנכסיו אינה צריכה שבועה שלא תפסה משלו. ואם נודע אח"כ שמת ובאה לגבות צריכה שבועה (כתובות פ"ג מ"א). יסוד פסק זה הוא התקנה שהנפרעת מנכסי יתומים p. 182 לא תפרע אלא בשבועה (שם פ"ז.). ובימיו לא היו נמנעים מלהשביעה כבימי ר"ג הזקן (גיטין ל"ד:), וא"כ חנן קדם לר"ג. ואמנם חנן דן מהסברה שבתביעת מזונות לא תשבע ולא חש שמא תפסה כנגדם שמזונות דבר שאין לו קצבה וכנגד הכתובה חש. השניה: מי שהלך למה"י ועמד אחד ופרנס את אשתו אבד מעותיו (כתובות פי"ג מ"ב) וזה נוסד על הכלל: שהמסלק חוב של חברו שלא בשליחות חברו רק מרצון עצמו אין חברו חייב בתשלומו, והכלל הזה נרמז בהלכה אחרת לענין מודר הנאה מחברו ששוקל לו שקלו ופורע חובו (נדרים ל"ג.) ולא ידענו איזו משתי הלכות האלה נסמכה על רעותה אם זו של חנן קודמת או להפך. על כל פנים הננו רואים שאלה הדיינים דנו על פי יסודות התורה שבעל פה ולא לפי אומד דעתם. ויתברר זה עוד יותר בשומנו לב על דברי אדמון שאמר שבעה דברים (שם) וכלם נוסדים ביסודי חקת התורה וטועה מי שאומר שאלה הדיינים לא היו כי אם ב"ד של הדיוטים ולא היו מן החכמים (יאסט צד 285). עוד נזכרו שני חכמים בכנוי גוזרי גזירות. האחד הוא חנן המצרי, ואין לנו ממנו כי אם הלכה אחת (יומא ס"ג: וש"נ). והשני נחום המדי. ואם גם שהאחרונים הטילו ספק בזה וכדין, כי נחום לא היה בב"ד של אדמון וחנן, יען כי אלה שני החכמים חיו קודם זמנו של ר"ג ונחום האריך ימים אחר החרבן כאשר יבואר בכל זאת אפשר שהיה מדייני גזרות באיזה זמן אחר ואולי בסוף ימי הבית. שש הלכות יש לנו ממנו (שבת פ"ב מ"א ב"ב פ"ה מ"ב ע"ז ז':) על שלש א"ל חכמים נשתקע הדבר ולא נאמר. ומצאנו עוד ארבע הלכות אשר יש ליחם אליו אף שלא נזכרו בשמו (תוספתא ב"ב פ"ט) וכלם בדיני ממון. אחר החרבן באו אליו נזירים מן הגולה ומצאו בית המקדש חרב, אמר להם נחום המדי אלו הייתם יודעים שבית המקדש עתיד ליחרב נודרים הייתם? אמרו לו: לאו, והתירם נחום (נזיר פ"ה מ"ה) וגם על זה לא הודו לו חכמי דורו. ואחד מחכמי ארצו גנה את החכמים על דבר התנגדותם באמרו כי אין זאת אלא מפני שעיניהם של חכמי ארץ ישראל צרה בתלמודם של החכמים מארץ אחרת (ע"ז ז':).

Of the priests, three are to be noted. Joshua ben Gamla served as [high] priest some years before the destruction, and he obtained his priesthood by bribery. Martha daughter of Boethus, the widow of a priest, was betrothed to Joshua ben Gamla and gave a tarkav of denarii as a bribe to King Agrippa, and he gave him the high priesthood; afterwards he married her and she became his wife (Yevamot 61a). He made a great enactment for the good of his people, and it set up for him a monument of eternal memory. His enactment was that teachers of children should be placed in every province and in every town, and they brought their sons there at the age of six or seven (Bava Batra 21a), because he saw that the people were negligent in raising their sons. This negligence caused most of the people to be unruly. Therefore it is no wonder how easily they opened their hearts to inciters, both to join with rebels and to believe in deceivers who arose from time to time, saying that the spirit of the Lord was upon them, and prophesied falsely that through them Israel would be saved. In the last revolt Joshua was one of the peaceable and the humble, and an opponent of the Zealots. He was also removed from his priesthood, but he was in the Sanhedrin, and was not among those who counseled war against the Romans (Josephus in his Wars, book 4, chs. 3 and 5). He was very rich and donated much for the glory of the house of God (Yoma 3:9). Although he was a sage and of the Sanhedrin, no halakhah is mentioned in his name. Zechariah ben ha-Katzav was also a priest, and he lived at the end of the days of the Temple and after it. The remnants of his words are two testimonies (Ketubbot 2:9; Eduyot 5:2), ⚠10 and two halakhot are mentioned in his name (Tosefta Megillah 1; Bava Batra 7, and the Babylonian Talmud ibid. 111a). He was the first from whom a midrash is brought that expounds a single letter as an extension (Sotah 2:1). ⚠11 Zechariah ben Avkulas was among those who counseled war, and his hand was with the Zealots (Josephus). The aggadah softens his wrongdoing against his people, he being among those who caused its harm, and attributes it to his humility (Gittin 56a; Tosefta Shabbat 17). We have one halakhah from him, in which he opposes both the School of Shammai and the School of Hillel and says: not according to the words of this one and not according to the words of that one. From this we may judge that there were sages at that time who did not attach themselves to either of the Schools, and surely Zechariah was not alone in this character. This too was one of the reasons why, when they assembled in the house of Hananiah ben Hezekiah, the School of Shammai outnumbered the School of Hillel: at that gathering they decreed on matters that were a need of their time, to widen the separation between them and the nations, and for this purpose they easily found agreement to the decrees even from sages who were not of the School of Shammai (see above, this chapter).

Most of the sages of the first generation after the destruction had already labored in the last days of the Temple, in the midst of the distresses, to help their people with their wisdom and their counsel. Some counseled peace and some war, and the intention of all of them was for good. But the counsel of man was in vain, for the Lord had not commanded it, and the Lord said: "It is an act of righteousness before Me to scatter Israel among the nations" (Pesaḥim 87b). He had counseled concerning Jerusalem that it should become a ruinous heap [Isaiah 17:1], and concerning the Temple that it should be burned with fire [Isaiah 64:10], and its land a desolation, as overthrown by strangers [Isaiah 1:7]. These storms of the times overpowered them and uprooted like a tree [Job 19:10] the future and hope of their political freedom; but they had no power to remove from their hearts the spirit of love for the heritage of their fathers. Their faith in the God of truth they did not weaken but strengthened; their desire to seek the Torah of the Lord did not falter but grew strong within them with double force; and like a tender plant out of a dry ground [Isaiah 53:2] a strong longing for the Torah and the testimony [Isaiah 8:20] rose up from the ruins of Jerusalem and the Temple. The remnant of the sages that escaped was the lamp to the feet of their people in the days of their affliction and their wandering [Lamentations 1:7]; these survivors among the sages guided the rest of the remnant of the people by the skillfulness of their hands [Psalms 78:72]. Since their main work came after the destruction, we will set aside a place there for the memory of their deeds. But here we turn our eyes and our attention to the state of the Oral Torah in general: how far its boughs had grown and its branches had lengthened [Ezekiel 31:5]. Since this subject is long and broad, we will devote a special section to it in the next book, and we will close this book with a short consideration of the causes through which the expansion of the Oral Torah came about in the long span of time we have passed through until now. The causes of the expansion were many and various. The events that befell the people from the day they returned from the land of their exile did very much. Chief among them all was that they were forced time after time to turn away from their customs and from the religion. At all times, compulsion on the one side produces stiffness of neck on the opposite side. This proved true with full force among the Jews: as their opponents strove to turn them from their religion, so they too strove to hold to it with excess of strength; and this caused them to add fences and to give statute upon statute and precept upon precept [Isaiah 28:10]. Many statutes were newly made for the purpose of keeping apart from the nations, and at times statutes were newly made to appease the minds of the nations. These are the external causes; but greater than these were the internal causes. Many particular events were at times the cause for fixing halakhot and laws. The opposition of the Sadducees did much to bring new statutes into being. And still more was done by the disputes of the Pharisaic sages themselves, one with another, which were like a flowing spring that gives forth new waters every moment. But one thing we will repeat here, since it is the greatest cause of the expansion of the Oral Torah. That is: in the course of time the ways of midrash grew, and they widened them in order to bring the fixed halakhot into agreement with Scripture; and this widening grew in proportion as the halakhot and their derivatives multiplied. Seeing that at the end of the days of the Temple they already expounded letters, we know that the multitude of halakhot compelled them to seek a basis in the exposition of letters. But just as the widening of midrash helped to give a basis to the received halakhot, so it caused new halakhot to be made by it; and in this way the Oral Torah went on step by step. If we wish to estimate the greatness of the level it had reached at the end of the days of the Temple, let us consider the multitude of halakhot called by the names of the sages who lived during this time: in particular the words of Simeon ben Shetaḥ and his colleagues, Shemaiah and Avtalyon, Shammai and Hillel, the School of Shammai and the School of Hillel and the sages of their generation, Admon and Ḥanan, and Rabban Gamaliel the Elder, and all the other sages named by name. And in general let us consider the great number of halakhot mentioned anonymously in the books of the Oral Torah, of which it becomes clear from their content, subject and language that they are ancient, or about whose meaning and limits the sages of the first generation after the destruction speak, or on which they founded new halakhot. In a number of these halakhot, laws and enactments it is clear from their content that they are not later than the destruction of the Temple, and in some of them it is stated explicitly that at a later time they departed from that halakhah, whether because they saw fit to make a fence, or because they expounded some exposition, or because the original reason had lapsed (Darkhei ha-Mishnah, 142).10 Now if we pay attention to all the details of these matters, we will easily understand to what height the level of the Oral Torah had reached at the time the Temple was destroyed. This is as regards the practical and halakhic part; but the part of beliefs and opinions also reached a high level. To recognize the level of these two parts, we will review their details and their general principles in the next book.

רוב חכמי הדור הראשון אחרי החרבן כבר היו יגעים להועיל לעמם בקרב המצוקות בימי הבית האחרונים בחכמתם ובעצתם. אלה יעצו לשלום ואלה למלחמה וכונת כולם היתה לטובה. אבל עצת אדם היתה לשוא כי ה' לא צוה וה' אמר: צדקה היא מלפני לפזר ישראל בין האומות (פסחים פ"ז:). הוא יעץ על ירושלים כי תהיה למעי מפלה, ועל המקדש כי יהיה לשרפת אש, וארצה שממה כמהפכת זרים, אלה סערי העתות גברו עליהם ויסיעו כעץ אחרית ותקוה לחפשיותם המדינית, אבל לא היה להם כח להעביר רוח האהבה אל מורשת אבותיהם מלבם, ואמונתם באלהי אמת לא החלישו אך החזיקוה, חפצם לדרוש תורת ה' לא נמוט רק התאמץ בקרבם בכפל כח וכיונק מארץ ציה עלתה תשוקה עזה לתורה ולתעודה מתוך חרבות ירושלים והמקדש. שארית הפליטה מן החכמים היא היתה הנר לרגלי עמם בימי ענים ומרודם, שרידי החכמים האלה, בתבונות כפיהם ינחו את יתר הפליטה מן העם, ובהיות עיקר פעולתם אחרי החרבן על כן גם שם ניחד מקום לזכרון מעשיהם, אבל פה נשים עינינו ומעיינינו על מצב התורה שבעל פה בכלל עד איפוא גדלו פארותיה והאריכו ענפיה, ובהיות הפנה הזאת ארוכה ורחבה ניחד לה מחלקה מיוחדת בספר הבא, ונחתום הספר הזה בבחינה קטנה על הסבות אשר על ידן נסבה התרחבות התורה שבעל פה בפרק הזמן הגדול אשר עברנו עליו עד כה. סבות ההתרחבות היו רבות ושונות, הרבה מאד פעלו הקורות אשר קרו את העם למן היום אשר שבו מארץ גלותם, העולה על כלנה היה אשר נאנסו פעם אחר פעם לסור ממנהגיהם ומן הדת. בכל עת האונס מצד האחד מוליד קשיות ערף בצד שכנגדו. וזה הדבר התאמת בכל תקף אצל היהודים, כאשר השתדלו מנגדיהם להעבירם מדתם כן גם הם התאמצו להחזיק בה ביתרון עז, וזה גרם שהוסיפו גדרים ונתנו חק על חק וצו לצו. חקים רבים נתחדשו לתכלית ההרחקה מן האומות, ולפעמים נתחדשו חקים להפיס דעתם של אומות. אלה הן הסבות החוציות; It is self-evident that one who takes upon himself a debt or a lien confirms his undertaking in his own handwriting, just as the husband writes the get, as it says "and he shall write her a bill of divorcement" [Deuteronomy 24:1]: for the husband is not obliged to divorce, and in divorcing he takes an obligation upon himself, and by law he must write the get. And in Jeremiah (32:10) it says "and I wrote it in a book": so the buyer wrote, although it is the seller who takes an obligation upon himself and withdraws from his property. The buyer needs proof of this withdrawal by the seller, and for this it will not help if the buyer writes; nevertheless Jeremiah wrote the deed. Moreover, this was made a statute: "Men shall buy fields for money, and write them in a book, and seal them, and call witnesses" [Jeremiah 32:44], and it does not specify who shall write; necessarily it does not matter who writes, and the main thing is the testimony of the witnesses. Now this is the basis for the sages who permitted the matter. For this was a small village with no fixed court, and villagers are not skilled in writing; and what could the old man do but write it himself? Therefore the sages too were compelled to depart from the custom. ↩ back to the text
