I. H. Weiss, Dor Dor ve-Dorshav

The history of the Oral Law, with an English translation paragraph by paragraph · דור דור ודורשיו

Isaac Hirsch Weiss (1815–1905), teacher of Talmud at the Vienna Bet ha-Midrash, wrote the first full history of the Oral Law in Hebrew, from the Bible to the end of the sixteenth century, in five volumes (Vienna 1871–91). It is still a standard work, and it has no English translation.

The Hebrew text

  • Base text: the Daat virtual library (vol. 1), about 300 dpi. Its title page has no imprint (Daat's stamp stands in its place). It is a later printing, not the first edition of 1871: vol. 1 cites vols. 4 and 5 of the work (1887–91) and a journal of 1889. The hidden text layer of the Daat PDFs (an older OCR) is not used: its words are reversed and out of order.
  • OCR: Kraken with the PP-OCRv6 model, line by line, then checked against the scan by eye.
  • Other scans: HathiTrust has the first edition (Columbia and California copies) and the Vilna 1904 and Berlin 1923 printings.

Method

  • Rows. One row is one heading or one paragraph of the print. Weiss's footnotes follow the text of each part.
  • English. Close to the Hebrew; Weiss's references to sources are kept as he gives them.
  • Uncertain renderings have the mark ⚠ and a note at the end of the page.
  • Translator. The translation is by Claude (Anthropic), directed and reviewed by Ezra Brand.

Translations that exist

  • None in English is known. Yiddish: Di grinder un boyer fun Yudentum (New York, 1920–22).
