Simeon ben Shetaḥ; Judah ben Tabbai; the victory of the Pharisees.

שמעון בן שטח, יהודה בן טבאי, ניצוח הפרושים.

3

Under the rule of Yannai the hand of the Sadducees was still uppermost, and they sat first in the Sanhedrin, for the decree of John had not come to an end and had not been annulled. The Pharisees refrained from sitting with the Sadducees in the Sanhedrin, except for one sage of the Pharisees, who nevertheless made the effort to sit in the Sanhedrin, although he was always forced to set aside his opinion before theirs, for they were the majority. But he did not do this out of love of honor and authority. For a man who sits in a seat among opponents, where none of his words are heeded, where is the honor and where is the authority? Rather, his inner recognition of the justice and truth of the views that he carried in his heart and on his lips did this. He would not tire or weary of opposing them, even if his strength was spent in vain. He saw and knew that their strength would not last forever and that their views had no standing. For the foundations of the views of the Sadducees cannot stand against time and its changes, whereas the views of the Pharisees, together with the ways of midrash of the Pharisees, are fitted to all times with their changes and their demands. Therefore his heart was confident that the days would come when the rule of the Sadducees would be humbled; and with this confidence, how should he bury his hand in the dish [Proverbs 19:24]? This sage was Simeon ben Shetaḥ. He refused to keep away from them and leave them a free hand to do as their heart desired. Rather, he stood against them in open opposition, guided by counsel and knowledge and carried on by compelling proofs and arguments. By these he hoped to overcome them little by little and to push them from their position step by step. Nor was his hope disappointed. Various qualities joined together in him for good and benefit, and they stood by him to make his purpose prosper in his hand. His wisdom and his faith, his uprightness and his righteousness toward God and man, and his calm of spirit in his dispute won him all hearts together; his tongue was choice silver [Proverbs 10:20], knowing how to order his words with judgment, and this gained him the favor of every man and gave him grace when he stood before king and princes. Through these qualities too he was close to the throne, and beloved and favored by Queen Shelamzion (according to some he was her brother (Berakhot 48a)). Not many days passed before he defeated the Sadducees with his proofs. Many of them, who would not bow under the force of his proofs and were powerless to answer them, chose to leave their seat in the Sanhedrin, until they had all separated from the Sanhedrin and Pharisees came in their place, as Simeon had hoped from the start. So it was, and so it was done before the eyes of King Yannai and Queen Shelamzion. For then, when the Sadducees were sitting in their Sanhedrin, King Yannai and Queen Shelamzion, who sat beside him, were sitting at their gathering, and not one of Israel (that is, of the Pharisees) sat with them except Simeon ben Shetaḥ. They would ask for responsa and halakhot and did not know how to bring proof from the Torah. Simeon ben Shetaḥ said to them: "Whoever knows how to bring proof from the Torah shall be fit to sit in the Sanhedrin." Once a practical case arose among them and they did not know how to bring proof from the Torah, except for one elder who babbled against him and said to him: "Give me time." He went and sat by himself, and when he saw that he did not know how to bring proof from the Torah, the next day he was ashamed to come to the Sanhedrin, and Simeon ben Shetaḥ set one of the students in his place. And so he did every single day until they had all withdrawn and the Sanhedrin of Israel sat according to their view; and that day they made a festival. That day was the twenty-eighth of the month of Tevet (Megillat Ta'anit ch. 10). And when Simeon ben Shetaḥ had defeated the Sadducees by his wisdom, he remembered Joshua ben Peraḥiah, who had come down from his office of Nasi and fled to Egypt, and he made haste to call him to Jerusalem. Simeon wrote to the heads of the people in Alexandria in these words: "From Jerusalem the holy city to you, Alexandria my sister: my husband dwells in your midst and I sit desolate" (Bavli Sotah, Amsterdam edition). Because of this call Joshua returned, and without doubt he became Nasi as he had been before, and Simeon ben Shetaḥ was Av Beit Din, for one raises in holiness and does not lower.1 At that time too they abolished the book of statutes called Sefer Gezerta, which the Sadducees had for capital cases, fines and civil cases. For when the Sadducees stood in the Sanhedrin, the Sadducees had a book of decrees written and laid out: these are stoned, these are burned, these are beheaded, these are strangled; and when they judged a man, they would keep asking and looking in the book. This book they abolished, and that day they made a festival; that day was the fourteenth of the month of Tammuz (Megillat Ta'anit ch. 4). Now we do not know how many years the rule of the Sadducees lasted, for we are told only that they grew strong at the end of John's life, but it is not known how many years before his death this event took place; and the reign of Aristobulus lasted only a little more than one year. When Yannai came to the throne, the matter remained as it had been at the end of John's reign. The wars that he fought and the hardships that passed over him did not allow him to set his eye on religious matters, and therefore Simeon ben Shetaḥ, whom the queen helped, succeeded in removing the Sadducees little by little from the Sanhedrin, until at last they had all been removed and all the members of the Sanhedrin were Pharisees. In any case it appears from all this that days and years passed during which the world was desolate, until Simeon ben Shetaḥ came and removed the Sadducees, until he left them not a single survivor in the Sanhedrin.

תחת ממשלת ינאי עוד יד הצדוקים על העליונה וישבו ראשונה בסנהדרין. כי גזרת יוחנן לא כלתה ולא נתבטלה. הפרושים נמנעו לשבת עם הצדוקים בסנהדרין. מלבד חכם אחד מן הפרושים, התאמץ בכל זאת לשבת בסנהדרין אף כי הוכרח לבטל תמיד את דעתו מפני דעתם כי הם היו הרוב. אבל לא מאהבת הכבוד והשררה עשה זאת. כי לאיש היושב במושב מתנגדים אשר כל דבריו אינם נשמעים איה הכבוד ואיה השררה? אבל הכרתו הפנימית מצדקת הדעות ואמיתתן אשר נשא בלבו ועל שפתיו היא עשתה זאת. הוא לא ייעף ולא יגע מהתנגד להם אף אם כלה כחו להבל. ראה גם ידע כי לא לעולם חסנם ואין מעמד לדעותיהם. כי יסודי דעות הצדוקים לא יתכנו עמוד נגד העת ושינוייה אשר לא כן דעות הפרושים אשר לעומתן ולעמת דרכי המדרש לפרושים כל הזמנים עם שנוייהם ומשאליהם יתכנו; על כן היה לבו בטוח כי יבואו הימים ושררת הצדוקים תכנע ועם הבטחון הזה איך יטמון ידו בצלחת? החכם הזה היה, שמעון בן שטח. הוא מאס להתרחק מהם ולהניח להם יד לעשות כחפץ לבם. רק עמד נגדם בהתנגדות גלויה מונהגת במועצות ודעת ועל יד מופתים וראיות מכריחים. מהם היה מקוה להתגבר עליהם מעט מעט ולדחפם ממצבם פסע אחר פסע. וגם לא היתה תוחלתו נכזבה. ענינים שונים התחברו בקרבו לטוב ולתועלת, והם עמדו לו להצליח חפצו בידו. חכמתו ואמונתו ישרו וצדקתו נגד אלהים ואדם ושאננות רוח בריבו הם קנו לו כל הלבבות יחד, לשונו כסף נבחר לדעת לכלכל דבריו במשפט, זה הפיק לו את רצון כל איש, וימציאהו חן בהתיצבו לפני מלך ושרים. אף בגלל המדות האלה היה קרוב למלכות ואהוב ונרצה למלכה שלמינון. (לדעת קצת היה אחיה (ברכות מ"ח.)) לא ארכו הימים עד כי נצח את הצדוקים בראיותיו. רבים מהם אשר לא רצו לכוף תחת כח מופתיו ומאין לאל ידם להשיב עליהם בחרו לעזוב מושבם בסנהדרין, עד p. 128 כי נבדלו כלם מתוך הסנהדרין ויבואו פרושים תחתיו, כאשר קוה שמעון מאז. כן היה וכן נעשה לנגד עיני ינאי המלך ושלמינון המלכה. כי אז בהיות הצדוקים יושבים בסנהדרין שלהם, היה ינאי המלך ושלמינון המלכה היושבת אצלו יושבים במסיבתם ולא אחד מישראל (כלומר מן הפרושים) יושב עמהם חוץ משמעון בן שטח. והיו שואלים תשובות והלכות ולא היו יודעים להביא ראיה מן התורה אמר להם שמעון בן שטח כל מי שיודע להביא ראיה מן התורה יהיה ראוי לישב בסנהדרין — פעם אחת נפל דבר של מעשה ביניהם ולא היו יודעים להביא ראיה מן התורה. חוץ מזקן אחד שפטפט נגדו ואמר לו תן לי זמן — הלך וישב בינו לבין עצמו וכיון שראה שלא היה יודע להביא ראיה מן התורה למחר נתבייש מלבא בסנהדרין והעמיד שמעון בן שטח אחד מן התלמידים במקומו — וכן עשה בכל יום ויום עד שנסתלקו כלם וישבה סנהדרין של ישראל על דעתם ואותו היום עשאוהו יום טוב. וזה היום היה בעשרים ושמונה לחדש טבת (מ"ת פ"י). ויהי כאשר נצח שמעון בן שטח בחכמתו את הצדוקים, אז זכר את יהושע בן פרחיה אשר ירד מנשיאותו ויברח לו מצרימה, ויתאמץ מהר לקרוא לו ירושלימה, ויכתוב שמעון אל ראשי העם אשר באלכסנדריא כדברים האלה: מני ירושלים עיר הקדש לך אלכסנדריא אחותי, בעלי שרוי בתוכך ואני יושבת שוממה (בבלי סוטה דפוס אמ"ד). ואף לסבת הקריאה הזאת שב יהושע ובלי ספק נעשה נשיא כאשר היה מקדם ושמעון בן שטח היה אב בית דין שמעלין בקדש ואין מורידין1. גם בעת ההיא בטלו את ספר חקת הדינים הנקרא ספר גזרתא אשר היה לצדוקים בדיני נפשות וקנסות ובדיני ממון כי כאשר עמדו הצדוקים בסנהדרין, היה כתוב ומונח לצדוקים ספר גזרתא, אלו שנסקלים ואלו שנשרפים ואלו שנהרגים ואלו שנחנקים וכשהיו דנים אדם שואל והולך ורואה בספר, וזה הספר בטלו ואותו היום עשאוהו יום טוב, וזה היום היה בארבעה עשר לחדש תמוז (מ"ת פ"ד). הנה לא ידענו כמה שנים התמידה ממשלת הצדוקים, כי לא הוגד לנו רק שבסוף חיי יוחנן התחזקו אבל לא נודע כמה שנים לפני מותו אשר היה המאורע הזה, ומלכות אריסטובול לא נמשך רק מעט יותר משנה אחת. וכאשר בא ינאי למלוכה נשאר הדבר על איתנו כאשר היה באחרית ממלכת יוחנן. ומלחמותיו אשר נלחם והתלאות אשר עברו עליו לא נתנוהו לשים עינו על העניינים הדתיים, ועל כן הצליח שמעון בן שטח, אשר המלכה היתה בעוזריו, להרחיק מעט מעט את הצדוקים מתוך הסנהדרין, עד כי לבסוף נתרחקו כלם והיו כל בעלי הסנהדרין מן הפרושים. ואמנם על כל פנים נראה מכל זה שעברו ימים ושנים מה שהיה העולם משתומם עד אשר בא שמעון בן שטח והרחיק את הצדוקים עד בלתי השאיר להם שריד אחד בסנהדרין.

But the days of tranquillity did not last long. Yannai returned from his wars, and for an unknown reason his heart turned against the Pharisees. Then it happened once, on the Festival of Tabernacles, that he stood on the altar to pour out the water on the festival according to the custom of the Pharisees. But with special intent he did not do it as commanded; rather he poured the water on his feet, to show the Pharisees that he did not acknowledge the water libation (Sukkah 48b). This was a clear sign to them that his heart was not loyal to the Pharisees, and from his deeds they recognized his intention, that he was seeking a pretext to come against the Pharisees with a charge. Because of this, wrath broke out on the part of the people, and they pelted him with their etrogim and called him "son of a captive woman" (Josephus, Antiquities, book 13). Then he rose against them in burning anger and took his revenge, and made a great slaughter among them. There was then a fierce strife between him and the people, and his hatred of the Pharisees grew day by day. He hanged eight hundred of the rebels on the tree, and of those who followed the Pharisees some fled (Megillat Ta'anit ch. 12) and some hid, and matters were not restored until after his death. Simeon too was in hiding from the king's wrath. But it is not known for certain whether he was among the rebels and escaped with his life like all his companions, or whether the cause of his flight was another quarrel that arose between him and Yannai. For it is told thus: Once three hundred poor Nazirites came, and Simeon interceded for them before Yannai, that he should give from his money enough for half the offerings that they were obliged to bring for their naziriteship, and he (Simeon) himself would undertake to give them the remaining half. Yannai granted his request and gave half the offerings, and Simeon used a stratagem and released the vow of half of them, so that they no longer needed an offering. The matter became known to Yannai, and his wrath against Simeon was great, because he had dared to make sport of him; and for this reason Simeon hid from him. So the cause of his flight is told. And of the cause of his return it is told: At that time noble princes came from Persia to visit Yannai in his palace. As they sat at the banquet, they said to Yannai: We remember that many years ago, when we were here to visit you in your palace as today, an old man sat before you all of whose words were wisdom, and we delighted in them; why has he not come to the banquet today as at other times? Then, at his guests' request, Yannai sent messengers to seek Simeon, to call him and to assure him that he had forgotten how he had provoked the eyes of his glory and that he wished to make peace with him. He was sought and found, and Simeon did as the king said and came before him. Yannai bade him sit, and Simeon set his seat between the king and the queen. But Yannai, although he had promised Simeon to make peace with him, still could not restrain himself from demanding an account from Simeon of the affair with the Nazirites, and he asked him: Now tell me, what led your heart to make sport of me? Simeon answered and said: I did not make sport of you; I spoke what is right and true. For just as you gave half the offerings from your money, so I exempted them from the second half by my Torah; and do you not know that "wisdom is a defence, and money is a defence" [Ecclesiastes 7:12]? "And why did you hide by fleeing?" He answered him: Because of your anger, for I said to myself, "hide thyself as it were for a little moment, until the indignation be overpast" [Isaiah 26:20]! Yannai went on and asked him: Why did you set your seat between the king and the queen? Simeon answered him: So it befits masters of wisdom, according to the words of Ben Sira: "Exalt her, and she shall promote thee, and she will seat you among princes." ⚠1 Then Yannai ordered that Simeon be given a cup of wine to say the Grace after Meals. They gave him the cup, and Simeon began: Let us bless for the food that Yannai and his companions have eaten. Yannai said to him: Indeed you are still in your stubbornness. "But," said Simeon, "shall I say: Let us bless for the food that I have not eaten?" Then Yannai ordered that he be given food, and he ate and said the blessing as is proper (Yerushalmi Berakhot 7:2 and Nazir 5:5; Genesis Rabbah 91; and Ecclesiastes Rabbah). So the cause of his flight and of his return is told. But be it one way or the other, in any case we see that there was a great rift between him and the sages, so much so that even Simeon, who was (according to some) the queen's brother, feared that for so small a cause he would kill him. Indeed there is no doubt that Simeon's return was for the good and benefit of all the Pharisees: even if matters were not restored to their former strength, Yannai still did not persecute them, through Simeon's efforts. Indeed, after Yannai's death the Pharisees returned to their place in the Sanhedrin; and Yannai himself recognized at the end of his days that if the Jewish ruler leaned on the Pharisees, his kingdom would be firm in his hand, since most of the people followed them and conducted themselves by their counsel. Accordingly he advised his wife Shelaminon [Salome Alexandra] before his death to strengthen the hands of the Pharisees (Antiquities, ibid.). And these were the last words he spoke to his wife: Fear neither the Pharisees nor those who are not Pharisees; fear the hypocrites who resemble Pharisees, who do the deed of Zimri and seek the reward of Phinehas (Sotah 22b).

Shelaminon kept Yannai's last words, because they agreed with the desire of her heart; for she had long favoured the ways of the Pharisees. Immediately after her husband's death she made a covenant with the Pharisees, and her desire prospered in her hand, for the Pharisees upheld her rule. Then Simeon stood with all his strength against the Sadducees. His first effort was to make a public demonstration against them, and all the teachings he taught and the ordinances he enacted were aimed against the views of the Sadducees, to uproot them. The sages of that time gave a general reinforcement to their words by expounding from the book of the Torah of Moses that any man who rebels against a statute of the Torah and rules on that statute in a way other than the statute was understood according to the views of the majority of a Sanhedrin of Pharisees, his ruling being for practice, is called a rebellious elder (zaken mamre), and his sentence is death; for he is the man of whom, according to the midrash of the Pharisees, it is said: "even that man shall die: and thou shalt put away the evil from Israel" (Deuteronomy 17:12). Whether this statute was decreed for every kind of ruling that touches any one of the commandments of the Lord, or was said only of matters whose wilful transgression incurs karet, as one of the sages of the Mishnah holds, this we cannot decide. But an old halakhah, cited as an example to show in what way the rebellious elder becomes liable, is the witness and the proof that the point was not the severity of the punishment; rather, everything depended on whether the elder's ruling opposed one of the words of the Scribes in the interpretation of the Torah. For that halakhah gives as an example: one who says there are five totafot (in tefillin), to add to the words of the Scribes, is liable.2 Indeed, in the days of the later sages the original reason of the statute had already been lost, and it no longer had any practical purpose, only a theoretical one. Therefore it came to be counted among several other laws that were no longer practised, which they studied only to "magnify the Torah and make it glorious" [Isaiah 42:21]; and at times they drew consequences from it by the ways of their midrash which had not been so in former times, while the statute was still alive in practice. And perhaps already at that time they gave reinforcement also to the ordinances and decrees of the Scribes, and based them on the word of the Torah, "thou shalt not decline from the sentence which they shall shew thee" [Deuteronomy 17:11], meaning: the Torah obliges one to act according to the teaching of the Scribes even where they do not come by the force of their midrash.3 This, then, is the general reinforcement; but in particular matters too there is opposition with a special intent. Most of the halakhot mentioned in the name of Simeon ben Shetaḥ are laws in matters of judicial statutes, in capital and monetary cases. Opposition in this area was most necessary for them, since during the whole time that the hand of the Sadducees was uppermost they enacted statutes that were not in accordance with the Torah in the view of the Pharisees, and they dared to make a book of statutes on capital and monetary law. Some of those statutes have come down to us. The Sadducees used to say: A plotting witness (ed zomem) is not put to death until the accused has been put to death (Makkot 5b). Judah ben Tabbai himself relates that he put a plotting witness to death for his intent, to remove [the view] from the hearts of the Sadducees, who used to say that plotting witnesses are not put to death until [the accused] has been put to death. In his zeal he overstepped the bounds and erred in law, putting to death the one witness proven a plotter when the second witness had not been proven a plotter; and Simeon ben Shetaḥ rebuked him, saying: You have shed innocent blood, for the sages said: Plotting witnesses are not put to death until both of them are proven plotters (ibid., and Ḥagigah 16b; Tosefta Sanhedrin ch. 6; and Mekhilta Mishpatim ch. 20). But this was agreed among them: that plotting witnesses are put to death for their intent if the verdict against the accused has been concluded; and that is the meaning of the halakhah: "a plotting witness is not put to death until the verdict has been concluded." The statute that plotting witnesses are put to death only if the accused was put to death on their testimony was newly enacted by the Sadducees, but the Pharisees' understanding of the law of hazamah was the received one; for from of old it was a statute that plotting witnesses were judged with the fitting punishment even for their intent. Indeed there is no doubt that when they said, "a plotting witness is not put to death until the verdict has been concluded," they did not mean to exclude the case where they killed, as if they are then not put to death; for if we say so, the measure of justice would be defective. And although the later sages thought so, and one whose personal name is not known said, "If they did not kill, they are put to death; if they killed, they are not put to death" (Makkot 5b), he did not say this from tradition; it is his own opinion. For had this been a tradition received by all the sages, the father of that sage would not have contradicted his son from reasoning and the judgment of the intellect (also there). But this stood firm: plotting witnesses are judged for their intent. Already in the days of the author of the apocryphal story "Susanna" the law of hazamah was understood in this way. For he relates that the two elders who testified against Susanna that she had committed harlotry were proven plotters after her death sentence had been concluded, and they were dealt with according to the law of Moses: "then shall ye do unto him, as he had thought to have done unto his brother" [Deuteronomy 19:19] (Susanna 61, 62). The time of composition of this story is very early, for in it the law of hazamah was still understood in a way far removed from its meaning in the days of Simeon ben Shetaḥ. For according to that story the witnesses contradicted one another in the examination: one said that Susanna had committed the lewd act under an oak, and the other said under a terebinth; ⚠2 and because of this contradiction they judged that they had testified falsely against her, and sentenced them to death according to the law of plotting witnesses. And it is true that several traces of this understanding are found in the books of the Oral Torah, which have preserved for us old halakhot in this area whose plain sense upholds this understanding. When they said, "he is not liable until he contradicts himself," "until they prove themselves plotters," "the witness makes himself false, makes his fellow false" (Sifre Shofetim §§189, 190), do these expressions not show that the essence of hazamah is the contradiction of the witnesses, so that through the contradiction, when they contradict one another in the inquiries or contradict themselves, it becomes clear that they testified falsely? And this understanding agrees with the understanding of the law in the days of the writer of the story of Susanna. But at a later time they gave all these expressions another sense, and explained them all as hazamah that comes from outside, through other witnesses who testify about the persons of the first witnesses (Geiger, Urschrift, p. 196). ⚠3 But this seems clear: the halakhah that Simeon put forward in the name of his predecessors, "plotting witnesses are not put to death until both are proven plotters," can be explained only if we decide that the hazamah comes from outside; for of witnesses who contradict one another one cannot say that both were proven plotters. Certainly this halakhah speaks of hazamah that comes from other witnesses; and already in Simeon's days they had changed the reason of the old halakhah.

הלכות ישנות בפנה הזאת אשר משמעות פשוטן מקיימת את המובן הזה. ואם אמרו אינו חייב עד שיכחיש את עצמו, עד שיזימו את עצמם, שהעד עושה עצמו שקר, עושה חבירו שקר (ספרי שופטים פי' קפ"ט, ק"צ) הלא אלה הדבורים מראים כי מהות ההזמה היא הכחשת העדים עד שעל ידי ההכחשה שמכחישים זה את זה בחקירות או שמכחישים את עצמם מבורר שענו שקר וזה המובן מתאים עם מובן החק בימי כותב הספור שושנה. אמנם בזמן מאוחר נתנו לכל הדבורים האלה כונה אחרת ופירש כלם על הזמה הבאה מחוץ על פי עדים אחרים המעידים בגופם של העדים הראשונים (רא"ג א. ש. 196). אבל זה נראה ברור כי ההלכה אשר העמיד שמעון בשם קדמוניו: אין עדים זוממין נהרגים עד שיוזמו שניהם, שהיא אינה מתפרשת רק אם נחליט שההזמה באה מצד חוץ כי על הכחשת העדים זה את זה לא יתכן לאמר שנוזמו שניהם. וודאי שהלכה זו מדברת מהזמה הבאה מעדים אחרים, וכבר בימי שמעון שנו טעם ההלכה הישנה.

Indeed, this ancient halakhah, that plotting witnesses are judged for their intent, which the Sadducees removed from their statute of law and which Simeon ben Shetaḥ and Judah ben Tabbai restored, had great consequences. The first: that witnesses who have testified, and the verdict has been concluded on their word, cannot retract and testify otherwise. For if witnesses could retract and testify otherwise, how could it happen that plotting witnesses are put to death once the verdict has been concluded? Could they not retract their first words? And we have a clear hint, in a story of an incident that happened in those days, that this halakhah concerning the retraction of witnesses from their words is closely related to the ancient halakhah mentioned above; and if we look into the matter we see that the one is a consequence of the other. For it is told that Simeon ben Shetaḥ sentenced eighty women sorcerers and hanged them on the tree in one day (Sanhedrin 45b). This was evil in the eyes of their relatives, and they wished to take revenge on him. Two base men arose and testified against Simeon's son that he had committed a capital sin. And as he was led out to be executed, the witnesses repented and confessed that they had testified falsely. And the condemned man said to his father: Father, if you seek to bring deliverance through your hand, make me as a threshold (Yerushalmi Sanhedrin ch. 6). From this one may judge that Simeon wished to believe the witnesses who had retracted their testimony. But the son reminded his father that the teaching of the Pharisees in the laws of testimony does not allow witnesses to retract their first testimony after the verdict has been concluded. For if witnesses could retract and testify otherwise, it would be impossible to punish plotting witnesses for their intent and put them to death even if they did not kill: would not every witness, if proven a plotter, retract his testimony and go free? And this halakhah, "if they did not kill, they are put to death," was surely among the greatest points of opposition between the Sadducees and the Pharisees. Whether the Sadducees also opposed the halakhah that witnesses are not put to death until both are proven plotters is not known to us; but it seems that, although Simeon brought it from his predecessors, it was not widely known, for we see that Judah ben Tabbai erred in it and thought that even if only one witness was proven a plotter he is put to death (Makkot 5b, and in several places). No doubt Judah's error came to him from the sense of the verse that says, "and, behold, if the witness be a false witness" [Deuteronomy 19:18], which, taken literally, refers also to a single witness. But Simeon ben Shetaḥ relied on a binyan av: in one place the Torah was precise and wrote "one witness" [Deuteronomy 19:15], to teach that wherever "witness" is said without qualification it is to be understood in a general sense and refers to complete testimony, that is, two witnesses (Sotah 2b). Now there is no doubt that the Sadducees too expounded the words literally, as was their way, and put a plotting witness to death even if both were not plotters.4 Several halakhot we learn from an incident that happened to him with King Yannai. Yannai's slave killed a person. They sent word to Yannai: Your slave has killed a person. Yannai sent his slave to the court. But they answered him that he himself was obliged to come, since by the law of the Torah the master stands trial for the damages of his slave, just as the owner of an ox must stand trial for his ox. Yannai came and sat before them. Simeon ben Shetaḥ said to him: King Yannai, stand on your feet and give honour to the statute of the Torah, as it is said: "Then both the men, between whom the controversy is, shall stand" [Deuteronomy 19:17]. Yannai said to him: Not as you say, but as your colleagues say. But his colleagues, out of fear of the king, were silent. Simeon said to them: You are men of [hidden] thoughts; let the Master of thoughts come and exact punishment from you (Sanhedrin 19a). Now if on the one side we see that Simeon showed no favour in judgment, on the other side it appears that Simeon himself recognized from this incident that not every time is fit to hold fast to the rule "let the law pierce the mountain"; for because of this incident it became a statute: The king does not judge and is not judged (Sanhedrin 19b). From the details of this incident we also learn two further principles in the understanding of the laws of the Torah. The first: that the slave was regarded by them as a man's ox. The second: that they understood the verse "Then both the men, between whom the controversy is, shall stand" literally: not as referring to coming forward to judgment, but that the word "shall stand" is to be understood as actual standing, teaching that the litigant is obliged to stand (Shevu'ot 30a).5

אמנם ההלכה הקדומה הזאת שעדים זוממין נדונים על המחשבה, אשר הצדוקים העבירוה בחקת משפטם והחזירוה שמעון ב"ש ויהודה ב"ט היו לה תולדות גדולות. האחת שעדים שהעידו ונגמר הדין על פיהם שאינם יכולים לחזור ולהגיד. כי אלו יכולים עדים לחזור ולהגיד איך נמצא שיהרגו עדים זוממים משנגמר הדין הלא יכולים לחזור מדבריהם הראשונים? ויש לנו רמז ברור בספור אחד ממעשה שאירע בימים ההם, שזאת ההלכה בבחינת חזרת העדים מדבריהם יש לה יחוס גדול להלכה הקדומה הנזכרת ואם נסתכל בדבר נראה שהאחת תולדה מהאחרת. כי יסופר ששמעון ב"ש דן שמונים נשים מכשפות ותלה אותן על העץ ביום אחד (סנהדרין מ"ה:) ויהי הדבר הזה רע בעיני קרוביהן, ורצו להנקם ממנו. ועמדו שני אנשים בני בליעל והעידו על בנו של שמעון שעבר על חטא משפט מות. ויהי כאשר הוא מוצא ליהרג התנחמו העדים והודו כי שקר ענו. ויאמר הנדון לאביו: אבי אם בקשת להביא תשועה על ידך עשה אותי כאסקופה (ירוש' סנהדרין פ"ו). מזה יש לשפוט כי רצה שמעון להאמין להעדים שחזרו מהגדתם. אבל הבן יזכיר את אביו, כי תורת הפרושים בדיני עדות אינה סובלת בעדים חזרה מהגדתם הראשונה אחר שנגמר p. 132 הדין. כי אילו יכולים העדים לחזור ולהגיד אי אפשר לענוש עדים זוממים על מחשבתם ולהרגם אף אם לא הרגו, הלא כל עד אם יוזם יחזור מהגדתו ויפטור. וזאת ההלכה לא הרגו נהרגים הלא היתה מן ההתנגדות היותר גדולות בין הצדוקים והפרושים. אם התנגדו הצדוקים גם להלכה: שאין עדים נהרגין עד שיוזמו שניהם, לא הודע לנו, אבל נראה כי אף ששמעון הביאה מקדמוניו לא היתה מפורסמת כי ראינו שיהודה ב"ט טעה בה וחשב שאף אם לא הוזם רק עד אחד שיהרג (מכות ה': ובכ"מ), ובלי ספק טעותו של יהודה יצא לו ממשמעות הכתוב שאומר: והנה עד שקר העד. שלפי הדברים ככתבם מכוון גם על עד אחד, אבל שמעון ב"ש סמך על בנין אב. שבמקום אחד דקדקה התורה וכתבה עד אחד ללמד שכל מקום שנאמר עד סתם יובן בהוראה כללית ומכוון על עדות שלמה שהוא שני עדים (סוטה ב':), ועתה אין ספק שגם הצדוקים דרשו דברים ככתבם כדרכם והרגו עד זומם אף אם אין שניהם זוממין4. כמה הלכות נלמד ממעשה שאירע לו עם ינאי המלך. עבדו של ינאי הרג את הנפש, שלחו לו לינאי: עבדך הרג את הנפש, וישלח ינאי את עבדו לבית דין. אך הם השיבוהו, כי הוא עצמו חייב לבא, יען כי לפי דת התורה יעמוד האדון בדין על נזקי עבדו כמו שבעל השור חייב לעמוד על שורו. ויבא ינאי וישב לפניהם. ויאמר לו שמעון ב"ש ינאי המלך עמוד על רגליך ותן כבוד לחק התורה שנאמר ועמדו שני האנשים אשר להם הריב. אמר לו ינאי: לא כשתאמר אתה אלא כשיאמרו חבריך. אבל חבריו מיראתם את המלך החרישו. ויאמר אליהם שמעון: בעלי מחשבות אתם יבא בעל מחשבות ויפרע מכם (סנהדרין י"ט.) והנה אם מצד האחד אנו רואים כי לא נשא שמעון פנים בדין. הנה מצד השני נראה כי שמעון עצמו הכיר מתוך המעשה כי אין כל זמן כשר להחזיק את הכלל „יקוב הדין את ההר" כי לסבת המעשה הזה היה לחק: המלך לא דן ולא דנין אותו (סנהדרין י"ט:) גם מפרטי הדברים במעשה הזה אנו למדים עוד שני עיקרים במובן משפטי התורה. האחד: שהעבד היה אצלם בערך שורו של אדם. והשני שהבינו את המקרא: ועמדו שני האנשים אשר להם הריב, דברים ככתבם. לא שיובן על הקריבה לדין אלא שדבור „ועמדו" יובן על עמידה ממש ולהורות שחובה על הנדון לעמוד (שבועות ל'.)5.

The strife with the Sadducees and the victory over them brought to light several laws in various matters. In that time is found the first trace of the midrash on the statute of the Torah "eye for eye" [Exodus 21:24], whose meaning is payment for an eye (Bava Kamma 83a; Mekhilta Mishpatim), against the view of the Sadducees, who said: an actual eye. The Sadducees' view in this is the view found among all the ancient lawgivers of most lands6 ⚠4; and the first among the nations who had the statute of paying for bodily injuries with money were the Romans. Now it is an error to think that the Pharisees shaped this statute of the Torah according to what they knew of the law of retaliation among the Romans. For this description is rooted in the nature of man's recognition of law and justice; and since this recognition of justice changes according to the level of enlightenment, the law of retaliation laid down in this statute also changes, and becomes different among a people that has risen from step to step on the ladder of enlightenment from what it was among that same people while it stood on a lower step of that ladder. Therefore the change in the recognition of justice, and the product of that recognition, which is the change in the meaning of the statute, are natural matters and are alike among every people. Now this interpretation of the statute "eye for eye" as referring to payment of money they did not take from any nation; it was original among the Jews, and they weighed, searched out and founded it from their inner recognition of justice and according to the level of enlightenment to which they rose in the course of time. Likewise the interpretations that the Sadducees gave of the statute of one who brings an evil name upon his wife, saying that the meaning of the Torah's words "and they shall spread the cloth" [Deuteronomy 22:17] is literal, and likewise that the words "and spit in his face" in the statute of ḥalitzah [Deuteronomy 25:9] mean only the literal sense, that she should spit in his very face, that is, on the surface of the face: all these were abolished in the days of Simeon ben Shetaḥ (Megillat Ta'anit ch. 4).7 On one matter Simeon and his colleagues strove with the Sadducees with great force, and the strife lasted eight days, until they defeated the Sadducees. For the Sadducees said that the daily offering (tamid) comes from the property of an individual, and they did not want the daily offerings to be offered from public funds, since this caused great expense to the Temple treasury. Their proof was from the Torah's words, "The one lamb shalt thou offer in the morning" [Numbers 28:4]: the singular implies an individual (Menaḥot 65a). The sages said to them: You are not permitted to do so, for a public offering comes only from the property of all Israel; "thou shalt offer" means all Israel, and for that reason it is said in the same section, "shall ye observe to offer unto me" [Numbers 28:2], in the plural. And when they prevailed over them and defeated them, they ordained that they would weigh out their shekels and deposit them in the chamber, and the daily offerings were offered from public funds (Megillat Ta'anit ch. 1). As long as the hand of the Sadducees was uppermost, no doubt the priests acted according to their views; and against them is aimed what the sages said, "You are not permitted to do so," that is, to offer the daily offerings from an individual's donation. And although it is not said explicitly that this happened in Simeon's days, there is no room for doubt about it: the phrase "when they prevailed over them and defeated them" points to a fierce strife and a victory of the Pharisees, and this was in Simeon's days and under the rule of Yannai (Graetz, vol. 3, p. 472). Another ordinance concerning the Temple service, directed against the view of the Sadducees, should also be ascribed to Simeon's time. For the Sadducees said that the red heifer of purification is prepared only by those whose sun has set [after their immersion]; and because of this view the Pharisees ordained that they would actually defile the priest who burns the heifer and then immerse him, so that the heifer would be prepared by a tevul yom (Parah 3:3). ⚠5 The account of these things shows that they speak of an incident that actually occurred at some time, when it happened that they prepared a red heifer, and then they enacted this ordinance to make a public demonstration against the Sadducees. The most suitable time for this event and this ordinance was after the victory of the Pharisees, that is, after the death of Yannai, when his son Yoḥanan Hyrcanus was high priest; for at that time they prepared a red heifer. For in the matter of the red heifer it is told that Simeon the Just made two, and Yoḥanan the high priest also made two (ibid. 3:8). ⚠6 This is very strange: in the days of Simeon the Just, which were at most forty years (according to the Talmud), they needed two; from then until the days of Yoḥanan the high priest the one that Simeon made sufficed; and in the thirty-one years (or, according to the Talmud, eighty years) that Yoḥanan reigned, one did not suffice them and he had to make two. And it is very unlikely that Yoḥanan made one at his beginning, and afterwards, when he had become a Sadducee and wished to make a demonstration against the Pharisees, made a second by those whose sun had set, according to the view of the Sadducees; for although Yoḥanan followed the Sadducees, it is still not credible that he would cast aspersion on a red heifer that he himself had made. But let us suppose that the tradition of this Mishnah is true in its substance but not exact. It is true that a high priest called Simeon the Just made a red heifer, and it sufficed until the days of the second Simeon the Just, who made another; and from this arose the aggadah that Simeon the Just made two. And so it is with Yoḥanan the high priest: it is true that Yoḥanan the high priest made a red heifer. And indeed in the days of Simeon ben Shetaḥ they strove with all their strength to do things publicly in order to oppose the Sadducees; therefore it is very likely that after Yannai's death, when the hand of the Pharisees grew strong and they enacted many things to subdue the Sadducees, then too they prepared a red heifer by a tevul yom. Both were called by the name of Yoḥanan the high priest because in truth both were made by a high priest named Yoḥanan, except that the first was made by the first Yoḥanan the high priest, the son of Simeon the Hasmonean, and the second was made by Yoḥanan the high priest, the grandson of the first and the son of Yannai. Now we see that in the days of Simeon ben Shetaḥ a red heifer was prepared, and on that occasion they ordained that they would defile the priest who burns the heifer.

הריב עם הצדוקים והנצוח עליהם הוציא לאור כמה דינים בענינים שונים. בזמן ההוא נמצא הרושם הראשון למדרש על חק התורה: עין תחת עין, שכונתו, תשלומי עין (ב"ק פ"ג. מכילתא משפטים) נגד דעת הצדוקים שהיו אומרים עין ממש. ודעת הצדוקים בזה היא הדעה הנמצאת אצל כל המחוקקים הקדמונים של רוב הארצות6. והראשונים בין העמים אשר היה להם החק לשלם נזקי הגוף בתשלומי ממון היו הרומיים. והנה זה טעות לחשוב p. 133 שהפרושים תארו החק הזה שבתורה על פי הנודע להם ממשפט הגמול אצל הרומיים כי המתואר הזה מונח בטבע הכרת האדם את המשפט והצדק, ובהיות שהכרת הצדק הזאת מתחלפת לפי מדרגת ההשכלה, על כן גם משפט הגמול אשר הונח בחק הזה מתחלף הוא ומשתנה להיות באופן אחר אצל עם אחרי אשר עלה ממעלה אל מעלה בסולם ההשכלה מאשר היה אצל העם ההוא עצמו בעוד שעמד על הסולם ההוא במדרגה למטה, ועל כן התחלפות הכרת הצדק ותולדת ההכרה הזאת אשר היא השנוי במובן החק הם דברים טבעיים ושוים הם אצל כל עם ועם. ועתה פירוש זה החק של עין תחת עין שהוא מכוון על תשלומי ממון לא לקחו להם משום אומה, אבל היה מקורי אצל היהודים, ואזנו וחקרו ויסדו אותו מצד הכרתם הפנימית מן הצדק ולפי מדרגת ההשכלה אשר התנשאו עליה במשך הזמנים. גם הפירושים אשר פירשו הצדוקים את החק של מוציא שם רע על אשתו ואמרו שכונת מאמר התורה: ופרשו את השמלה, היא דברים ככתבם, וכמו כן המאמר וירקה בפניו האמור בחק החליצה לא יכוון כי אם דברים ככתבם, שתהיה רוקקת בפניו ממש כלומר על שטח הפנים כלם בטלו בימי שמעון ב"ש (מ"ת פ"ד)7. על דבר אחד רבו שמעון וחבריו עם הצדוקים בחזק גדול, והתמידה המריבה שמונת ימים עד שנצחו את הצדוקים. שהצדוקים אמרו שקרבן התמיד בא משל יחיד, ולא רצו שיהיו התמידים קרבים משל צבור אשר זה גרם הוצאה גדולה לאוצר המקדש. וראיתם ממאמר התורה את הכבש אחד תעשה בבקר ליחיד משמע (מנחות ס"ח.) אמרו להם החכמים אי אתם רשאים לעשות כן לפי שאין קרבן צבור בא אלא משל כל ישראל, שתעשה, הכונה לכל ישראל שאף על כן נאמר באותה הפרשה תשמרו להקריב לי בלשון רבים, וכשגברו עליהם ונצחום התקינו שיהיו שוקלים שקליהם ומניחים אותם בלשכה והיו תמידים קרבים משל צבור (מ"ת פ"א) כל זמן שהיתה יד הצדוקים על העליונה בלי ספק היו הכהנים נוהגים כדעותיהם ואף נגדם מכוון מה שאמרו החכמים אי אתם רשאים לעשות כן כלומר להקריב תמידים משל נדבת יחיד. ואם גם שלא נזכר בפירוש כי בימי שמעון אירע כן, אין להטיל ספק בזה, המאמר „כשגברו עליהם ונצחום" מורה על מריבה חזקה וניצוח הפרושים וזה היה בימי שמעון ותחת ממשלת ינאי (גרעץ ח"ג 472) עוד תיקון אחר בבחינת העבודה במקדש ונגד דעת הצדוקים יש ליחס גם כן לזמנו של שמעון. שהיו הצדוקים אומרים כי פרת חטאת נעשית במעורבי שמש, ובגלל הדעה הזאת תקנו הפרושים להיות מטמאים בפועל את הכהן השורף את הפרה ומטבילים אותו כדי שתהיה הפרה נעשית בטבול יום (פרה פ"ג מ"ג). ספור הדברים האלה מראים כי הם מדברים ממעשה שנעשה בפועל באיזה זמן, שאירע שעשו פרת חטאת, ואז תקנו התקנה הזאת לעשות פירסום נגד הצדוקים. ואמנם הזמן היותר נאות למאורע ולתיקון הזה הוא היה אחרי נצוח הפרושים, וזה אחרי מות ינאי והיה בנו יוחנן הורקנוס כהן גדול כי בעת ההיא עשו פרת חטאת. כי בענין פרת חטאת יסופר ששמעון הצדיק עשה שתים ויוחנן כהן גדול עשה גם כן שתים. (שם מ"ח). והדבר הזה הוא זר מאד כי הצריכו בימי שמעון הצדיק שתים שאינם לכל היותר כי אם ארבעים שנה (לדעת התלמוד) ומאז והלאה עד ימי יוחנן כהן גדול היה די באותה שעשה שמעון, ול"א שנה (או לדעת התלמוד פ' שנה) שמלך יוחנן לא הספיקה להם אחת והיה צריך לעשות שתים. וזה רחוק מאד שיוחנן בתחלתו p. 134 עשה אחת ואחרי כן כשנעשה צדוקי רצה לעשות פרסום נגד הפרושים, ועשה שניה במעורבי שמש כדעת הצדוקים, כי אף שנמשך יוחנן אחרי הצדוקים בכל זאת אין להאמין שיהיה מוציא לעז על פרת חטאת שעשה הוא עצמו. אבל נניח שקבלת המשנה הזאת בעצמותה אמת אבל אינה בדקדוק. אמת הוא שכהן גדול הנקרא שמעון הצדיק עשה פרת חטאת והיתה מספיקה עד ימי שמעון הצדיק השני אשר הוא עשה אחרת ומזה נולדה ההגדה כי שמעון הצדיק עשה שתים. וכן הוא הענין ביוחנן כהן גדול אמת הוא כי יוחנן כהן גדול עשה פרת חטאת, ואמנם בימי שמעון בן שטח היו משתדלים בכל כחם לעשות מעשים בפרסום כדי להתנגד לצדוקים ועל כן קרוב הדבר מאד שאחרי מות ינאי שתקיפה יד הפרושים ותקנו הרבה דברים לרדות בם את הצדוקים שגם אז עשו פרת חטאת בטבול יום, ונקראו שתיהן על שם יוחנן כהן גדול כי באמת שתיהן נעשו על ידי כהן גדול הנקרא יוחנן, רק שהראשונה עשה יוחנן כה"ג הראשון והוא בן שמעון החשמונאי והשניה נעשית על ידי יוחנן כה"ג נכד הראשון ובנו של ינאי. ועתה נראה כי בימי שמעון ב"ש נעשה פרת חטאת ובמאורע ההוא תקנו שיהיו מטמאים את הכהן השורף את הפרה.

8

Since we see that the time of Simeon was the time of victory over the Sadducees, there is no doubt that the Pharisees then enacted many things against the Sadducees; and certainly, besides the matters of which it is said expressly that they were enacted to subdue the Sadducees, there are further ordinances and laws whose reason was to make a public demonstration against them, even though this is not mentioned explicitly; and it is very possible that the time of their birth was in the days of Simeon ben Shetaḥ, when the victory over them took place. Now we know that the opinion of the Sadducees is that the offering of the omer at Passover is "on the morrow of the sabbath" [Leviticus 23:15] literally, that is, on the first day of the week, and accordingly the festival of Atzeret also falls on the first day of the week; and we also know how many ordinances the Pharisees made to show the error of the Sadducees publicly. Therefore perhaps this too is the reason for their ordinance that they enacted: that people should begin to count aloud and publicly from the sixteenth of Nisan, which is "the morrow of the sabbath" according to the opinion of the Pharisees, and should count each and every day, which day it is of the days of the omer, so that there would be a public demonstration of the matter every day against the Sadducees (R. Aaron Jellinek). And this is our sign that this counting had a special reason: we see that in other commandments too the Torah spoke in this language, as in the commandment of the Jubilee, "And thou shalt number unto thee seven sabbaths of years" [Leviticus 25:8], and in the commandment of the woman's separation in the days of her impurity it is said, "then she shall number to herself seven days" [Leviticus 15:28]; and it did not occur to them to say that the Torah's intent was that they should count every week of the years of the Jubilee and each and every day of the seven clean days (Menaḥot 65b, Tosafot; Ran at the end of Pesaḥim). This can only be because in the counting of the days of the seven weeks between Passover and Atzeret there was a special reason, namely to make a public demonstration against the Sadducees.

אחרי ראותנו שזמנו של שמעון היה זמן הנצוח על הצדוקים על כן אין ספק כי אז תקנו הפרושים הרבה דברים נגד הצדוקים, וודאי שמלבד הדברים אשר נאמר עליהם ביחוד שנתקנו לרדות בם את הצדוקים, יש עוד תיקונים ודינים אשר סבתם לעשות פרסום נגדם אף שלא נזכרה זאת בפירוש, ואפשר מאד שזמן הולדת אותם היה בימי שמעון ב"ש שאז היה הניצוח עליהם. והנה ידענו כי דעת הצדוקים שהקרבת העמר בפסח היא ממחרת השבת ממש שהוא יום הראשון, ולפיהו יחול חג העצרת גם כן ביום הראשון בשבוע וידענו גם כן כי כמה תיקונים עשו הפרושים להראות טעות הצדוקים בפרסום, על כן אולי גם זה הסבה לתקנתם שתקנו שיהיו מתחילים לספור בפה ובפרסום מיום ט"ז בניסן, אשר הוא ממחרת השבת לדעת הפרושים, ויהיו מונים את כל יום ויום כמה הוא לימי העמר למען יהיה פרסום לדבר בכל יום ויום נגד הצדוקים (הר"ר אהרן יעללינעק) וזה לנו האות שהיתה זאת הספירה לסבה מיוחדת, אחרי שאנו רואים שגם במצות אחרות אמרה התורה כלשון הזה כמו במצות יובל, וספרת לך שבע שבתות שנים ובמצות פרישת האשה בימי נדתה נאמר וספרה לה שבעת ימים ולא עלה על דעתם לאמר שכונת התורה שיהיו סופרים את כל שבוע משנות היובל וכל יום ויום של שבעת נקיים (מנחות ס"ה: תוס', ר"ן סוף פסחים). אין זאת כי אם שבספירת ימי שבע השבועות שבין פסח לעצרת היתה סבה מיוחדת והיא לעשות פרסום נגד הצדוקים.

9

From these events it follows clearly for us that the testimony of the later sages, that Simeon restored the Torah to its former state (Kiddushin 66), is very trustworthy. For a long time the field of halakhah had lain desolate; the ways of study had changed their sense under the oppression of the Sadducees, and the midrashim of the Torah had gone into chaos and been lost. The Sadducees paved new roads in the statutes of law and justice, but they went a crooked way, for they invented from their own hearts interpretations and laws that the ancients had not imagined. And how much their sages strove to annul the teaching of the Pharisees forever, we may perceive from the fact that their heart prompted them to make a book of laws to stand for all time. Now, since the world had been desolate for a long time, it cannot be denied that most of their traditions from their ancestors had been forgotten and closed off from the eyes of inquiry, and we can easily understand how heavy the labor was for Simeon to restore the matter to its former state! But the strength of his spirit was among his helpers, for he overcame all obstacles, until under his hand the Oral Torah was fruitful and multiplied like a planting. And we see how many statutes and laws in matters of punishment, both capital and monetary, were fixed then, and how many he restored to their former state. And it is a clear matter that many halakhot concerning the Sanhedrin, the laws of testimony, the rules of justice between a man and his fellow, and all matters of punishment that have survived for us in the Mishnah and the baraita are children of that time; and we may even justly say that then they began to establish anew the rules of justice "between blood and blood, between plea and plea" [Deuteronomy 17:8], and for all "matters of controversy within thy gates" [Deuteronomy 17:8]. Had the book of decrees (Sefer Gezerata) that the Sadducees composed survived for us, then from this book we could judge the reason for several statutes that the Pharisees founded at that time, just as we know the reason for some of them from what has reached us of the opinions of the Sadducees about them. In the way of midrash of the Torah that Simeon and the Pharisaic sages of his generation went, they did not yet distance themselves from the plain sense of Scripture. And although at that time they used some of the measures by which the Torah is expounded, and we have seen that most of the later midrashim expounded by the aforementioned ⚠7 measures depart from the way of the plain sense, we also know, and it will be made known to us further in the coming chapters, that this deviation from the plain sense did not come through the fault of the measures; rather its cause was that those who used the measures sometimes overstepped the limit and went beyond the measure. If we find that Simeon expounded by binyan av, and likewise used the measure of gezerah shavah (above, in the note), every intelligent person will admit that his use of these measures is very proper and that the midrashim he derived through them are sound and good. And the sum of the matter is this: the deviation from the clear plain sense on both sides of the opponents came in the wake of the opposition, as will be made clear before us further. For this emerges explicitly from all the events concerning the disputes between the Pharisees and the Sadducees whose memory has survived for us: that many matters and ordinances were founded with all strength and force, even where no clear proof for them is found in the Torah, only to make a public demonstration against the Sadducees. Were not all the particular things they enacted for the reaping of the omer, such as that they did not hesitate to reap the omer on the Sabbath, and various other customs that they observed in its reaping and its offering (Menaḥot 65a), all prepared and founded only because they saw that the dispute with the Sadducees required it? And indeed we have also seen that the Sadducees on their side followed this same practice of deviating in several religious matters only to oppose the Pharisees. And if someone says to us: "that the Sadducees are the stationary ones, who hold fast to the tradition of the old covenant and to the tradition of the first Scribes, who explained the Torah very plainly and defined the commandments according to the rules of the plain sense and the original intent; and the Pharisees were the movers among the stationary, those who travel on and who widen, in their definition, the ways of performing the commandments in the manner of derash," we too will say that "the witness is a false witness" [Deuteronomy 19:18]. Indeed this witness (the book Ma'amar ha-Ishut) perverted the line of justice out of love for the one side and from bias toward the other. But the truth is this: the Pharisees kept the way of tradition and walked in the footsteps of their ancestors. And it was received among them that the heads of the generations are authorized by the Torah to do as they did in expounding the Torah, for this was laid down in the wisdom of the Giver of the Torah: not to give the Torah cut and closed. Therefore even the new interpretations were in their eyes as received ones, and even when they departed from a received interpretation, this was not a departure from tradition but a departure by the power of tradition. Whoever looks at the course of the interpretation of the Torah in every generation and in all the ancient times will know that this view goes on from generation to generation; and therefore, if the Pharisees were the movers, they acted according to the original intent, and so did the prophets and after them the Scribes. But the Sadducees were not the stationary ones. Indeed, at the moment when they kindled the fire of dispute, for one reason or another, both these and those stood at one position in understanding the Torah; and as the division of hearts grew stronger, both these and those were movers: the Pharisees were the movers forward, like their ancestors in every generation, and in this they were the bearers of tradition; and the Sadducees too were movers, but they went backward and not forward, and through this they broke the bonds and distanced themselves from the way of their ancestors entirely. Now the guilt of the Sadducees is great on two sides. On their own side, that they went backward and abandoned the original intent. And in this too their guilt is great, that they caused the Pharisees to be forced at times to go beyond the measure in their midrashim, which was the reason that in the course of time the plain midrash changed the sense it had had from days of old.

מן המאורעות האלה יוצא לנו ברור כי עדות החכמים המאוחרים נאמנה מאד כי שמעון החזיר התורה ליושנה (קידושין ס"ו). כי משך זמן ארוך היה שדה ההלכה שממה דרכי הלמוד שנו את טעמם תחת עקת הצדוקים. ומדרשי התורה הלכו בתהו ויאבדו. מסלות חדשות סללו הצדוקים בחקי משפט וצדק אך הלכו דרך עקלתון כי בדו מלבם פירושים ודינים אשר לא שערום הקדמונים. ולדעת כמה חכמים התאמצו להפר תורת הפרושים לעולמי עד, זה נתבונן ממה שמלאם לבם לעשות ספר דינים לעמוד כל ימי עולם. ועתה אחרי שזמן רב היה העולם שמם, הלא אין להכחיש כי רוב קבלותיהם מקדמוניהם נשתכחו ויסתתמו מעיני הדרישה ובקל נבין כמה כבדה העבודה על שמעון להחזיר הדבר לישנו! אבל אומץ רוחו היה בעוזריו כי התגבר על כל המעכבים עד שתחת ידו היתה התורה שבעל פה פרה ורבה כנטיעה. ואנו רואים כמה חקים ודינים בעניני עונשים בין בנפשות ובין בממון נקבעו אז וכמה החזירם לקדמותם. ודבר ברור הוא שהרבה הלכות ביחוס אל הסנהדרין ובדיני עדות ובחקת משפט בין איש לרעהו ובכל עניני עונשים אשר נתקיימו לנו במשנה ובברייתא הם ילדי הזמן ההוא, ואף בצדק נוכל לאמר כי אז הוחל ליסד מחדש p. 135 חקת המשפט בין דם לדם ובין דין לדין ולכל דברי ריבות בשעריהם, לו נתקיים לנו ספ[ר] גזרתא אשר חברו הצדוקים כי אז מן הספר הזה היינו יכולים לשפוט על סבת כמה חקי[ם] אשר יסדו הפרושים בעת ההיא. כאשר אנו יודעים סבת מקצתם מתוך מה שהגיע אלינ[ו] מדעות הצדוקים על אודתם. בדרך מדרש התורה אשר הלך שמעון וחכמי דורו הפרושי[ם] לא התרחקו עוד מפשוטו של מקרא. ואם אמנם שהשתמשו בעת ההיא במקצת המדו[ת] שהתורה נדרשת בהן, וראינו שברוב המדרשים המאוחרים הנדרשים על פי המדות הנ[זכרות] יוצאים מדרך הפשט, הלא ידענו גם כן ועוד יודע לנו בפרקים הבאים, שאין זאת הנטיי[ה] מן הפשוט באה באשמת המדות כי אם סבתה שהמשתמשים במדות עברו לפעמים הגבו[ל] והפריזו על המדה. אם מצאנו ששמעון דרש בבנין אב, וכן השתמש במדת הגזרה שו[ה] (למעלה בהערה). הנה יודה כל משכיל כי שמושו במדות האלה צודק מאד והמדרשים שהוציא על פיהן בריאים וטובים. וכללו של דבר: כי הנטייה מן הפשט הברור בכל שני צדדי המתנגדים, היא באה לרגלי ההתנגדות כאשר יתברר עוד לפנינו; One thing is told of Simeon ben Shetaḥ which by our views today is a cruel deed. It is this: in his time the daughters of Israel were devoted to the practice of witchcraft, and to cut off this superstition from the land he took action and hanged eighty witch women on a tree in one day, not because they deserved it but because the hour required it (Sanhedrin 46a). But who can judge today a deed done close to two thousand years ago, and who can weigh in the balance of the opinions of our time ancient actions whose causes, which forced their doers to do them, we do not know? At any rate we can know from this that he was not exacting about the letters of the statutes to a hair's breadth, for he knew what the time demanded of him, and he acted according to the demands of the time. We have already seen in the course of our account, and will see further, that this was a great principle among the sages of all generations: to issue rulings for the hour as the hour required them, whether to make a fence for the Torah or for another reason necessary for the welfare of the nation; and from such rulings for the hour there sometimes came forth halakhic consequences that became "a statute which shall not pass" [Psalms 148:6]. We have seen this in the clearest manner in the prohibition, fixed and standing, of fighting on the Sabbath. The Ḥasidim at the beginning of the Hasmonean war gave their lives for it; afterward they permitted it by a ruling for the hour; at various times they went back to treating it as forbidden; and after a time it was permitted entirely. And the outcome of this permission was the principle that they laid down, that the saving of life overrides the Sabbath, and it became a standing halakhah that all the winds cannot move from its place. Simeon ben Shetaḥ and his colleague decreed impurity on the land of the gentiles and on glass vessels (Shabbat 14b); and although these decrees had been founded by Yose ben Yoezer and his colleague, they were nevertheless compelled to found them once again, because from the end of the time of Yoḥanan the High Priest, from the time he turned his mind to follow the Sadducees, these decrees had sunk into oblivion; for they were not permitted to keep the teaching of the Pharisees at all, as Yoḥanan had decreed upon them. And therefore, when Simeon ben Shetaḥ and his colleague came and prevailed over the Sadducees and restored their teaching to its former state, these decrees too were renewed.8 A second decree they made concerning the impurity of metal vessels (Shabbat, ibid.). And although the impurity of metal vessels is explicit in the Torah (Numbers 31:22), perhaps in those days they were lax in the matter and treated it as permitted against the law; or they used a stratagem and passed them through fire, and after passing them through fire did not practice immersion in water for them, nor seven days' impurity, but declared them pure at once, since the plain meaning of the words of the Torah as written does not indicate immersion in water and seven days' impurity after passing through fire. At any rate the Sadducees did not admit their impurity, and since the opinion of the Sadducees was rooted in the people, Simeon ben Shetaḥ was compelled to decree impurity upon them. Do we not see that Queen Shelamzion [Salome Alexandra], who cleaved to the customs of the Pharisees, nevertheless thought to remove the vessels that had become impure in her house from their impurity by breaking them, giving them to a smith, and having him weld them (Shabbat 16b)? From this we can judge that even those who followed the Pharisees did not know the conditions and limits of this impurity, because they had neglected it all the days of the Sadducees' rule. And it is possible that according to the Sadducees the intent of the Torah, which speaks of kinds of metal, is not to vessels made of metal but to the raw lumps of the kinds of metal.9 However it may be, Simeon and his colleagues were compelled to restore the matter to its place and its limit, and this is what is meant when they said that Simeon made a decree concerning metal vessels. Simeon enacted another great ordinance concerning the woman's ketubbah. He saw in his time that the bond of marriage had grown weak and that it was a light thing in the husband's eyes to divorce his wife, and all the ordinances that had been enacted with regard to the woman's ketubbah had not helped to remove the stumbling block. For at first the custom was that her ketubbah was kept with her parents, and it was easy in his eyes to send her away (for he said to her, "Go to your ketubbah"); and they enacted that her ketubbah be kept with her husband, and even so it was easy in his eyes to send her away (for the ketubbah was ready in his hand at all times). For this they enacted that he should buy with his wife's ketubbah cups, bowls and dishes (and this too did not help, for even then he could say to her, "Take your ketubbah," that is, the vessels that he had bought and that were set aside for her ketubbah); and they enacted that he should do business with his wife's ketubbah, for since he does business with it he loses it (Yerushalmi Ketubbot, end of chapter 8). And after they enacted this, it is self-evident that he was compelled to pledge all his property to her for her ketubbah; and therefore this ordinance is a great obstacle, so that he cannot divorce her lightly; and it is attributed to Simeon ben Shetaḥ.10 This ordinance sheds light on matters of divorce in general: for if they said that it was easy in his eyes to send her away, it appears that in ancient times they were accustomed to divorce for any matter and not only for a matter of unchastity, as the House of Shammai wished to establish a law that a man may not divorce his wife unless he has found in her "some unseemly thing" [Deuteronomy 24:1] (Gittin 88b ⚠8). He enacted another great ordinance, namely that it should be a duty upon every man in Israel to send the children to school (Yerushalmi Ketubbot, ibid.). And to this ordinance the events of the time without doubt forced him. For during the days when the hand of the Sadducees was uppermost, the schools too were without doubt laid waste. After the decree was issued in the days of Yoḥanan the High Priest to remove the opinions of the Pharisees, the spirit of the Sadducees spread in the school as well. And the good people, especially among the common folk, who always followed the opinions of the Pharisees, refrained from sending their sons to school, and this neglect lasted until the days of Simeon; and therefore there was great necessity to make this ordinance.

להעביר דעות הפרושים התפשט גם בבית הספר רוח הצדוקים. והטובים ובפרט מהמון העם אשר נמשכו תמיד אחרי דעות הפרושים נמנעו מלשלח בניהם לבית הספר וההתרשלות הזאת היתה קיימת עד ימי שמעון, ועל כן היה הכרח גדול לעשות זאת התקנה.

11

Further, there have survived for us from Simeon ben Shetaḥ and Judah ben Tabbai precious sayings that warn the judges to be deliberate in judgment, to hold to the measure of justice, to keep far from wrong, from favoritism in judgment and from perverting it, and to be careful in their words in the examination of the witnesses (Avot chapter 1). Of the events of Judah's life nothing remains beyond what has been mentioned; but of Simeon's life several more events are told, all of which show that he acted by the measure of strict justice in all his deeds, as was without doubt fitting for his time. We have seen that he let his son be sentenced to death and fulfilled in himself the principle: "Let the law pierce the mountain." So too the deed with the women in Ashkelon shows it, whom he sentenced to death, hanging eighty in one day against the fine points of the law because of the needs of the hour. A trustworthy proof of his love of justice and uprightness in all his affairs is given us by an incident that happened to him with an Ishmaelite. Simeon bought a donkey from an Ishmaelite for its full price; and when they brought it to his house, his disciples found a precious stone hanging on its neck, and they said to Simeon: "Our master, 'the blessing of the Lord, it maketh rich'!" [Proverbs 10:22]. He answered them and said: "A donkey I bought; a precious stone I did not buy." He went and returned it to the Ishmaelite, and that Ishmaelite proclaimed over him: "Blessed be the Lord, the God of Simeon ben Shetaḥ" (Deuteronomy Rabbah, Ekev). We see from this that the measure of justice was his measure; and even though his lot did not fall in the pleasantness of wealth, for he made a meager living and was compelled to trade in flax (Yerushalmi Bava Metzia 2:5), he was not tempted on that account to "get riches, and not by right" [Jeremiah 17:11], and he dealt justly even with a man of another religion. In beliefs and opinions his thought was pure, and he did not go beyond the measure in piety. In his days there lived a man who was held by the people to be practiced in miracles; this man was called Ḥoni the Circle-Maker. Once there was famine in the land for three years, and when most of Adar had passed without rain they sent to him, saying: "Pray, that rain may fall." He said to them: "Bring in the Passover ovens, so that they do not dissolve"; and he prayed, and no rain fell. He drew a circle and stood within it and said before Him: "Master of the world, Your children have turned their faces to me, for I am like a son of the house before You. I swear by Your great name that I will not move from here until You have mercy on Your children." The rains began to drip. He said: "Not thus did I ask." They came down in fury. He said: "Not thus did I ask," until they fell properly. . . . Simeon ben Shetaḥ sent to him: "You deserve to be excommunicated" (Ta'anit 19a; and see the Yerushalmi and Megillat Ta'anit chapter 18 ⚠9). For Simeon could not restrain himself when one "formed out of the clay" [Job 33:6] spoke thus toward God; and Simeon was zealous against him especially because he believed in himself that he had the power to compel his Creator by his prayer to do his will, and caused the people too to believe so, which in Simeon's view is not so, as the words put in his mouth show: "Had a decree been decreed as it was decreed in the days of Elijah, would you not have brought the many to the desecration of the Name?" From these brief words we learn that in his view the prayer of the righteous does not avail against the divine decree. And therefore, if a decree has been decreed as in the days of Elijah, there is no power in man's prayer to turn back the decree; but for the common people he would thereby cause great harm to their faith. For Ḥoni showed in his words that it was certain that his request would be done in any case, and thereby he strengthened the people's expectation that his prayer would certainly help; yet perhaps a decree had been decreed and the prayer would not help, and what would be the end of these things? A weakening of faith, and the name of Heaven would be desecrated. "These are the edges of his ways" [Job 26:14]. When did Simeon die? It has not been made known to us. Nor do we know how long he lived after the death of Queen Shelamzion, who conducted the government according to the counsel of the Pharisees for nine years.

עוד נתקיימו לנו מאת שמעון ב"ש ויהודה ב"ט מאמרים יקרים להזהיר הדיינים להיות מתונים בדין ולאחוז במדת הצדק להתרחק מן העול וממשוא פנים בדין, ומהטייתו, ולהיות נזהרים בדבריהם בדרישת העדים (אבות פ"א). מקורות חיי יהודה לא נשאר מאומה יותר ממה שנזכר אך מחיי שמעון יסופרו עוד איזה מאורעות אשר כלם מראים שנהג במדת הדין בכל מעשיו כאשר בלי ספק היה נאות לזמנו. ראינו שהניח לדון את בנו למיתה וקיים בעצמו הכלל: יעקור הדין את ההר. וכן מורה המעשה עם הנשים באשקלון אשר דן למיתה ותלה שמונים ביום אחד נגד דקדוקי הדין מפני צורך השעה; מופת נאמן על אהבתו את הצדק והיושר בכל עניניו יתן לנו מעשה אחד אשר אירע לו עם ישמעאלי. שמעון לקח מישמעאלי אחד חמור בכסף מלא, ויהי כי הביאו לביתו מצאו בו תלמידיו אבן טובה תלויה בצוארו, ויאמרו אל שמעון: רבינו ברכת ה' היא תעשיר! ויען להם ויאמר: חמור לקחתי אבן טובה לא לקחתי, הלך והחזירה לישמעאלי. וקרא עליו אותו הישמעאלי ברוך ה' אלהי שמעון בן שטח (דברים רבה פ' עקב). ונראה מזה כי מדת הצדק היתה מדתו, ואם גם לא נפל חבלו בנועם העושר כי התפרנס בדוחק והוכרח לעשות סחורה בפשתן (ירושלמי ב"מ פ"ב ה"ה) לא התפתה בעבור זה לעשות עושר ולא במשפט ונהג בצדק גם עם בעל דת אחרת. באמונות ודעות היתה מחשבתו טהורה ולא הפריז על מדת החסידות. בימיו חי איש אחד אשר הוחזק אצל העם למלומד בנסים: האיש הזה נקרא חוני מעגל. פעם אחת היה שלש שנים רעב בארץ, ויהי כאשר יצא רוב אדר בלא גשמים שלחו אליו לאמר: התפלל וירדו גשמים. אמר להם הכניסו תנורי פסחים בשביל שלא ימוקו, והתפלל ולא ירדו. עג עוגה ועמד בתוכה ואמר לפניו: רבונו של עולם בניך שמו פניהם אלי שאני כבן בית לפניך נשבע אני בשמך הגדול שאיני זז מכאן עד שתרחם על בניך, התחילו מנטפים. אמר לא כך p. 138 שאלתי. ירדו בזעף. אמר: לא כך שאלתי. עד שירדו כתיקנן . . . . שלח לו שמעון בן שטח צריך אתה לנדות (תענית י"ט. ועי' ירושלמי ובמ"ת פי"ח). כי לא יכול שמעון להתאפק כי ידבר קרוץ מחומר ככה לעומת האל וביחוד קנא שמעון קנאה נגדו על אשר האמין בנפשו שיש לו כח להכריח את יוצרו בתפלתו לעשות רצונו, וגרם שגם העם האמינו ככה, אשר לדעתו אינו כן כאשר יורו הדברים המושמים בפיו לאמר: שאילו נגזרה גזרה כמו שנגזרה בימי אליה לא נמצאת מביא את הרבים לידי חלול השם. מדברים הקצרים האלה אנו למדים כי לדעתו נגד הגזירה האלהית אין תפלת הצדיק מועלת. ועל כן אם נגזרה גזרה כמו בימי אליה אז אין כח בתפלת האדם להשיב את הגזרה, אבל להמון העם יגרום בזה נזק גדול באמונתם, כי חוני הראה בדבריו כי נכון הדבר שתעשה בקשתו בכל ענין, ועל ידי זה החזיק תוחלת העם שתועיל תפלתו בבירור, ואמנם אולי נגזרה גזרה ולא תועיל התפלה, ומה אחרית אלה? התרשלות אמונה ויתחלל שם שמים. אלה הם קצות דרכיו. מתי מת שמעון? לא הודע לנו. ולא ידענו כמה האריך ימים אחרי מות שלמינון המלכה אשר נהגה הממשלה על פי עצת הפרושים תשע שנים.

12

The great events that occurred in the time of Simeon necessarily brought a new spirit into the ways of study of the Pharisees. From now on they were compelled to widen the ways of midrash of the Torah in order to give the opinions of the Pharisees a foundation and a root in the Torah of Moses; and even more they were compelled to hem in the laws of the Sadducees, which had been rooted in the nation for many years, and to bring proofs against them from the written Torah. To achieve this purpose, several measures and rules of midrash were of great use to them, for these measures by their nature have the power of expansion; they are fitting means to widen the midrash of the Torah according to the understanding of the Pharisees and their traditions, as indeed the measures widened from generation to generation. And already in the first generation after the death of Simeon ben Shetaḥ and Judah ben Tabbai their disciples arose and occupied themselves much with the midrash of the Torah according to the measures, until they acquired among the people the honored name "great expounders," as will be explained before us in the next chapter.

המאורעות הגדולים אשר אירעו בזמנו של שמעון הביאו בהכרח רוח חדשה בדרכי הלמוד של הפרושים. מעתה היו מוכרחים להרחיב דרכי מדרש התורה כדי לתת לדעות הפרושים יסוד ושורש בתורת משה, ועוד יותר הוכרחו לכתור דינות הצדוקים אשר זה כמה שנים נשתרשו באומה, ולהביא נגדם ראיות מן התורה הכתובה, ולהשגת התכלית הזאת היו להם כמה מדות וכללי המדרש לתועלת גדולה כי אלה המדות בטבען יש להן כח ההתרחבות הן אמצעיים נאותים להרחיב מדרש התורה לפי הבנת הפרושים וקבלותיהם, כאשר באמת מדור לדור נתרחבו המדות. וכבר בדור הראשון שאחרי מות שמעון בן שטח ויהודה ב"ט קמו תלמידיהם ועסקו הרבה במדרש התורה על פי המדות עד שקנו להם שם המעלה בעם „דרשנים גדולים" כאשר יבואר לפנינו בפרק הבא.

I will remark here on an aggadah told about Simeon: Once he saw a man running after his fellow into a ruin. Simeon ran after him and saw a sword in his hand, its blood dripping and the slain man writhing. He said to him: Wicked one, who killed this man, I or you? But what can I do, since your blood is not given into my hand, for the Torah said: "At the mouth of two witnesses ... shall he that is worthy of death be put to death" [Deuteronomy 17:6]. So is the reading in Sanhedrin (36b); ⚠12 but in the Yerushalmi the reading is: What can I do, since your blood is not given into my hand; but He who knows thoughts will exact punishment, etc. Now in this incident there are two reasons for exemption: first, that Simeon is only one witness; second, that it is testimony by inference (umdana). But from Simeon's words one cannot prove in any way for which reason he said "your blood is not given into my hand": whether because he was only one witness or because his testimony was by inference. Therefore the baraita there, which says, "What is inference? For example, the incident of Simeon ben Shetaḥ," necessarily does not intend to prove that inference is not valid testimony, for this is not proven from the incident. Its intent is only to explain the halakhah in the Mishnah, which says "lest you say [from] inference," that this is not testimony; and this is a settled halakhah and needs no proof. The baraita rather brings an illustration of what testimony by inference is, and says: like the incident of Simeon ben Shetaḥ, where even had there been another witness with Simeon we could not have accepted their testimony. There is no need for the forced explanation of the Tosafists, who wrote that there was another witness with Simeon. ↩ back to the text

So, it seems to me, is the sense of the baraita in the Yerushalmi, which agrees with the baraita in the Bavli. And it is clear that the last ordinance was that the husband should do business with it; and since he does business with her ketubbah, there is no longer anything set aside for her ketubbah, and of necessity he must pledge all his property to her and write for her: "All the property I have is surety and guarantee for the ketubbah"; and this is also the sense of the Tosefta, chapter 12. And I have seen that the scholar Graetz, 533, emended the text of the Yerushalmi and read thus: "At first her ketubbah was deposited with her parents, and it was easy in his eyes to divorce her; they enacted that her ketubbah be with her husband — and they enacted that a man should buy with his wife's ketubbah cups, bowls and dishes of silver and gold (?), and still, when he was angry with her, he would say to her, 'Take your ketubbah'; and therefore they went back and enacted that a man should do business with his wife's ketubbah, until Simeon ben Shetaḥ came and enacted that he should write for her: 'All my property is surety for her ketubbah.'" This implies that already before Simeon they enacted that he do business with her ketubbah, and afterward Simeon enacted that he write "all my property, etc." But this is not so, for it is impossible to enact that he do business with her ketubbah unless he pledges his property to her, as is known to every student of the Talmud; and in the Tosefta too it is explicit that her ketubbah should be with her husband (that is, that he should do business with it) and that he should write, etc. — all of it the ordinance of Simeon; and in the Yerushalmi they said explicitly that Simeon enacted that he do business with her ketubbah. Therefore there is no room for Graetz's emendation, and the words of the Yerushalmi need no emendation, only explanation. ↩ back to the text
